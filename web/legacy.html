A click is a standing order, so the character acts on it at its next decision — and that delay is the intellect stat. Send something in and it will break off the walk to fight: a visible enemy outranks your order, and that is the whole idea.

Keys

  • clickdrag Click the floor to go there. Drag instead and you trace a path: it walks the legs in order, holds at the end, and the next plain click cancels whatever is left of it. Every leg is still a standing order rather than a rail — the character decides how to walk it.
  • right clickEsc Stand down — hold this ground.
  • F Free will — no order at all; the character decides for itself.
  • QE Open the Enemy rail on the left, or the Hero rail on the right.
  • SB Send in a skitterer or a brute on the body's own kit. These deliberately ignore the Enemy rail's template — a hotkey that quietly meant something different after you touched a panel would be the worse surprise. Use Spawn for the template. Shift+S sends eight skitterers at once, which is how you fill the room to sixty-four and see what the frame costs with a crowd in it. Both are shadowed while you hold Movement — see WASD below.
  • 12 Choose which of the two things you are carrying is in your hand. The swap costs real ticks and is refused mid-cut, so it is a decision and not a menu.
  • CVX Take the character's movement, its choice of what to hold, or its aim — three switches, in any combination. Whichever you leave alone, it keeps doing for itself, on its own reaction clock. Aim without Action is worth trying: you throw the cuts and it picks the weapon.
  • WASDmouse Move and aim, once you have taken them. Click to cut along the line you are pointing at — one click, one attack, with a windup you can be punished during. Holding a guard instead of a blade is a matter of what is in your hand, not a modifier key. W is up the screen in every view, so the keys mean what they look like rather than what the room's axes do — which is why walking up-screen covers ground more slowly than walking across it. While you hold Movement, S walks down-screen instead of sending in a skitterer, and Shift+S stops sending eight; hand the feet back with C to spawn again.
  • Y Show or hide the vision discs — how far each body can see. A Tactical and Dev control: World draws no discs to hide.
  • G Cycle how the room is drawn. World is the room as it looks, and only that — art, the weapon in hand, a health bar when it matters, and one faint ring where the body actually collides. Tactical drops the art for discs and facing wedges and turns every readout back on: sight, reach and facing. Dev drops the fog of war as well, so you can watch what the monsters are doing while the character cannot. The first two show you only what the character can see — ground it has seen before stays dim, and rock it has never reached stays black.
  • P Break the frame down by phase, beside the fps readout that is always up. Its own key rather than a part of Dev, because Dev draws the room with the fog lifted — which is a different amount of work from the room you are actually looking at, and so a different number.
  • Space Freeze the world, or let it go again. Every order still lands while it is frozen — click, drag a path, stand down — and nothing moves until you resume, which is what the button is for.
  • wheel Zoom, between the whole room and close in over the character's shoulder.
  • RRestart the room.
  • TabMove between the controls that are actually on screen.
