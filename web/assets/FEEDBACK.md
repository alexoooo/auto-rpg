No calibration batch has been delivered yet. **Mass production must not begin.**

This heading is the gate. It is the first thing to read in this file and the first thing to
change when a gate is held. While it says `NOT PASSED`, only the calibration batch named below
may be produced — nothing else, however easy it would be to draw.

| gate | what it covers | status |
|---|---|---|
| **Stage 1 — the room** | one floor top face, one wall side-face and top-face pair, one torch | **NOT PASSED** — not yet delivered |
| **Stage 2 — the body** | one archetype (the Fighter) complete: composite body in 8 facings × 4 frames, arm layer, shield layer, plus 2–3 weapons | **NOT PASSED** — blocked on stage 1 |

**Why calibration gets a gate of its own.** Mass production reuses whatever passed calibration —
the same settings, the same prompts, the same look, changing only the subject. So a defect that
slips through the gate does not stay one defect: it multiplies across the whole asset set, and it
is found after a hundred images exist rather than after three.

When stage 1 has been delivered and is being reviewed, this block reads:

```markdown
# Calibration: NOT PASSED
Batch 1 is under review. Mass production must not begin.
```

When both gates are held, it reads `# Calibration: PASSED` with the date each gate was held, and
production may begin.

## How this file works

**One section per asset, cumulative and current.** Cumulative: nothing is deleted, so the history
of an asset is readable in one place. Current: each asset has exactly one verdict at a time, and
it is the latest one.

**There are exactly two verdicts, and no third.**

| verdict | meaning |
|---|---|
| **`regenerate`** | this asset is wrong, here is precisely why, produce it again |
| **`passed`** | this asset is **locked**. Do not regenerate it. Do not overwrite it. Do not "improve" it |

There is no "acceptable", no "minor issue", no "fine for now". An asset is either locked or it is
coming back. Anything in between is a note that nobody acts on and that quietly becomes the
standard.

**A `regenerate` verdict states what is wrong, not what to do about it.** The defect is named in
terms of this repository's own contract — a clause of `ASSET_SPEC.md`, a dimension, a light
direction — and, wherever the defect is visible in the game rather than in the file, an in-game
screenshot is linked. A screenshot is worth more than any sentence here: most of these defects
are invisible in a file browser and obvious in the room.

### The two worked examples

A `regenerate` verdict, in full:

```markdown
## chars/fighter.png
**regenerate** — the `n` column's torso is lit from the front; every other column is
lit from the upper right. Screenshot: docs/review/2026-08-14-fighter-n.png
```

Read what that verdict does. It names the file. It gives one verdict word in bold. It states the
defect in one clause, in terms of the global light rule of `ASSET_SPEC.md` § 5. It says which
part of the asset is wrong (`the n column`) and what the rest of the asset does correctly, so the
fix is a re-run of one facing rather than a re-run of the archetype. And it links a screenshot.

A `passed` verdict, in full:

```markdown
## env/floor_a.png
**passed** — locked. Do not regenerate.
```

That is the entire form. A passed verdict does not need a reason.

**never regenerated by anyone**. They are reference material and documentation. Nothing loads
them and the game does not know they exist.

### `web/assets/CONCEPT.png`

**Permanent. Not an asset. Never regenerated.**

The painted concept image that is the mood target for the entire game. Its home is this
directory because a mood target that lives in a chat log is a mood target nobody can check a

It is a **style reference**, not a template: match its rendering, treat its content as
directional. It is not measured by `tools/measure_assets.js`, it has no manifest entry, and it

## Pipeline fixtures — not art, do not regenerate

Three PNGs exist to prove that the loading and drawing path works, one per transform the renderer
can get wrong. **They are deliberately, unmistakably ugly.** That is the point: a deliberately
ugly test asset is the only one whose defects are unambiguous, and a magenta checkerboard that
comes out as a rhombus of rhombuses catches the "projected twice" failure before it costs a
generation batch.

They stay in the repository permanently. The manifest keeps pointing at them until real assets
replace those keys. **Do not regenerate them, do not replace them with real art, and do not
report them as defects** — if one of them looks wrong to you, it is working.

## env/floor_a.png
**not art, do not regenerate** — pipeline fixture. A 344 × 344 magenta-and-black checker.
Proves the `surface` transform: that a tiled floor image lies flat in the projection, tiles
seamlessly, stays nailed to the level under a pan, and does not re-tile under a zoom. It is
*supposed* to look wrong.

## fighter/body_{facing}_{frame}.png
**not art, do not regenerate** — pipeline fixture. Thirty-two flat green rectangles, each with
its facing letter and frame number drawn inside it, and each with a small notch at a different
height per frame so a wrong frame is visible at a glance. Proves the `actor` transform: that a
body stands on its feet at every facing, that the letter matches the direction it is walking,
that the notch steps through `walk1, walk2, walk3, walk2` at a rate that tracks the ground going
past, and that the figure does not bob sideways.

## weapons/test_bar.png
**not art, do not regenerate** — pipeline fixture. A flat cyan bar with a red pixel block at the
hilt end and a yellow one at the tip end, and asymmetric notches along its length so that a
mirrored or reversed span is obvious. Proves the `weapon` transform: that the red end stays on
the hand and the yellow end on the tip through a full swing, at all eight facings, in both the
lifted blade and its ground shadow. The asymmetry is deliberate — a bar that comes out reversed
at four facings and correct at the other four is a sign error in the span, and a symmetric bar
would hide it.
