# The headroom audit

2026-09-26. Skill ceiling session 05 (`docs/plans/2026-09-25-skill-ceiling-05-headroom-audit.md`),
on main at afa1779 plus this session's commits. It measures and reports: nothing was removed and
nothing was retuned. Every figure is Node and headless, and each one names its harness:

- **"Bout runner"**: `research/headroom.mjs` through the research runner (`runJobs`, one Havok arena
  per worker realm), on the Node bout runner (`tests/harness/bout-runner.mjs`) under the research
  `PROTOCOL` (150 s cap, supported locomotion). Every cell is played as swapped seed pairs: A takes
  the left corner with one seed pair and the right with the same pair reversed, and a cell's
  figures are over whole pairs.
- **"Drill runner"**: `research/drills.mjs` on the Node bout runner and the fork harness
  (`tests/harness/fork.mjs`), paired by start, through `research/headroom-drills.sh`.
- **"Headless arena"**: `research/leverage.mjs`'s balance and commitment benches, a supported pair
  12 m apart in the Node headless arena (`tests/harness/golem-headless-arena.mjs`), read off the
  locomotion port.
- **"Impact bench"**: the Node effector bench's stroke into a free plate (`research/leverage.mjs`,
  deflection).
- **"Scoring arithmetic"**: bodies stood up by `createBout` and priced by `src/scoring.ts` and
  `vitality` in `src/bout.ts`, called, not copied (`research/leverage.mjs`, precision).

The tables are printed by `node research/headroom-report.mjs`; the runs are under
`research/runs/headroom-*` and `research/runs/leverage/`.

**The ruler** is `expert@c8,h1` (session 04's default), reported beside the persistence expert
`expert-persist@c8,h1` (it plans on the opponent repeating its last command rather than on its
real mind) and beside `expert@c4,h1` for the compute curve. The naive minds are `golem-walker` and
each family's own duelist (`golem-duelist`, `humanoid-duelist`, `skeleton-duelist`).

## Summary

- **Headroom.** Bout runner, n 6 expert bouts a body and 24 naive ones; drill runner, 40 starts.
  - The ruler wins every bout against the reference on 26 of 30 bodies, 11 of them bodies on which
    neither naive mind wins one. Its margin over the duelist is 0.53 to 1.72 of a bar there.
  - **One dead end: `human-unarmed`.** Its bout headroom is 0.79, its drill headroom -6.3, and its
    compute curve is flat. The thresholds came from the distribution: 1.26 bars and +13.5 points.
  - **Cannot win against the reference:** `skeleton-fists` and `skeleton-whip`, and
    `human-unarmed`, at 0 of 6. `skeleton-maul` wins 2 of 6.
  - Persistence headroom is zero on every human, skeleton and fists body and on the capped ram:
    there, what the ruler wins with is knowing the opponent's real policy.
  - The compute curve still climbs on the humans and the whips, so the c8 figures are lower bounds
    there.
- **Orderings.** Bout runner, 480 bouts a family, size x1.1.
  - Skill beats size at 100 % on stone and on the skeleton, losing at most 0.03 of a bar of margin to the bigger body.
  - Mind beats mind at 100 % on equal bodies.
  - Size pays only the expert on stone (84 %). It is a coin for the duelist (57 %) and a loss for
    the walker (32 %), and on the skeleton it pays nobody.
- **Idle gate.** Bout runner, the ruler against idle dummies of four families, 248 bouts, 60 s.
  - 50 of 124 cells have no win inside the mark.
  - `ram-capped` cannot hurt a body that stands still.
  - The humans can hurt one, but not finish it inside 60 s.
  - The skeleton's old zeros were the mind's.
- **Leverage.**
  - Balance: there is an off-balance window in a biped's gait, none in the multileg. The idle stone
    is felled because the waist's lean motor cannot hold the trunk (section 4.1).
  - Commitment costs 0.7 to 1.3 s out of guard, and the ruler punishes it; it does not cost
    balance.
  - Precision pays through the edge (7x) and through targeting the thin bodies; blunt damage is
    linear in energy.
  - Deflection is physical against an edge and weak against a mace.
  - Footwork is worth 22 points head to head, a lower bound.
- **Attributes.** Bout runner; expert n 16 a level, duelist n 64 on four bodies.
  - Toughness, arm speed, weight and size pay 3.3 to 4.2 points a step.
  - Movement, turning, stability, recovery and armour pay a quarter of that or less; movement runs
    backwards.
  - Stability and recovery are unreached rather than weak: most bouts never fall.
  - Only weight changes the ruler's plan.
- **Compute.** 151 lane-hours, about 9.5 h of wall time.

**What it says.**

- **There is skill to be had in almost every body.** The full-knowledge expert wins every bout
  against the reference on 26 of 30 bodies. Among them are 11 bodies on which neither naive mind
  wins a single one. The expert's share is at its ceiling there, so the headroom is read on the
  margin: the expert finishes 0.53 to 1.72 of a bar further ahead than the duelist on the same
  seeds.
- **Full knowledge and persistence part company on the other families.**
  - The persistence expert's headroom over the duelist is zero on 16 bodies: every human body, every
    skeleton body, every fists body (stone, reach, pitch) and the capped ram. It is also zero on
    `wheel`, but there the duelist already wins on those seeds.
  - It is 42 to 83 points on the stone blade bodies, 67 on `reach-maul-wheel` and `pitch-mace-ram`,
    and 17 on the stone mace, maul and whip.
  - So on the humans, the skeletons and the fists, what the ruler wins with is knowing the
    reference's actual policy. On stone blades a model that only assumes the opponent repeats itself
    is enough.
  - The first kind of headroom is a lower bound on what a person could find by playing. It is not a
    skill a person can be taught from the body alone.
- **Three bodies cannot win against the reference however they are played**: `skeleton-fists`,
  `skeleton-whip` and `human-unarmed` (expert 0 of 6). `skeleton-maul` wins 2 of 6.
  - Those bodies' expert margin is -0.2 to -0.45. The expert still loses by less than the duelist,
    so the headroom (0.55 to 0.8) is not zero.
  - This is the reference being out of reach, which the headroom rule, a difference between two
    minds, does not see. It is named separately below.
- **The compute curve** (c8 against c4, bout margin, 2 pairs) is flat or near it on the stone bodies
  (-0.11 to +0.31). It is still climbing on the humans and the whips:
  - `human-dual-swords` wins 0 of 4 at c4 and 6 of 6 at c8;
  - `human-maul`, `whip` and `human-whip` win half at c4;
  - the whip class is +33 points of share from c4 to c8.
  - On those bodies the c8 figures are a lower bound on what more search would find.

**The dead-end rule and its threshold.** A body is a dead end when all three of these hold:

- its bout headroom (expert minus duelist margin) is under the bout threshold;
- its drill headroom is under the drill threshold;
- neither compute curve is still climbing. That is, the c8 - c4 bout reading's 95 % interval holds
  zero, and the drill one is inside two standard errors or negative.

Each threshold is the midpoint of the widest gap between neighbouring bodies in the lower half of
that instrument's sorted distribution (`deadEnds` in `research/headroom-report.mjs`). They came out
at:

- **1.26 bars** on bouts, the gap between 1.19 (`human-dual-swords`) and 1.33 (`fists`);
- **+13.5 points** on drills, the gap between +6.3 and +20.8.

Thirteen bodies are low on bouts, three are low on drills, and one is low on both with a flat curve:

| **human-unarmed** | 0.79 | -6.3 | +0.03 | the expert does no better than the duelist on the drills (worse at get-inside), wins 0 of 6 bouts, and more compute does not change that |

- **Low on drills only:**
  - `human-whip` (+6.3): no mind lands a punish on it, 12.5 % at best.
  - `ram-capped` (+6.2): punish-miss is 5 % for every mind, and the ram has no blade drill.

**Instrument gaps.**

## 2. The three orderings

Bout runner, stone default, `research/headroom.mjs --exp orderings --pairs 16 --ladder-pairs 48`,

- **Skill beats size, by as much as the instrument can show.** The expert at x1 wins every bout
  against the duelist at x1.1, and its margin, 0.79 of a bar, is the margin it wins by on equal
  bodies (0.80). The duelist's extra third of mass costs the expert nothing it can measure. The
  share is at its ceiling, so the margin is the reading; this is at 1.33x mass, and the plan's 2x
  cannot be built.
- **What size is worth depends on who is playing it, and the order is backwards for a naive
  mind.** Under the walker the bigger body *loses* (32 %); under the duelist it is a coin (57 %,
  interval over 50); under the expert it wins 84 %. The expert turns a third more mass and reach
  into a real edge; the naive minds cannot, and the walker is hurt by it. So "size is penalty-only",
  skill ceiling 01's finding against the probe minds, is a finding about those minds: played well,
  x1.1 pays.
- **The walker beats the duelist on the stone default** (70 %, n 96). The naive ladder's order is
  walker over duelist on this body.
- The persistence expert sits between the naive minds and the full-knowledge one everywhere: it
  wins every equal-body bout against the duelist at 0.47 margin rather than 0.80.

## 3. The idle-dummy gate, with the expert

Bout runner, `research/headroom.mjs --exp idle --tag gate --pairs 1`, 248 bouts. Every audit body
and the giant was played by the ruler against an idle dummy of each family: the stone default,

## 4. The skill-leverage checks

### 4.1 Balance as state

Headless arena. The weakest fall impulse (`fallImpulseNs`: the fall line along the body's weakest
direction times its supported mass) through each state a body can be in, driven through the
controls a person has, 4 s a state. The first figure is the median, the second the 10th
percentile, N.s:

| body | still | walk | back | strafe | turn | stroke | stroke-step | rising | most vulnerable / least |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| default | 115/115 | 124/92 | 134/110 | 119/106 | 116/84 | 137/123 | 137/123 | 134/123 | 0.31 |
| maul | 142/139 | 122/88 | 121/87 | 100/84 | 106/85 | 120/104 | 151/122 | 172/154 | 0.30 |
| plated | 128/128 | 148/105 | 155/129 | 139/124 | 135/90 | 152/135 | 152/135 | 148/135 | 0.29 |
| multileg | 370/370 | 362/320 | 374/352 | 369/335 | 371/375 | 382/376 | 382/376 | 577/374 | 0.49 |
| wheel | 82/82 | 82/0 | 82/82 | 82/82 | 82/83 | 61/57 | 61/57 | 103/82 | 0.00 |
| human-warrior | 48/48 | 27/5 | 26/13 | 20/13 | 39/36 | 49/47 | 49/47 | 57/51 | 0.07 |
| skeleton-warrior | 9/9 | 4/0 | 3/0 | 2/0 | 8/8 | 9/8 | 9/8 | 12/11 | 0.00 |

The last column is the most vulnerable state's 10th percentile over the least vulnerable reading
anywhere (the strongest direction standing still). Standing still, the weakest direction is 0.41
to 0.63 of the strongest on every body. On the stone default it is straight back (115 N.s, against
166 forward and 234 to the side): the centre of mass stands 30 mm behind the soles' midpoint, so
a body pushed from the front is pushed along its weakest line.

**The reading is the threshold.** On every body a shove along the weakest direction at 0.8 and 0.95
of it stood and at 1.05 and 1.25 of it fell.

**What it says.**

- **There is off-balancing to be had on the bipeds, and it is in their gait, not their strokes.** A
  walking human's weakest line drops to a tenth of its still one (48 to 5 at the 10th percentile), a
  skeleton's to zero, and the stone biped's to 0.8 of still on a turn. A stroke leaves every body's
  line where it was or higher, because a stroke is thrown from a planted base.
- **The skeleton stands on almost nothing.** 9 N.s still is what a 1 kg blade at 9 m/s carries.
  Any contact fells it.
- **The multileg has none to take.** Its worst state is half its best and 320 N.s at the 10th
  percentile, so no blow in the game reaches it.
- The `off-balance` drill that should be the live test was removed in session 03 (idle passed it
  as often as the duelist, because a shove's lean decays in 0.3 s whatever the mind asks). What
  above says is that the window exists on a walking biped; the mind has no command that opens it
  on the other body.

#### The idle stone felled on almost nothing: diagnosed

Bout runner through `research/fall-loop-worker.mjs`, an idle stone default against a
`golem-brawler` on the stone default, 16 seed pairs, 32 bouts: **the idle body fell 212 times, 5.8
a minute**, 177 from standing and 35 from a stagger, none while rising, where physical contact's session 10 had seen most of them (release 1 changed the rise since). Every fall
was "stability threshold was exceeded". The largest blow the ledger was handed in the half second
before a fall was a median 0.03 m/s of specific impulse, against a still fall line of about
0.5 m/s (115 N.s over some 240 kg), and 67 falls had no blow and no held push on the ledger at all.
Nothing that reached the ledger felled it. 202 of the 212 had the pair in contact in the quarter
second before.

Traced a substep at a time (`idleFelledTrace`, 6 bouts, idle body on the left): the brawler walks
into it at 1.5 to 2.3 m/s, stops on contact at chest height (the pair push lands at 1.07 to 1.10 m),
and within 0.2 s the idle body's **trunk folds backward at the waist**: the pelvis does not move
(it is held to the carrier), the core goes back 0.14 m, the head 0.54 m and down 0.2 m, and the
centre of mass moves from 0.03 to 0.17 m behind the soles' midpoint, which is the back edge of the
base. The fall line there is zero, and the support state reads a fall on the next step. When the
brawler backs off before that, the trunk comes back upright in 0.3 s. 34 of 36 falls had the pair
in contact in the half second before.

The waist's lean motor is what lets it fold. `TORSO_WAIST.leanTorque` is `onBody(600)`, 1852 N.m.
Its own doc comment's table picks 1500 (before the stone body's mass factor) as "the smallest
ceiling at which neither option reaches its stop and neither is stuck", and records 600 as stuck,
"458 stuck steps": a trunk is an inverted pendulum and at 600 the motor cannot hold it back. The
value was moved to 600 on 2026-09-20 by a line comment ("600/360 provides movement headroom while
retaining finite impact response"). It is a third motor ceiling in `src/golem/config.ts` that breaks
the house rule the operational notes name for `CHAIN_REACH` and `HEAD_NECK`.

The same six bouts with the ceiling at 2.5 times shipped, the table's 1500 on the body's factor
(counterfactual, put back after each bout, not a retune):

| waist lean ceiling | falls | bout seconds | falls a minute | head back at the fall, median |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1852 N.m (shipped) | 36 | 394 | 5.5 | 0.54 m |
| 4630 N.m (the table's 1500) | 0 | 719 | 0 | - |

**The fall is geometric, not a blow.** A body pressed at the chest by another body folds at a
motor that cannot hold its trunk against gravity, and its centre of mass leaves its base.

### 4.2 The cost of commitment

Headless arena. One full stroke at the air from a settled guard, flat-footed and with the stroke's
step, the other body 12 m off:

| body | stroke done | back to guard | fall impulse before -> worst during, flat | stepped | lean on its own ledger | tip peak |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| default (blade) | 0.44 s | 0.74 s | 115 -> 115 | 115 | 0 | 31.5 m/s |
| mace | 0.52 | 1.27 | 124 -> 107 | 79 | 0 | 31.8 |
| maul | 0.52 | 1.22 | 143 -> **9** | 74 | 0 | 19.0 |
| fists | 0.44 | 0.69 | 110 -> 106 | 86 | 0 | 12.9 |
| whip | 0.67 | - | 114 -> 109 | 109 | 0 | 28.8 |
| ram-capped | - | - | 101 -> 101 | 93 | 0 | - |
| skeleton-warrior | 0.44 | 0.89 | 9 -> **0** | 0 | 0 | 21.2 |
| human-warrior | 0.44 | 1.11 | 48 -> 45 | 45 | 0 | 12.6 |
| human-maul | 0.52 | 0.94 | 46 -> 40 | 10 | 0 | 4.7 |

"Back to guard" is from the stroke's start until the tip is within 0.1 m of its guard place in the
carrier's frame and under 0.5 m/s. The whip and the ram never read as back (the lash does not return
to one place; the ram has no tip).

- **A miss is out of its guard for 0.7 to 1.3 s**, so there is a window to punish.
- **The swing's reaction never reaches the balance.** The lean a stroke files on its own ledger is
  zero on every body, and the carrier does not drift on a flat stroke. The standing carrier follows
  a virtual gait, and a stroke's reaction is not an input to it.
- **What a stroke does cost is where its mass goes.** The maul thrown flat moves the centre of mass
  to 10 mm inside the base, and its weakest line falls from 143 to 9 N.s at that instant. The
  skeleton's blade stroke takes its centre of mass 12 mm outside its base: it is standing on the
  support state's forgiveness. The stroke's step costs the stepping bodies a third of their line
  (mace 79, maul 74, fists 86, human maul 10).

**The live test, `punish-miss`** (drill runner, 40 starts a body; `node research/headroom-report.mjs`
prints the full table). A start is a miss by the reference, and a pass is punishing it.

- **The expert punishes nearly every miss on 15 of 30 bodies** (95 to 100 %). The duelist manages
  0 to 90 % on the same starts.
- The paired headroom is +40 on the stone default, +75 on the mace, +70 on the maul, +75 on the
  human warrior and +78 on the skeleton warrior.
- **It cannot be done with a ram, a whip or bare hands:**

  | body | expert pass rate |
  |---|---:|
  | `ram-capped` | 5 % |
  | `human-whip` | 12.5 % |
  | `skeleton-whip` | 12.5 % |
  | `human-unarmed` | 0 % |
  | stone `whip` | 47.5 % |

  The window exists, and these bodies have nothing fast enough to put into it.

So commitment has a cost, and it is time out of guard, not balance.

### 4.3 Precision pays

Scoring arithmetic, 200 J of striker kinetic energy a blow (the energy is `1/2 m v^2` with the
striker's effective mass at its striking point: blade 1.14 kg, mace 2.95, maul 7.59, fist 2.91, whip
0.12), priced at every part of each body with its armour. Bar per kJ, best part and worst:

| target | blade, edge at 1 | blade, edge at 0.4 | mace | maul | fist | whip |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| stone default | 2.01 head / 1.13 foot (1.8x) | 0.32 / 0.18 | 0.28 / 0.14 (2.0x) | 0.26 / 0.11 (2.4x) | 0.28 / 0.14 | 0.29 / 0.17 |
| plated | 2.01 head / 0.93 core (2.2x) | 0.32 / 0.15 | 0.28 / 0.13 | 0.26 / 0.11 | 0.28 / 0.13 | 0.29 / 0.13 |
| skeleton-warrior | 3.88 forearm / 0.72 waist (5.4x) | 0.62 / 0.12 | 1.02 / 0.16 (6.4x) | 0.85 / **0** foot | 1.02 / 0.16 | 1.16 / 0.21 |
| human-warrior | 1.61 pelvis / 0.03 hand (58x) | 0.26 / 0.004 | 0.23 / 0.003 | 0.22 / 0.003 | 0.23 / 0.004 | 0.23 / 0.004 |

- **Blunt damage is exactly proportional to energy.** A crush scores `E / 436.3`, a cut
  `along^2 x E / 62.1`. So a club's only precision is target choice, and on stone that is worth 2x.
- **Precision pays through the edge, and through cut against crush.** The same 200 J is worth 7x
  more as a clean cut than as a crush, and a cut placed at 0.4 of the edge is worth 1/6 of one at
  the edge. Stone's head empties in 1.1 clean blade blows and in 8.1 mace blows.
- **Target choice pays on the thin bodies.** 5x to 6x on the skeleton; the human's 58x is its hand,
  whose vitality weight makes a blow there nearly worthless, which is a way to waste a blow rather
  than a lever.
- The maul's reduced mass against a skeleton's foot puts a 200 J blow under the crush floor
  (29.7 J at the contact), so it scores nothing.
