# Orders: a person commands, and every mind obeys

Skill ceiling 06, the orders half (`docs/plans/2026-09-25-skill-ceiling-06-commander-and-command-surface.md`).
The command-surface half is not part of this; `Intent` is unchanged.

Every figure names its harness. "Node bout runner" means `tests/harness/bout-runner.mjs` run
through `runJobs` in `research/runner.mjs`, with supported locomotion and the research `PROTOCOL`
at 120 Hz physics and control. "Fork harness" means the drill fork in `tests/harness/drills.mjs`
over that runner.

## What landed

Five commits on the worktree branch, in the order the plan asked for: plumbing, UI and dungeon,
drill, retirements. Every commit passes `npm test`, `npm run check` and `npm run build`.

1. **Plumbing** (683cbf7).
   - `src/orders.ts` defines `Orders { target, destination }`. A target is a body id, a ground
     point or null, and a destination is a ground point or null.
   - The `OrderFollower` carries a destination out on top of any mind's command. It takes the
     footwork only: arms, trunk and the natural striker stay the mind's, and the mind keeps its
     facing while the enemy is near. So a body under a move order goes on defending itself.
   - A body counts as arrived inside `ORDER_TUNING.arriveM` (0.3 m) and is walked back once it
     strays past `leashM` (0.8 m). Between the two, a holding body fights freely.
   - `Mind.decide(view, dt, orders?)` takes the orders beside the view. A mind that carries orders
     out itself says so with `obeysOrders`, and the follower then leaves it alone. None does today.
   - `ControlEndpoint.commander` is read by `GolemDriver.step` at every decision. The commanders are
     a person's `StandingOrders` and the auto-commanders `attack-nearest` and `hold-here`.
     `createBout` takes `leftCommander` and `rightCommander`.
   - **No orders is not an order.** With no commander, or one that hands over null, the driver
     makes the call it made before, argument for argument: `mind.decide(view, dt)`.
2. **The arena takes orders** (c75c488).
   - A person commands a side instead of taking a body:
     - click the enemy to fight it;
     - click the floor to go there and hold;
     - right-click the floor to attack-move;
     - WASD steers a destination a step ahead in the camera's frame (`STEER_LEAD_M`, 1.2 m);
     - H holds and X clears.
   - Each corner carries an auto-commander, chosen on the setup screen and carried in the matchup
     link. Command / Stand down replaces Take / Let go, and standing down hands the side back to its
     auto-commander.
   - `OrderDisplay` in `src/targeting.ts` draws the target's outline and the destination marker.
   - The old arena `Controls` moved into the bench as `PuppetControls`.
3. **Party orders in the dungeon** (0a6d9ce).
   - `DungeonRun` takes companions (two by default, up to three), built after the enemies so that
     no enemy's seed moves.
   - A mouse order goes to the selected party members:
     - everybody is selected until the person picks;
     - 1-4, a row or a click on the body selects one member;
     - Shift adds a member;
     - 0 selects everybody.
   - A companion sent somewhere holds there, F calls it back, and an idle companion follows the
     hero. Several members sent to one point take slots about it.
   - Enemies go for the nearest party member they see. The run is lost with the whole party and
     won by anybody at the exit.
   - Party members slide past each other in the group resolver, where strangers stop.
4. **The hold-under-orders drill** (814b81d). It joins session 03's suite through the same
   `DRILLS` table, so `research/drills.mjs` runs it unchanged. See below.
5. **Retirements** (c9173ae). See below.

## The drill: hold under orders

The subject is ordered to a point 1 to 1.5 m off to one side while a duelist attacks it. It
passes on three conditions:

- it arrives within 2 s;
- it stays inside the follower's leash for 95 % of the time after arriving;
- it takes a wound under 0.03, the same threshold survive-cut and get-inside use.

A start where the idle control takes no wound is void.

The drill was run 200 times (Node bout runner and fork harness, 4 lanes, 288 s wall). 150 runs
were scored and 50 were void.

| Rung | Arrived <= 2 s | Held >= 0.95 | Pass | Mean arrival | Mean wound (sd) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| idle | 150/150 | 150/150 | 0.0 % | 0.589 s | 0.114 (0.065) |
| golem-walker | 150/150 | 150/150 | 4.0 % | 0.607 s | 0.147 (0.091) |
| golem-duelist | 150/150 | 150/150 | 10.0 % | 0.607 s | 0.122 (0.089) |
| golem-duelist-guardless | 150/150 | 150/150 | 30.0 % | 0.607 s | 0.077 (0.067) |

Paired by start, n = 150:

| Comparison | Pass-rate difference |
| --- | --- |
| walker - idle | 4.0 +/- 3.1 points |
| duelist - idle | 10.0 +/- 4.8 points |
| guardless - idle | 30.0 +/- 7.4 points |
| duelist - walker | 6.0 +/- 5.6 points |
| guardless - walker | 26.0 +/- 8.0 points |
| guardless - duelist | 20.0 +/- 8.5 points |

**Obedience is not the gap.** Every rung arrives and holds on every scored start. The pass rate is
set by the wound taken over a live 3 s exchange, and there the guardless duelist beats the duelist
by 20 points. Guarding does not beat not guarding. That is a finding about the duelist's guard,
not about orders, and it belongs to the expert and the headroom sessions.

**What obeying costs.** For 40 of the drill's own starts (22 scored both ways), each rung was run
with orders and without them, the second with the subject left unordered on the same start. This
was the fork harness called directly (`runDrill` in `tests/harness/drills.mjs`), in four
processes. Ordered wound minus free wound, paired by start, mean +/- 1.96 standard errors:

| Rung | Ordered - free wound |
| --- | --- |
| idle | +0.005 +/- 0.036 |
| walker | +0.022 +/- 0.037 |
| duelist | -0.005 +/- 0.059 |
| guardless | -0.010 +/- 0.029 |

None is distinguishable from zero. On this drill, moving under orders costs a mind nothing
measurable in wound.

## Retirements

Before each deletion, every reader in `src/`, `tests/`, `research/` and `scripts/` was listed. No
benchmark mind and no `research/` script in use (drills, league, side-mirror, stat-sweep,
release-baseline) read any of the targets. The research scripts read `result.behaviour[side]`'s
`rangeBins`, `engagement`, `blocks` and `seconds`, and all four are kept.

`research/results/provenance.json` names `src/buttons.ts` and `src/options.ts`. It is a historical
fingerprint that `research/cli.mjs publish` wrote from the live tree. Nothing reads those paths
back, and it breaks nothing. **Nothing was refused.**

| Retired | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| `splitMind`, `handover`, `handoverFromCursors`, `humanMind`, `HumanOwnership` | Deleted from `src/mind.ts`, along with `installHuman`/`releaseHuman`, `HumanDriverSource`, `humanDriver`, `lockTarget` and the `human`/`cursorSeed` options on the control endpoint, the golem and the unit rows. The endpoint's unread policy-name bookkeeping, which existed to restore a policy after a release, also went. |
| Cursor and pose inverses | Deleted: `cursorForAzimuth`/`cursorForElevation` and the rest of the azimuth block in `src/policies.ts`; `cursorForPose`, `cursorsForPoses`, `HandCursor(s)` and `poseShiftMm` in `src/mind.ts`; every chain's `cursor()` inverse; `Golem.cursorSeed` and `drivenPose`. |
| `src/buttons.ts` | **Moved, not deleted**, to `src/bench/buttons.ts`, because the bench puppet still presses buttons and has to keep the levels-not-edges rule. `BUTTON_REACH`, the reach a hand at rest asks for, is read by bodies, minds and tests, so it moved to `HAND_REACH` in `src/hands.ts`. The bow's `nextDraw` had no reader left and went. |
| The takeover UI | Take / Let go were replaced by Command / Stand down in c75c488. The `takeover` config block, `Matchup.channels`, `withChannels`, `drivesMove` and `drivesAttack` went now. Old links that carry `channels` still decode, into a commanded side. |
| `COMBAT_FIELDS` parity fixtures | `tests/fixtures/intent.mjs` was deleted. The remaining camera-state check reads the field set off `NEUTRAL`. |
| `src/options.ts` and the recorder's use of it | Deleted, with `tests/options.test.mjs` and `tests/human-ownership.test.mjs`. The behaviour record (contacts, blocks, damage, range bins, engagement) moved into `src/recorder.ts`, without its option-keyed fields (`options`, `transitions`, `attackAttempts`, `longestOptionOccupancySeconds`), which nothing in `research/` read. `src/action-primitives.ts` lost the exports only the option layer read. `ACTION_TUNING` stays frozen, and `action_primitives_have_no_mutable_config_backdoor` pins it. |

The test count went from 1046 to 994. The 52 missing tests are exactly the ones in the deleted
files and sections, less the two tests added. `AGENTS.md`'s first house rule was rewritten: the
authority half stays, and the parity half became "a person commands and does not puppet". The
input traps were updated or cut in the same commit.

One defect was found and fixed on the way. The shadow-caster exclusion in `src/arena-room.ts`
still named the retired `takeover.` meshes, so the new `orders.` markers cast shadows. It names
`orders.` now.

## For the owner's eye

The eye gate is deferred. What to look at, on the dev server:

- **An arena bout:**
  - clicking the enemy, clicking the floor, right-click attack-move;
  - whether orders are obeyed promptly;
  - whether a fighter under a move order still defends itself.
- **A holding body:** it should drift freely between 0.3 and 0.8 m of its point while it fights
  and be walked back past that. Check that this reads as holding ground and not as a twitch.
- **WASD steering:** whether a destination 1.2 m ahead in the camera's frame feels like steering.
- **The order markers:** the target outline and the destination marker are visible, and neither
  casts a shadow.
- **A dungeon party run:**
  - selection by key, row and click;
  - companions holding their posts, and F regrouping them;
  - members sliding past each other in a corridor;
  - the slot spread when several are sent to one point;
  - the frame cost of the two-companion default, on Firefox especially.
- **The bench puppet** (`/bench.html`) still drives a module by mouse.
