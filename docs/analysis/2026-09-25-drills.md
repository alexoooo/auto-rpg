## 1. How a start is built, and why not by teleport

A drill run builds its start once, in an original world, and plays every rung from an exact fork of
it.

1. **The bodies are walked there under physics.** Both bodies are built facing each other at a
   seeded separation of 2.3 to 2.6 m. A scripted driver (`DrillDriver`) then walks each to the
   drill's range. The range is measured on each body's own published striker: socket to mark for a
   hand, carrier to carrier for a natural striker, so a multileg, a wheel and a biped are placed by
   one rule. The drill's action runs on the same clock: a committed cut, a guard held on a stated
   line, a shove past a body's fall line.
2. **At t0 the world is captured with Havok's heap**, and each rung is an `exactFork` of the
   capture: a fresh instance and the same world to the bit.
3. **Minds.** A mind already running is snapshotted and restored through `src/fork/mind.ts`. The
   subject's rung mind is built fresh at t0.

**Why walked rather than teleported.** A teleported body starts out of its own equilibrium, and the
solver's warm start is cold. That is the "floor" in `docs/analysis/2026-09-25-fork.md`, and such a
body would have to settle anyway. Walking it there costs a second of simulation and leaves a body
standing the way it stands. Seeded jitter comes from the separation, the range fraction, the line,
the pause and the lead, all drawn from the run's seed. The subject's side is drawn too, so the runs
split between the corners.

**Paired by start.** Every rung of a run plays one start, so every comparison between rungs is a
paired difference per start. `summarizeDrill` reports it with its standard error.

`every_rung_plays_the_same_start_and_no_rung_leaks_into_the_next` (`tests/drills.test.mjs`) holds
that a rung's result is identical whether it is played alone or after others. Mutating the harness to
share one event list across rungs turns it red. Sharing the scripted drivers across rungs does *not*
turn it red, and correctly so: the exact fork restores the driver's plain fields from the capture,
because the world graph walks `golem.mind`.

**The prelude is clean.** Staging went through four repairs before a start stopped sparring on the
way in:

- the striker is carried in a pose aimed at the other body, with the spare hand tucked;
- a scripted stroke is followed by holding its follow-through;
- each driver is fed the other body's range, off that body's own view, because an opponent's
  capabilities are hidden from a view;
- the forward command is guarded against a non-finite first publication.

In the odd-morphology pilots every start reached had a prelude damage of 0. On the default pair a
few survive-cut and punish-miss starts still carry a stray prelude contact, recorded in each run's
`start.preludeDamage`.

## 2. The drills and their verdicts

Drill runner, default golem against itself, 1,000 runs a drill. The opponent's mind, where a drill
hands it one, is `golem-duelist`. "Runs for ±2" is p(1 − p)(1.96/0.02)²: the runs needed for the
pass rate to be known to ±2 points at 95 %. Paired differences are in points, as ±1.96 SE per start.

### survive-cut: their committed cut arriving

- **Setup.** The opponent walks to 0.80 to 0.95 of its own striker range and holds for 0.3 to 0.7 s.
  It then cuts at one of three lines (high +0.3 m, middle, low −0.35 m on the mark). t0 falls in
  the first half of the chamber.
- **Pass.** A wound under **0.03** of the bar within 1 s.
- **Control: idle.** A start whose cut does not wound an idle body over the threshold is void. The
  forks are exact, so the cut idle took is the cut every rung faced.
- **The threshold was set from 90 pilot starts.** An idle body takes a mean 0.089 from a high cut.
  At 0.03 the high line read idle 0 of 37, walker 1, duelist 14 and guardless 0. At 0.01 the pooled
  counts had idle passing 13 of 60 and the duelist 7, because a cut at the middle and low lines
  often did not reach an idle body at all. That is what the control is for.

| idle | 0.0 % | 0.000 | 0 | 0.067 (0.027) |

| duelist | 27.5 % | 0.199 | 1914 | 0.061 (0.047) |

### get-inside: a longer-armed opponent holding its stand-off

- **Setup.** The opponent is the same body at size 1.25. It stands, and strikes (0.5 s cooldown)
  whenever the subject is inside its strike fraction. The subject is walked to 1.02 to 1.12 of the
  *opponent's* range.
- **Pass.** Reach the subject's own striking range within 3 s with a wound under 0.03. Striking
  range is 0.92 of the striker's range for a hand, and the whole of reach plus lunge for a natural
  striker. A ram's carrier gap cannot close past the two bodies' collision radii (0.92 m on the
  wheel pair, against a ram's 0.68 + 0.35 m), so no smaller fraction of it is a place a body can be.

**Verdict: keep, as a headroom drill.**

- The duelist fails it by rule. Against a longer arm its hold is its own reach, so it sits at a

- **Not understood: the guardless duelist passes every run.**
  - Against an idle larger body, the duelist and the guardless duelist close identically to 0.8 of

  - So the difference needs the opponent's strikes.

- **Setup.** The subject stands at 0.82 to 0.90 of its range. At t0 the opponent walks in at 0.25 to

- **Control: idle.** A start where the closing opponent does not drive an idle body out of the band

  idle held it in 3 runs of 3.
- **Needs** a hand striker on the subject.

- **Needs** a striker that reaches the ground (`groundReach`: the socket, minus the reach at the

## 3. The three drills removed

Drill runner, default golem against itself, 40 runs each. In each drill idle passed at least as
often as the duelist:

| drill | start | success | idle | walker | duelist | guardless |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| recover | shoved at 0.80 to 0.95 of the fall line | still up after 2 s | 40 | 40 | 34 | 40 |
| recover-up | shoved at 1.1 to 1.3 of the line | up again inside 2 s | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| off-balance | opponent pre-shoved at 0.55 to 0.80 of its line | down it within 1 s | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

The reasons were measured on the Node bout runner with the default golem: a fall line of 0.45 to
0.48 m/s, a mass of 247 kg and a fall impulse of about 117 N·s.

- A shove's lean is the locomotion layer's to right. The ledger decays at about 1.2 to 1.3 m/s², so a
  shove under the fall line rights itself in 0.25 to 0.3 s whatever the mind asks.
- A fall's rise takes 2.7 to 3.0 s whatever the mind asks. In recover-up the mean was 2.93 to
  2.97 s, censored at the horizon, for every rung.
- No rung's blow reached a pre-shoved body before its lean had decayed.

**Balance is not yet a thing a mind can do, so a drill of it measures nothing.** It comes back when
the command surface gives a mind a lever on it. That is session 06's orders, or a sustained-press
stage, which would have to hold a lean against the decay rather than add one impulse to it.

## 6. The league protocol

`research/league.mjs` is the one protocol `runJobs` runs for a full-bout comparison. Its worker is
`research/league-worker.mjs`.

- **Asymmetric pairs, reported by weapon class.** There are seven weapon classes, each a named build:
  blade (`default`), two-blades, mace, maul, whip, fist (`fists`) and ram (`ram-capped`). Each is
  played against itself with one body module changed: `torso.plated`, `locomotion.multileg`,
  `locomotion.wheel` or `head.ram`.
  - A variant the registry refuses is left out and said so, and so is one that changes nothing. Only
    ram + ram is omitted, which leaves 27 pairs.
  - Both corners carry the class's weapon, which is what lets a report be read by weapon class.
- **The unit is the corner-swapped pair.** Mind A on one body against B on the other, A left and then
  A right, with each mind keeping its own seed (the pair is reversed with the sides). A cluster is
  one seed pair on one class and variant: two corner-swapped pairs, one for each body assignment.
- **The paired criterion.** Cohen's d of A's bar margin over corner-swapped pairs. The interval is a
  bootstrap over whole clusters.
- **Side-split scores.** A's score on the left and on the right.
- **Structural guard columns, printed and never optimised.**
  - Near-range stall seconds and retreat-outside-reach seconds a bout, per mind (the recorder's
    `EngagementTracker`).
  - Changes of lead a bout: a lead is a bar difference over 0.02, and inside that band the lead stays
    where it was.
  - The winner's remaining bar.
  - The share decided before the cap: ending `exhausted` with a winner.
  - Falls a bout: edges into `fallen` on each body's published support state, both bodies summed.
- **The mirror.** A = B, read by the left corner's score against 50 % ± 1.96·√(0.25/n) (session 01's
  gate).
- **Not yet done: bouts start from release 1's built-at-guard opening.** That opening is not on this
  branch. The league's bouts start the way `createBout` does today.

`tests/league.test.mjs` holds the arithmetic without a bout:

- the pairs are asymmetric by one module and keep the weapon;
- seeds and bodies travel with their minds;
- a mind winning everything scores 1 on both sides;
- a left-biased mirror fails the gate and a level one passes;
- the cap is not a decision;
- the lead counter's band.

Four mutations each turned it red: the margin read off fixed sides, seeds left in place across the
swap, the mirror reading A instead of the left corner, and the lead counter without its band.

**The walker's mirror passes.** League, blade class against its four variants, 8 clusters, 128
bouts: the left corner scored 51.6 % [48.4, 54.7] against a band of ±8.7.

- Every bout was decided before the cap, in a mean 39.4 s.
- The winner kept 0.670 of its bar, with 0.63 changes of lead and 0.19 falls a bout.
- The walker stalls at near range for 31.8 s a bout, which is most of each bout. It walks to its hold
  and swings on a clock whether or not anything is open, so a drought of progress at near range is
  what it does.
