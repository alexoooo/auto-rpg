## 1. Design

A **receding-horizon sampling search over plans, on exact forks of the live world.** Every
`1 / decisionHz` seconds (4 Hz by default) the expert does the following:

1. It captures the world with Havok's heap (`captureBout(bout, { heap: true })`).
2. It scores each candidate plan by rolling the plan out for `horizon` seconds in an exact fork.
3. It commits the best plan and plays it until the next decision.

**A candidate is a plan, not a command list.** A plan has one or two segments and a switch time.
Each segment is a small closed-loop controller over the published view:

- **`follow`**: a naive-ladder mind's continuation, with forward and strafe offsets;
- **`hold`**: the last command, held;
- **`stance`**: footwork, pointing at their shoulder or covering a line;
- **`stroke`**: one stroke at a sampled mark, lifted and moved sideways, then a covering stance.

Every segment reads the view every substep. So the prefix the expert commits to reproduces the
rollout's commands exactly whenever the world does. That is what
`a_rollout_is_the_future_it_predicts_in_a_bout` holds, bit for bit, on the odd morphology.

**Proposals.** Round 1 is a fixed list in priority order, and round 2 jitters the best few.

- The fixed list: the duelist's own continuation, the plan in progress carried warm, cut-mid,
  step-in-and-cut, back off, cut-high, press, the walker, guard, cut-then-back-off, circle left and
  right, cut-low, hold, duelist-then-cut, and back-off-then-cut.
- A decision on which every candidate ties is the duelist's, because ties go to the earlier
  candidate.
- The search does not care where a plan came from.

**The command surface is `Intent`**, the controller a person uses, as the owner decided. The
segments write it through the same tactics helpers the naive minds use (`aimAt`, `writeAim`,
`strokeCommand`, `pointAt`, `restHand`). Nothing reaches past `Intent`.

**The objective** is `scoreRollout`, in bars, with every term logged per decision:

| term | weight | what |
|---|---:|---|
| damage | 1 | bar dealt minus bar taken over the horizon |
| end | 1 | +1 / −1 if the bout is decided inside it |
| down | 0.08 | each body's terminal support state (fallen 1, rising 0.5), theirs minus mine |
| position | 0.03 | how far my striker's range fraction ends outside [0.65, 1.0] |
| stall | 0.03 / s | session 03's near-range stall seconds, accrued by me |
| retreat | 0.03 / s | session 03's retreat-outside-reach seconds, accrued by me |
| task (drills only) | 1 | the drill's own judge over the rollout (`DRILL_TASKS`) |

- **Stalling is priced**, as the owner asked. Across the league gate the expert's stall and retreat
  columns read 0.00 s a bout, against the duelist's 0.00 too.
- **In a drill** the rollout stops where the rung does, and the drill's judge scores it in the fork:
  - a pass is worth 1;
  - the drill's margin shapes the score on either side of a pass: a wound, a time to pass, the
    share in band.

  `a_rollout_is_the_future_it_predicts_in_a_drill_rung` checks every candidate's task term against
  survive-cut's pass less the rollout's own wound.
- **`-bout`** plays a drill on the bout objective alone. It is the control for the task term.
  - It is level with the task objective on punish-miss and land-clean-blow.
  - It is 30 points worse on survive-cut, because a wound under 0.03 is not what the bout
    objective prices.
  - It holds range in **0 %** of hold-range starts, because the bout objective's 0.03 a fraction
    for position is outweighed by a blow.

**The two instruments**, as the owner decided, full knowledge first:

- **Full knowledge.** Inside a rollout the opponent is its real mind. The fork restores it from the
  capture, and its random streams are reseeded once per decision, so every candidate is compared on
  common random numbers. The expert knows the policy and not the dice.
  - The duelist draws its dice only on a few state changes. Over a 1 s rollout it barely draws, so
    against the duelist full knowledge is close to perfect prediction.
  - That is why the prediction test's control is the persistence model and not restarted dice.
    With the dice restarted, every prediction still came true.
- **Model-only (persistence).** The opponent in a rollout keeps applying the command it applied
  last in the live world.

**Two experts in one bout** (the mirror).

- Both take their moment before either searches, so neither sees the plan the other is choosing on
  the same frame.
- In a rollout, the other corner plays its committed plan forward from its own restored shadows.
  `an_expert_opponent_is_played_forward_from_its_own_plan` holds this bit for bit.

**The expert's own state is not world state.** The world graph pairs its slot with an empty
`Forkable` (`ExpertShell`).

- Its shadows travel by `snapshotMind` / `restoreMind`. The shadows are the duelist and the walker,
  kept running beside it so that a plan can follow one.
- Its plan in progress travels by `Program.clone`.

**Fork reuse.**

- One fork world is kept and restored for every candidate. Havok's whole memory is copied over it
  and the JavaScript graph is rewritten, which is the same restore a fresh fork gets.
- It is rebuilt when the capture's topology differs from its own (a sever, a ruin or a grip), or
  when a rollout changed its own.
- `a_reused_fork_is_a_fresh_fork` holds that a reused fork chooses and predicts exactly what a fresh
  exact fork per candidate does.
- In the league gate it built 925 fork worlds for 21,944 rollouts.

**Timing.** A search forks Havok instances, which is asynchronous, and a mind's `decide` runs inside
the solver's step. So the search runs between frames:

- in a bout, `runExpertBout` awaits it before each frame;
- in a drill, the drill runner's planner hook does.

The drill runner builds a rung's planner with `rungFactory`, and keeps the planner's summary on the
rung's result.

**Mutation checks**, each run and each turned red:

- the shadows not restored into a rollout;
- a capture without Havok's heap (the teleport fork);
- a reused fork not restored;
- an expert opponent left to its fork slot's shell, or played without its shadows;
- the drill task judging the live world, never framed, or left off;
- the search keeping the worst plan;
- `-lag` searching with no stale capture, or on the live moment, or parsed without its wait.

Two mutations survive, and both are equivalent here:

- **not cloning a warm plan**, because the moment already holds a clone and only one rollout of a
  fresh moment plays it;
- **the drill host's range feed**, because no drill reads it inside a rung.

The runner's wall limit per job is now an option (`jobLimitMs`, `--job-minutes`): an expert bout
takes longer than five minutes. The limit is held by a test in `tests/research.test.mjs`, which was
mutation-checked.

### Drills

The drill runner, paired by start against the ladder: idle, walker and duelist, plus the guardless

The three-second drills (`research/runs/expert-drills-long`, n = 60 each):

- **get-inside.** The expert beats the duelist by 8.3 ± 7.1 points and by 0.76 ± 0.17 s inside.
  - It does not beat the walker, which walks straight in at 0.33 s against the expert's 0.38
    (−0.048 ± 0.026).
  - The duelist fails this drill by its stand-off rule (session 03). The walker is the ladder's
    best rung here, and the expert matches its pass rate without matching its speed.
- **hold-range.** Everyone at or above the duelist passes. The expert's share in band is 0.996
  against the duelist's 0.981 (+0.014 ± 0.009). The walker cannot pass it by construction.
- **finish.** Saturated on pass rate, and level on time to first blow: 0.112 against 0.095 s
  (−0.017 ± 0.024). The duelist already strikes a downed body's live core at once (`finishPoint`).
  The expert's first candidate *is* the duelist's continuation, and it leaves it only for a plan
  it scores higher: a heavier blow a moment later.

**Where the gate is thin.** On get-inside's time and finish's time, what the expert maximises and
what the drill's margin measures are not the same quantity. Its task term prices the pass at 1 and a
second of earliness at 0.25 / 3 bars, so a heavier or safer blow 50 ms later outscores a lighter one
now. That is a property of the objective and not a fault in the fork or the search:
- the shorter horizon (h0.5) closes the gap to the walker;
- the lighter c4 search is the slower one on finish, so more search helps rather than hurts.

If session 05 needs these margins as headroom, the task term should weight time more heavily. That
is an owner decision, because it changes what "better" means in a drill.

### A blinded fork

The plan's mutation is "a fork that restores a stale state". The drill grid ran it as `-blind`: four
decisions stale, and, before four decisions have passed, the oldest capture held. **In a one-second
rung that means the first decision is exact and each later one plans on the start again.**

| rung | survive-cut | punish-miss | land-clean-blow (s to clean blow) |
|---|---|---|---|
| full (c8, h1) | 86.0 % | 100 % | 100 % (0.206) |
| `-blind` (stale 4) | 83.9 % (−2.1) | 59.0 % (−41.0) | 97.0 % (0.522) |
| `-lag` (stale 1, no search before it) | 29.0 % (−57.0) | 77.0 % (−23.0) | 100 % (0.316) |

- `-blind` is level with full knowledge on survive-cut, the drill whose outcome is settled by the
  first decision. That decision is exact under `-blind`, and every later one re-chooses the same
  parry from the same start.
- `-lag` was added for that reason. It is the lightest stale fork there is: one decision (0.25 s)
  old, and no search at all until a capture that old exists.
  - On survive-cut it is the duelist (−3.2 ± 7.0 against the duelist), because its first quarter
    second *is* the duelist's.
  - On the three-second drills (n = 60):
    - get-inside: 96.7 % and 0.477 s inside, against full knowledge's 100 % and 0.378 s;
    - hold-range: level (100 %, 0.995);
    - finish: it never searched. The pass comes at 0.1 s, before its first search at 0.25 s, so it
      is the duelist there by construction.
  - So it is measurably worse than full knowledge wherever it gets to search before the drill is
    decided.
- The lag rung was run on the grid's starts in a directory of its own
  (`research/runs/expert-drills-lag`) and joined by job id. Its duelist rung is identical to the
  grid's in every one of the joined runs, which confirms that the starts are the same.

The lesson is about the mutation. A stale fork whose staleness starts after the drill has been
decided detects nothing, and a green check of it would have been a detector that could not fire.

- **Survive-cut is reading.** A parry has to be chosen before the cut shows, and only the

- **The other five drills are search.** On punish-miss, land-clean-blow, get-inside and finish,
  persistence is within a few points and a few hundredths of a second of full knowledge. It holds

- **In the league, the gap is 14 points of score and more than half the margin** (0.833 against
  0.389).
  Model-only's A-left and A-right are 93.8 and 78.1 %. That is within the noise at 32 bouts a side,
  but it should be watched.
- So the plan's warning applies, and is measured here. Full knowledge against the duelist is close
  to perfect prediction, because the duelist barely draws dice. Part of its margin is reading this
  one opponent, and a body's headroom read by it carries that part.

## 7. What this leaves for session 05

- **The ruler.** `expert@c8,h1` at 4 Hz: flat against compute on the drills measured, at 11 to
  13 s of wall a game second.
  - A league cell of 128 bouts against the duelist costs about 2.3 lane-hours, and longer bouts
    cost proportionally more.
  - One c8 rung of a drill costs 0.4 lane-hours for 200 one-second runs, and about 2 for 200
    three-second runs.
- **Read the persistence expert beside it.** A body whose headroom shows only under full knowledge
  is being read through the duelist's predictability.
- **Survive-cut's low line** is the one place where more search still paid, from 20 % at c4 to 33 %
  at c32. It is worth a closer look before it is called a body's ceiling. A proposal that covers a
  low line on purpose is the first thing to try.
- **Hold the horizon fixed.** On the three-second drills it trades earliness against what follows,
  so a change of horizon changes the question as well as the budget.
- **Decisions for the owner:**
  - **Is `expert@c8,h1` at 4 Hz the ruler?** It is flat against candidates. It costs 12× real time.
  - **Full knowledge or persistence as the ruler, or both side by side.** Full knowledge reads the
    duelist almost perfectly, and persistence is closer to "a fact about the body".
  - **Should the drill task weight time more heavily?** If it does, the expert beats the walker on
    get-inside and the duelist on finish's time; as it stands it does not.
  - **Which mutation stands as the blinded-fork sanity check.** `-lag` bites everywhere the
    expert searches before the drill is decided. `-blind` (stale 4) is inert on survive-cut.
