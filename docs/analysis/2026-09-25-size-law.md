# Size law: from dynamic similarity to biology

2026-09-25. Skill ceiling session 01, part 2 (`docs/plans/2026-09-25-skill-ceiling-01-body-release-1.md`
section 2, and item 8 of `docs/plans/2026-09-25-skill-ceiling-00-overview.md`).

**The question.** The size stat scaled a body's strength by dynamic similarity: force as s³ and
torque as s⁴. That made a larger body exactly as strong for its mass as a small one, and it moved
on one clock, sqrt(s). The owner asked for the biological law instead: force as s², torque as s³,
so that a larger body is weaker and slower for its mass. Which other laws does that move, what does
the bench then allow as a range, and does the stat still do something in a fight?

**Answer.**

- **Force is s² and torque s³.** Every drive then runs on a clock of s: its times go as s and its
  rates as 1/s. A limb's point speed is s⁰, so a larger body's limbs move no faster and turn more
  slowly. Gravity keeps its own clock of sqrt(s). So a knockdown's lie and rest times, and the
  shove that fells a body, keep the powers they had, under new names.
- **The range is x0.8 to x1.1. It was x0.8 to x1.25.**
  - The floor is still the arm. At x0.75 the wrist blade strays three times its x1 figure and the
    pitch hinge overshoots by 0.85 rad, under either law.
  - The ceiling is now the stone biped's foot slip. It leaves its 300 mm/s budget between x1.1
    (253 mm/s) and x1.15 (413 mm/s).
  - What drives that slip is not the law of the leg torques. It is `LOCOMOTION_BIPED.targetRate`,
    which at 120 Hz sits five to ten per cent above a cliff at x1. With 10 % more rate the walk
    holds its budget from x0.75 to x1.5. That is a tuning of the x1 walk at 120 Hz and is left to
    that tuning. Done, it would put the ceiling back at about x1.25, bounded by the arms.
- **The stat still does something, and only below x1.** Clustered by mind pairing, x0.8 scores
  -0.28 ± 0.11 against the x1 control and x0.9 scores -0.20 ± 0.07. x1.1 scores -0.05 ± 0.17: it
  fells the other body more often but does not win by it. Over the new row, size is a penalty
  below x1 and buys nothing measurable above it (section 6).

**Base.** Every figure below was measured on the branch `worktree-agent-a2f255c16aa8ce981`, based on
release-120 at 5ac61ce (physics and control at 120 Hz). `git log --oneline release-120..main` was
empty when the sweep started, so release-120 was main's tip, and nothing was rebased. The "old
law" columns are the same base with `SIZE_LAW_POWER` at its old values.

## 1. The laws

`SIZE_LAW_POWER` in `src/golem/attributes.ts`. Its doc comment carries the argument for each law.

| Law | Old power | New power | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| `one` | 0 | 0 | angles, ratios, health, armour, a saturated damping |
| `length` | 1 | 1 | every metre |
| `perLength` | -1 | -1 | a stride's radians per metre of foot travel |
| `mass` | 3 | 3 | constant density |
| `inertia` | 5 | 5 | mass times length squared |
| `force` | 3 | **2** | a muscle's cross-section. No table carries one today |
| `torque` | 4 | **3** | a force on a lever |
| `speed` | 0.5 | **0** | a linear rate a drive holds: a joint's rate times its lever |
| `frequency` | -0.5 | **-1** | an angular rate, a servo response, a damping |
| `duration` | 0.5 | **1** | the time a drive takes: a lunge, a ramp, a rise |
| `acceleration` | (was `one`) | **-1** | force over mass: a carrier's acceleration. A new law |
| `angularAcceleration` | -1 | **-2** | torque over inertia |
| `fallSpeed` | (was `speed`, 0.5) | 0.5 | a speed gravity sets: a lying body still coming down |
| `fallDuration` | (was `duration`, 0.5) | 0.5 | a time gravity sets: a fall finishing and settling |
| `fallImpulse` | (was `impulse`, 3.5) | 3.5 | the impulse that tips a body: its mass times its tipping line |

**The argument.**

- **Drives.** An actuator turning a link through a fixed angle has an angular acceleration of torque
  over inertia, s³ / s⁵ = s⁻². So the time it takes goes as s, and every rate it holds as 1/s. A
  point on the link, s out, then moves at s / s = s⁰. That is Hill's result: animals of one shape
  run at about one top speed whatever their size. A linear drive (legs pushing the carrier)
  accelerates at force over mass, s² / s³ = s⁻¹. Damping is on this clock too, because it exists to
  settle the drives, and at the drive's own frequency the loop keeps its damping ratio.
- **Gravity.** What gravity alone does has no actuator in it: a body tipping over its base, and a
  lying body settling. Dynamic similarity never changed those. A pendulum's period goes as
  sqrt(length) whatever its muscles. So they keep sqrt(s) under their own names, so that no drive
  field can pick them up by accident.
- **Under dynamic similarity the two clocks were one.** Torque s⁴ on inertia s⁵ is s⁻¹, so drive
  time went as sqrt(s), the same as gravity's. That coincidence is what made the similarity exact.
  It is also why the old tables could put a knockdown's rest time and a rise under one `duration`.

**What moved, table by table.**

- `CARRIER_SIZE` (`src/golem/config.ts`): speeds `speed` (now s⁰), and acceleration
  `acceleration`, which was `one`. A larger body walks no faster: 3.2 m/s at every size, where it
  was 3.92 m/s at x1.5.
- `KNOCKDOWN_SIZE`: rest speed, rest seconds and the lying cap are on the fall clock
  (`fallSpeed`, `fallDuration`, the powers they had). The rise's peak speed is a drive's `speed`
  (s⁰).
- `shoveImpulseNs` in `LOCOMOTION_BIPED_SIZE`, `LOCOMOTION_WHEEL_SIZE` and
  `LOCOMOTION_MULTILEG_SIZE` is `fallImpulse`, the power `impulse` had. Each slip budget stays a
  `speed` (now s⁰), and each `riseBudgetSeconds` stays a `duration` (now s).
- The supported locomotion port (`src/supported-locomotion-state.ts`): `sizeTime` was one factor,
  sqrt(s), for both floors of a knockdown. It is now two:
  - `sizeFallTime` (sqrt(s)) sets the fallen dwell;
  - `sizeDriveTime` (s) sets the rising floor. `KnockdownSettle.risingDurationS` in
    `src/golem/locomotion.ts` and the production rise in `src/supported-locomotion-production.ts`
    use the same factor.
- Every arm chain, the waist, the neck, the ram's lunge and the biped's legs already named their
  fields `torque`, `frequency`, `duration` and `speed`, so their numbers moved with the law. None
  of their field assignments changed.
- `strokeTimeScale` in `src/golem/tactics.ts` reads the sized chain, so it now goes as about s: 1.10
  at x1.1, where it was about sqrt(s).

**What a larger body now is.** Against its own weight it holds a torque of s³ against a load of s⁴,
so it is weaker for its mass by 1/s. Its limbs move at the same tip speed as the x1 body's and take
s times as long to cross an angle. What fells it grows as its mass does. Items keep their size, as
before, so a larger body is also a relatively smaller weapon.

## 2. The bench, and two artefacts in it

**Harness.** Node bench, `research/size-bench.mjs` (checked in by this session at ab60a47), release-120
at 5ac61ce.

- `arm` mode builds one chain on the bench stand at the size and runs `runGolemBench` (the rest
  column) and `runStrokeBench` (the stroke column).
- `move` mode walks each locomotion module on the headless arena through `runGolemLocomotion`.
- `body` mode reads a whole standing golem's mass and its stability diagnostic.

**The harness walks run into the ring of posts.** `WALK_SEQUENCE` (6 s) and `FAST_WALK_SEQUENCE` (3 s)
in the locomotion harness were cut for 1.2 and 2.0 m/s carriers. Since 2026-09-18 every carrier but
the multileg does 3.2 m/s, so both walks reach the arena's posts at 9.5 m. A trace of the wheel put
the start of its slip at 9.2 m of travel. Before this was found, the wheel appeared to fail at small
sizes under the new law, and big wheels appeared to fail under the old one. Both readings were the
posts. `move` therefore defaults to `--sequence clear`: stand 1 s, walk 2 s at full forward, stop 1
s. That covers 6.4 m at 3.2 m/s, and the `reach m` column shows it. On that course the wheel reads
0.0 mm/s of contact slip at every size from x0.6 to x2, under both laws. **Any test reading taken
over the harness's own walks at 3.2 m/s is contaminated by the posts.** That is an open item.

**The pitch hinge fails at x0.85 under both laws.** It takes 2.50 s to arrive, with 0.83 rad of
overshoot, under the new law. Under the old law the figures are 2.558 s and 0.85 rad. This is inside the
old range, and it is not the law: it is in the recover phase after the chop, and it did not move
for any rate or torque law tried. It is a pre-existing defect, and it is left as an open item.

## 5. The range, and what bounds each end

`ATTRIBUTES.size` is x0.8 to x1.1 in steps of 0.05. It was x0.8 to x1.25.

|---|---|---|---|
| floor | x0.8 | the arm: below it the wrist blade strays about three times its x1 figure and the pitch hinge overshoots | x0.75: wrist blade 143 mm, pitch arrival 1.34 s with 0.85 rad overshoot. x0.8: 83.9 mm, 0.283 s |
| ceiling | x1.1 | the stone biped's planted-sole slip against its 300 mm/s budget | x1.1: 253 mm/s. x1.15: 413 mm/s |

The pitch hinge's x0.85 failure (2.50 s) sits inside the range. It is pre-existing and not the
law's (section 2). The human stays fixed at x1 (`FAMILY_FIXED_ATTRIBUTES`), which the attributes
test pins.

**What fells a body did not move.** In `body` mode, stone's weakest-way fall impulse reads 62.1,
85.9, 117.1 and 156.9 N s at x0.8, x0.9, x1 and x1.1. The readings are the same under both laws,
because they are geometry and mass alone. That is about s³, not the s^3.5 of the shove tables'
`fallImpulse`: the live tipping line barely moves with size (0.479 to 0.480 m/s over the row).
The skeleton reads 0.00, 0.62, 3.99 and 8.44 N s. The table law and the live reading disagree by
s^0.5, and they did before this session too. That is an open item.

**Tests that moved with the range.**
