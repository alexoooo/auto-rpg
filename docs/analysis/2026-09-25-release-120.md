## 1. The arm servos at 120 (`31cd544`)

**The root cause.** `JointServo.track` corrects towards the target *this* step is sent to, not the
one it starts from. On a moving command that is a lead: the joint settles one step ahead of its
command, v x dt. Every cover, stroke and parry was tuned with that lead at 1/240 s. At 120 it
doubled, so a chain arrived early by twice as much and overshot a stop by more. Two other things
in the arm count in steps rather than seconds:

- **the gain**, where one step of `response x dt` is not two steps of half of it;
- **the arm command filter** (`stepToward` in `src/golem/effectors/chains/arm-core.ts`).

**The repair holds all three per second at `CONFIG.world.solverTuningHz`**, the rate Havok's ideal
step is already held to:

- the lead is `servoLead(dt) = min(1, (1 / solverTuningHz) / dt)` of the step's motion;
- the gain is `servoGain(k, dt) = (1 - (1 - k / 240)^(dt x 240)) / dt`, the compounded decay of the
  240 servo over the same time;
- the filter runs 240's substeps inside a longer step.

The human arm's drive takes the same lead and gain. At 240 all of it is bit-identical: Node golem
bench, all twelve modules, every field.

No motor ceiling moved. The table is in `src/golem/joint-servo.ts`'s doc comment. Node golem bench,
200 perturbed trials per cell, paired 120 - 240, with the shipped servo at 240 as the reference:

- Every stray row fell with the overshoot.
- The shipped servo's +0.83 m/s of cut peak at 120 was the early arrival itself.
- The two speed rows are one-step-difference peaks, which read low at 120 whatever the arm does.
  On the shipped cut, trial 0, the 240 physics read at 120's spacing peaks at 17.95 or 17.51 m/s
  by sampling phase, against 18.14 read at every step and 17.50 for the 120 physics. So most of
  the −0.60 is the reading (section 3).

The exact discretisation on branch `physics-rate-servo-tuning` (`(1 − e^(−k dt)) / dt` and an
exactly integrated filter) was measured beside these and not taken. It changes the arm at 240
as well, and at 120 it reads −0.79 m/s of cut peak and −1.43 of lash against 240.

## 3. The stroke instrument (`bb714bb`)

The 120 Hz "slower cut" was the probe.

- **What it read.** The speed at the mark was the published points differenced back across one
  step, taken at the step nearest the mark. That is the mean speed of the step *before* the
  sample, half a step behind it, on a blade gaining about 400 m/s every second. It is also placed
  up to half a step either side of the blade's real passage. Both errors scale with the step.
- **What it reads now.**
  1. It places the approach at the minimum of a parabola through the squared miss of the three
     steps around the nearest one.
  2. It takes a fourth-order central difference at each of the three.
  3. It interpolates between them quadratically.

Shipped cut, trial 0, Node golem bench (m/s):

|------------------------------------|--------------:|--------------------:|
| 240, every step                    |         15.45 |               15.63 |
| 240, every other step, phase 0     |         14.70 |               15.52 |
| 240, every other step, phase 1     |         13.11 |               15.19 |
| 120, every step                    |         13.44 |               15.16 |

The same 240 physics read at 120's spacing reads what 120 physics reads.

- **The chosen sword cut**, same four rows: 13.37, 12.67, 11.22 and 12.66 the old way, against
  13.47, 13.33, 13.14 and 13.12 now.
- **The bars held.** The shipped bar is 14.5 and the chosen bar is 13.

**Decision: yes, per kind.**

- **`empty` 0.62.** The fists land on the settled length (+0.007). The default mirror's plate is
  the same kind, and its length does not move (−0.031 against −0.029): its blade decides it.
- **`club` 0.62.** It is the one value measured on both club mirrors.
  - It leaves the mace 0.024 short and the maul 0.074 long, each inside its clustered interval.
  - By squared residual, 0.62 and an interpolated 0.63 are indistinguishable (0.0060 and 0.0056).
  - The maul's remainder travels with falls, +3.49 a bout, which are locomotion's to answer.
- **`whip` stays at 0.56.**
  - The whip mirror's length hardly answers the fraction: −0.022 over 0.06, a quarter of the
    others' slope. Its "crossing" is an extrapolation to 0.79.
  - Its excess travels with falls (+2.9 a bout at both fractions).
  - A fraction that bought that length back would be a rule standing in for a cause elsewhere,
    which is the `copyStabilityEvent` trap in AGENTS.md.
- **The rest keep the blade's 0.56**: sword, axe, bow, shield, buckler, arrow, bite and ram. No
  research mirror fields one except the sword, so none has a length to hold.

**The code.**

- `CONFIG.combat.arrivalReadFraction` became `arrivalReadFractions`, one row per `Striker`, with
  `satisfies Record<Striker, number>`.
- `arrivalReadFraction(kind, table)` in `src/combat.ts` is total over the union, with a `never`
  default, and throws by name on a row outside (0, 1].
- A `Combat` copies the table at construction, as it did the single number, and validates each of
  its weapons' rows there.
- The hand-fired fixtures read their own kind's row.

**The test.** `each_striker_kind_is_billed_at_its_own_arrival_fraction` in
`tests/contact-reading.test.mjs` checks:

- one row per kind and no other;
- bad rows throw;
- a club and a blade, hand-fired at different rows, are each billed at their own.

  7.840".
