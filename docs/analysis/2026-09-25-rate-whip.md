# The whip at 120 Hz: a limit, or one chaotic trial?

2026-09-25. This follows up section 6 of `2026-09-25-physics-rate-2.md`, which left the whip at
"not shown to be a limit, unresolved". There, one bench trial per rate had the whip's peak tip at
17.3 m/s at 240 Hz and 13.9 m/s at 120 Hz, and its stroke peak at 14.8 and 6.1.

**What is compared.** The baseline is main as it stands:

- **120**: `CONFIG.world.physicsHz` 120, with Havok's ideal step held at 1/240 by
  `solverTuningHz`. This is the previous analysis's t120. It uses the shipped servo, not that
  study's exact-discretisation experiment.
- **240**: physics at 240 Hz, its s240.
- 216, 200, 180, 160 and 144 fill in between where that was cheap.

The preload `.review/whip/hz.mjs` (not committed) writes `physicsHz`, `solverTuningHz` and
`controlHz` before any harness module reads them. It does not have the earlier study's
`SERVO_TUNING` knobs.

Every figure names its harness, and all of them are Node and headless. Intervals are 95 %: a t
interval on a mean, a Wilson interval on a proportion. **Paired** means the same trial index or
bout id at both rates, and the paired difference is 120 minus 240.

## Answer

- **The peak is not limited at 120.** With 400 trials per rate from varied start states, the
  stroke peak, the lash peak after the stroke, and the scripted lash's peak each come out within
  ±0.4 m/s of 240, or higher. The earlier single trials were single draws from a chaotic spread.
  Under the shipped bench timings:
  - 240's scripted-lash peak of 19.6 m/s is its own distribution's **95th percentile**;
  - 120's single 12.5 is its **11th percentile**.

  That is where "loses a quarter of its peak tip" came from.
- **What the lash physically delivers is the same at both rates.** On a free 20 kg target it lands
  just as often (87 % against 86 %). The target's final momentum is within 0.14 ± 0.24 N·s, and
  its kinetic energy within 0.08 ± 0.07 J.
- **In a fight the whip scores less at 120, and most of that is how the blow is priced.** Its
  closing speeds are unchanged; p90 is 5.3 m/s at 240 and 5.7 at 120. What falls is its scored
  blows per minute, 1.47 → 0.96 (paired −0.50 ± 0.23), and the share of its contacts that clear
  the crush floor, 0.70 % → 0.46 %.

  The main cause is in `Combat`, not in the lash:
  - Havok reports the contact point in the pose from the *start* of the solver step, and `Combat`
    prices it against the pose from the *end*.
  - The distance the striker moved in between becomes a false lever arm, and that lowers
    `effectiveMassAt`. On the 60 mm weight the false lever reads 26 mm at 240 and 50 mm at 120,
    where the true value is exactly zero.
  - So the weight is priced at 0.31 kg of effective mass at 240 and 0.15 at 120, against 0.42 at
    either rate once the pose is consistent.
  - The same mismatch costs the sword about 5 %.
- **Two small residuals are genuine, and neither is the whip's own.**
  - **Chamber crack −1.8 m/s (−6 %).** This is the fast flick while the stroke winds up.
  - **Stroke stray +16 mm.** A rigid sword through the identical stroke plan strays +17 mm more at
    120 too. It is the arm's residual. It looks like 2.8x on the whip only because the whip's
    stray at 240 is small.
- **The one real peak loss is at the fast attribute extremes.** At arm speed 1.5 the lash peak
  loses 2.3 ± 0.5 m/s (−12 %) at 120, and at size 1.25 it loses 1.7 ± 0.6 (−8 %). At x1 and at
  arm speed 0.5 it loses nothing.
- **Design-arounds.**
  - **Fewer, longer beads** (4 x 0.32 m) make the lash rate-insensitive at x1 and at size 1.25,
    and they halve the relative loss at arm speed 1.5. But at 240 they make a different weapon:
    +60 % stroke peak and 2.6x the delivered impulse. That is not a rate fix to land without the
    owner.
  - **Six beads** is worse at 120 than eight.
  - **Doubling the arm's torque ceilings** closes no rate gap.
  - **A speed limit** has nothing to limit, because the peak is not inflated at 120.
- **No code change is landed.** The pricing mismatch is diagnosed and a prototype repair was
  measured (section 7), but no repair is ready to land.

## 1. Method

**Bench trials** use the Node golem bench (`runGolemBench` in `tests/harness/golem-bench.mjs`).
Each run stands one `effector.wrist.whip` on the stand with a fresh Havok. There is one Havok
arena per worker realm, and at most six lanes run at once. The scripts are in `.review/whip/`
(not committed):

- `trial.mjs`, run by `batch.mjs` with the job lists `jobs-p1.mjs` and `jobs-d1.mjs` to
  `jobs-d6.mjs`;
- the aggregators `agg.mjs`, `hist.mjs`, `bagg.mjs`, `mu.mjs` and `detail.mjs`.

A trial index seeds a mulberry32 stream, so **the same index is the same start state at every
rate**. Three trial kinds were run:

- **stroke**:
  - The stroke bench's own `strokeSequence`, with the whip's `STROKE_SHAPES` entry, aimed at the
    mind's mark. The mark is `tacticalRanges(...).strike` ahead of the socket.
  - It adds a 0.6 s tail after the recover phase, because the lash lands after the arm's stroke
    has finished.
  - **Stroke peak** and **stroke stray** are `strokeProbe`'s `peakTipSpeedDriven` and
    `peakAnchorStrayMm`, taken from the end of the chamber to the end of the follow.
  - **Chamber crack** is the tip peak from the guard's end to the chamber's end.
  - **Lash peak** is the tip peak from the chamber's end to the end of the tail.
  - Both of those apply the startup exclusion and the 0.25 s post-contact exclusion from
    `BENCH_READOUT`.
- **lash**: the scripted `WHIP_SEQUENCE`, with phase times, pointers and rolls jittered. The
  readout's `peakTipSpeedDriven` over the run is the **scripted-lash peak**.
- **target**: the stroke trial with a free, gravity-free sphere added. It weighs 20 kg, has a
  0.3 m radius and is on the right-trunk layer. It appears 0.05 s before the stroke starts, either
  at the mark or 0.4 m nearer the socket ("near").
  - **Landed** means any whip striker touched it.
  - **Delivered** is its momentum and kinetic energy at the end of the tail.
  - **Priced** is `impactEnergyJ(effectiveMassAt(striker, point, normal), 20, closing)`, which is
    what `Combat` would charge.

Perturbation sizes:

- **broad**:
  - the guard is held 2.5-3.0 s, so the lash is settled;
  - the chamber and stroke durations vary ±15 %;
  - swings and lifts vary ±0.10-0.15 rad and rolls ±0.15;
  - the mark moves ±0.15 m ahead, ±0.25 m up and ±0.15 m to the side;
  - in the scripted lash, the rest end varies ±0.25 s, the chamber ±15 % and the lash ±20 %.
- **tiny**: 2 % of broad.
- **early**: broad perturbations on the shipped benches' timings, a 0.5 s stroke guard and a 1.40 s
  lash rest.

n = 400 per rate gives about ±0.2 m/s on a mean peak and ±0.2 m/s on a paired difference. The
controls, the attribute extremes and the tiny and early sets use n = 200.

**Bouts** use the Node bout runner: `createBout` from `tests/harness/bout-runner.mjs`, with the
research `PROTOCOL` (150 s cap, supported locomotion) and a fresh Havok per bout. They run
sequentially inside each of six worker processes.

- The named build `whip` fights `default`.
- Each of the 16 PROBE_MINDS pairs is played with the whip on either side, over 16 replicates, for
  512 bouts per rate.
- Seeds depend on the pair and replicate only, so bout *k* is paired across rates.
- The per-contact detail sets (b2 to b4) are the first 128 of those bout ids.

## 2. The single trials were a sample from a chaotic transient

The shipped benches start the whip's stroke 0.5 s after the build pose, and its scripted lash
1.40 s after it. At those points the lash is still collapsing from the straight pose it is built
in. With no perturbation at all, the 240 trial reproduces the previous analysis's s240 row
exactly: scripted-lash peak 19.6, stroke peak 14.3, stroke stray 8.1. Every value in the table is
m/s. Node golem bench, the early set, n = 200 per rate.

| rate | single trial: scripted-lash peak | its percentile in the distribution | single trial: stroke peak | its percentile | distribution stroke peak, mean ± 95 % |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 240 | 19.6 | 95th | 14.3 | 46th | 14.56 ± 0.47 |
| 180 | 14.4 | 36th | 6.0 | below every one of 200 | 14.24 ± 0.51 |
| 120 | 12.5 | 11th | 9.5 | 14th | 14.20 ± 0.50 |

In the early set the paired stroke-peak difference is −0.36 ± 0.37 m/s at 120.

**It is chaos rather than noise.** Perturbations at 2 % of the broad set's size still leave most
of the spread (Node golem bench, n = 200):

| perturbation | stroke peak s.d., 240 / 120 (m/s) | lash peak s.d. (m/s) |
|---|---:|---:|
| tiny (2 %) | 1.63 / 1.13 | 2.13 / 1.73 |
| broad | 2.07 / 2.27 | 2.41 / 2.47 |

One neighbourhood of start states does not speak for the others. In the tiny set, which is one
neighbourhood, 120 is 1.0 ± 0.3 m/s lower on the stroke peak and 1.3 ± 0.4 lower on the lash.
Averaged over the broad set it is 0.2 lower on each.

## 4. The stroke stray belongs to the arm

The same stroke plan and perturbations were run with rigid terminals in the socket. Node golem
bench, broad, n = 200 per cell, stray in mm.

| module | stroke stray 240 | stroke stray 120 | paired | lash-window stray paired | stroke peak paired (m/s) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| wrist whip | 8.89 ± 0.19 | 25.10 ± 1.36 | +16.2 ± 1.3 | +33.3 ± 0.8 | −0.29 ± 0.26 |
| wrist blade | 36.0 ± 0.6 | 53.3 ± 1.2 | +17.3 ± 1.1 | +30.6 ± 0.5 | +0.80 ± 0.07 |
| wrist mace | 131.7 ± 5.4 | 135.0 ± 6.1 | +3.3 ± 1.7 | +24.2 ± 2.5 | +0.83 ± 0.10 |

- **The whip and the blade stray the same absolute amount more at 120.** The whip's 2.8x is +16 mm
  on an 8.9 mm base.
- **At 120 the whip's stray splits into two modes.** About half the trials peak around 12-16 mm
  and half around 27-40 mm.
- **Those high trials peak at the very end of the follow phase**, 0.28 s into the window against a
  follow end of 0.28 s. That phase drops the drive's ceiling on purpose, and the passive lash's pull
  carries the hand further off its anchor.
- **A stiffer drive does not touch it.** Doubling `CHAIN_REACH`'s three torque ceilings (1440 /
  2400 / 1440 N·m) leaves the stroke stray at 8.85 → 24.89 mm, a paired +16.0 ± 0.9 (section 8).
  The stray is not ceiling-bound.

The previous analysis's exact servo discretisation brought the whip to 15.4 mm at 120. That is the
servo residual it describes, and it is not the whip's.

## 7. Why: the contact point and the pose come from different ends of the step

`Combat.resolve` hands `effectiveMassAt` two things:

- **the event's contact point**;
- **the meshes' current transforms.** Havok's `syncTransform` wrote them at the end of the step.

The weight is a sphere, so its true lever about its own centre along the normal is exactly zero.
The bench's target trial read the same contact four ways (Node golem bench, near target, n = 400
per rate, the best contact of each landed trial, medians):

| pairing | weight lever 240 / 120 (mm) | weight effective mass 240 / 120 (kg) | bead effective mass 240 / 120 (kg) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| event point, post-step pose (what `Combat` does) | 25.6 / 49.5 | 0.317 / 0.161 | 0.127 / 0.071 |
| event point, **pre-step pose** | **0.0 / 0.0** | **0.423 / 0.426** | 0.495 / 0.338 |
| event point carried by the centre of mass's step, post-step pose | 0.6 / 3.7 | 0.419 / 0.415 | 0.310 / 0.139 |
| point moved onto the line through the centre of mass | -- | 0.418 / 0.416 | 0.256 / 0.275 |

- **Havok's manifold is in the pre-step geometry.** The lever vanishes exactly there.
- **The mismatch's false lever is the distance the body moved in one step.** It scales with the
  step, so it doubles at 120.
- **On a small light body it dominates.** It is already a 25 % undercount on the weight at 240,
  and 62 % at 120. On the sword, heavy and long, it moves the effective mass about 5 %.
- **The struck part has the same mismatch.** Its effective masses are 40-200 kg, so it matters much
  less there.

**A prototype repair was measured, and it is not enough on its own.** The prototype carried the
event point by one step at each body's centre-of-mass velocity before pricing. The diff is kept at
`.review/whip/contact-carry-prototype.diff` and was reverted from `src/combat.ts`. Node bout runner,
the first 128 bout ids, per-contact detail:

| weapon, rate | shipped: weight / bead effective mass (kg) | carried: weight / bead | shipped: clears floor | carried: clears floor | shipped blows/min | carried blows/min |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| whip 240 | 0.308 / 0.102 | 0.415 / 0.210 | 0.76 % | 1.38 % | 1.61 ± 0.41 | 2.91 ± 0.53 |
| whip 120 | 0.154 / 0.043 | 0.411 / 0.118 | 0.33 % | 0.67 % | 0.63 ± 0.22 | 1.39 ± 0.28 |
| sword 240 | 1.787 | 1.890 | 10.98 % | 8.86 % | 66.9 ± 7.0 | 65.1 ± 6.6 |
| sword 120 | 1.688 | 1.763 | 10.10 % | 7.27 % | 50.2 ± 6.2 | 47.4 ± 6.6 |

What the prototype showed:

- **It makes the weight exact:** 0.415 against 0.411.
- **It leaves the beads at 120 at 56 % of 240.** A capsule struck near its end has a real lever,
  and it turns during the step, so moving the centre of mass alone does not put it back.
  Even the fully consistent pre-step pairing reads the beads lower at 120 (0.338 against 0.495).
  The bench cannot say whether that is a different mix of contacts or physics.
- **It changes the sword's price at both rates.** Its closing speeds move as well, because
  `velocityAt` is read at the moved point.
- **At bout level the whip's 120/240 ratio of scored blows is about 0.48 either way.**

A real repair would read the whole chain's pose from before the step, or put Havok's point into the
end-of-step frame body by body. Either way it changes how every weapon is priced at 240 as well as
at 120. That is a scoring decision for `src/combat.ts` and `tests/scoring.test.mjs`, with bouts on
both sides of the change, so it is left open (section 10).

## 8. Design-arounds, each measured at 240 and at 120

Node golem bench, broad, n = 400 per cell, paired within each variant.

- The base row is section 3's. Its target figures come from section 5's mark set.
- `seg4` is 4 beads x 0.32 m, `kg(1.14)` each, 2 of them striking.
- `seg6` is 6 x 0.213 m, `kg(0.76)` each, 3 striking.
- Both keep the lash's total length and mass and its striking fraction. `lashReach` was left at 1.25.
- `stiff` doubles `CHAIN_REACH`'s yaw, shoulder and elbow torque ceilings.

The target sits at the mark unless a column says "near".

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| base (8 x 0.16) | 15.36 → 15.13 (−0.23 ± 0.19) | 16.20 → 16.03 (−0.17 ± 0.28) | 29.64 → 27.87 (**−1.77 ± 0.15**) | 8.9 → 25.1 | 51 % / 52 % | 1.50 / 1.69 |
| seg4 | 24.54 → 23.82 (−0.72 ± 0.66) | 28.16 → 27.53 (−0.63 ± 0.66) | 38.18 → 38.39 (+0.21 ± 0.17) | 12.0 → 26.4 | 46 % / 56 % | 2.88 / 3.92 |
| seg6 | 19.21 → 17.25 (**−1.97 ± 0.36**) | 21.34 → 19.03 (**−2.31 ± 0.37**) | 32.34 → 31.59 (−0.75 ± 0.22) | 11.0 → 25.9 | 20 % / 31 % | 0.26 / 0.45 |
| stiff | 15.25 → 14.65 (−0.59 ± 0.19) | 15.91 → 15.33 (−0.58 ± 0.24) | 28.37 → 26.51 (**−1.86 ± 0.16**) | 8.9 → 24.9 | 68 % / 69 % | 1.99 / 2.19 |

At the near target, `seg4` lands 93 % / 94 % and delivers 9.07 ± 0.63 / 10.20 ± 0.63 N·s. The
base whip lands 87 % / 86 % and delivers 3.50 / 3.36.

- **`seg4` removes the chamber deficit, and its peak differences are inside their intervals.**
  - At 240 it is a different weapon: +9.2 m/s of stroke peak (+60 %), +12 m/s of lash peak, and
    2.6x the delivered impulse at the near target.
  - Its miss distance at the mark grows from 0.66 m to 0.89 m. With fewer joints the lash hangs
    straighter, so `lashReach`, which is measured for eight beads, would have to be re-measured
    before a mind could aim it.
  - As a rate fix it works. As a design change it is the owner's call, and it makes the whip much
    stronger at 240.
- **`seg6` is worse than the base at 120.** A stroke peak loss of −10 % is the one genuine
  rate-limited peak on the x1 body.
- **`stiff` closes no gap.** The chamber deficit and the stray are unchanged, and its peak deficits
  are slightly larger than the base's (−0.6 m/s). It does make the whip land the mark more often at
  both rates (68 % against 51 %), which is a separate finding.
- **A speed limit** was not tried. At x1 there is no peak at 120 above 240's to cap, and clamping
  would only take peak off both rates.

- **The faster the lash, the more 120 takes off it.** At arm speed 1.5 the lash peak loses 12 %;

## 10. Verdict, and what is open

**Verdict.**

- **The whip's peak is not limited at 120 on the shipped body.** Its peaks are within ±0.4 m/s of
  240 once start states are sampled, and the previous analysis's single trials were a 95th- and an
  11th-percentile draw.
- **It is limited at the fast attribute extremes**, by 8-12 % of lash peak.
- **What it physically delivers to a target is the same at both rates.**
- **In a fight it scores about a third less often at 120.** The larger part of that is `Combat`
  pricing a pre-step contact point against a post-step pose. The rest is the general 120 slowdown
  every build shows, plus bead contacts that still read lighter at 120 when the pose is consistent.
- **The whip build loses about 99 % of its bouts to default at both rates.** So none of this
  changes a result, and the whip is not what decides a fight.

**Open.**

1. **The contact-pose mismatch in `Combat`.** It needs a decision and a repair that reads the
   chain's pre-step pose, or carries Havok's point into the end-of-step frame body by body. The
   mismatch prices every light, fast striker below its own mass at 240 already: the whip's weight
   at 75 %. It is a plausible part of the previous analysis's "damage per real blow falls 15 %" at
   120. The repair changes scoring at 240, so it needs bouts on both sides of the change.
2. **Why bead contacts read lighter at 120 even with a consistent pose.** It could be a different
   mix of contacts or genuinely different geometry at impact. This set did not separate the two.
3. **The follow-phase stroke stray at 120.** It is +16-17 mm on any arm and bimodal on the whip.
   The previous analysis's exact servo discretisation is the candidate. A stiffer ceiling does not
   help.
4. **Whether the lash drags on the floor in bouts.** The bench cannot reach the floor.
5. **`seg4` as a design.** It is rate-robust and much stronger at 240, and its `lashReach` would
   need re-measuring. It is the owner's call.
