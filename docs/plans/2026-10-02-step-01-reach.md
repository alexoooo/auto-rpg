# Step 01: the reach solver's Jacobian in closed form, its passes counted, and a bed of real solves

## Goal

The longest steps measured are the hands' reach: `solveReach` (`src/core/control/kinematics.ts`)
runs to its 200 passes in 117 of a bout's 144 solves, and 42 steps in a row take 6 to 11 ms where
the median is 1.2 ([step-cost.md](../reference/step-cost.md#the-reach-solver-at-its-cap)).

This plan lands what is measured to work, and the instrument for what is not yet:

- **The Jacobian in closed form.** The solve reads it by differences of 1e-7 rad, which leaves it
  noise of 1e-7 m/rad, a thousand times what the stop asks; in closed form it is right to 1e-10.
  On the bout's own 144 solves it ends 74 of the 117 that ran to the cap, and takes all passes
  from 24583 to 11470 ([Readings](#readings)).
- **A meter.** Every solve says how many passes it took and whether it ended, motor control sums
  them, and a test holds the count. Work is counted, not timed: the count is the same on every
  machine.
- **A bed.** The bout's 144 solves, kept as a fixture, solved again by whatever solve is in the
  tree. A change of the solve is judged on the same questions, where in a bout every change is
  another bout.

**What it does not do.** The 43 solves left are of a place out of reach. Six step rules were
tried on the bed's solves and none ends them at an answer a second solve leaves alone
([step-cost.md](../reference/step-cost.md#the-reach-solvers-remedies-tried)). That is the
roadmap's, and the bed is what it will be judged on.

It needs no other plan. The design is `2026-10-02-step-00-design.md`.

## Chunks

### A. The meter and the bed

Changes no answer: the bout's digest and every fingerprint line stand.

`src/core/control/kinematics.ts`:

```ts
/** How a solve ended: the passes it took, and whether it stopped of itself (no angle moved `IK_TOLERANCE` in its last pass) or at `IK_PASSES`. */
export interface ReachEnd { passes: number; still: boolean }

export function solveReach(chain: readonly BuiltJoint[], angles: number[][], free: readonly ReachFreedom[],
  tasks: readonly ReachTask[], end?: ReachEnd): number
```

The `passes` parameter goes: nothing passes it.

`src/core/control/motor.ts`, in its `state`:

```ts
/** The hands' solves since the body was made: how many, their passes, and how many ran to the cap. */
interface ReachMeter { solves: number; passes: number; capped: number }
```

`solveAt` hands `solveReach` one `ReachEnd` made with the motor and adds it to `state.reach`.
The meter is plain data in the motor's state, so a fork carries it (`tests/core-fork.test.mjs`).
Its readers are `research/step-time.mjs`, which prints it for each side when a bout ends, and
the tests below.

`tests/fixtures/reach-solves.json`: every solve of the bout `research/bout-trace.mjs` plays (the
Warrior against the Rogue), in order, each as `{ hand, chain, angles, free, tasks }`: the hand's
segment and the chain's joints by name, the angles it started from, its freedoms (`joint`, `k`,
`min`, `max`, `preferred`) and its tasks (`point`, `target`). The solves are the questions a bout
asked: they stay real whatever the solve becomes, and the fixture is not captured again when the
solve changes.

It is captured at `8d783172` and lies outside the repository at
`research/runs/scratch/reach-solves.json` on the development host (144 solves, 146 KB): this
chunk moves it in, and `research/reach-bed.mjs`'s header says where it is from. To capture it
again: a first line in `solveReach` that pushes a copy of its
`angles`, `free` and `tasks` and the chain's names onto a global list, the bout played to its
verdict (`buildBout` of `research/bout.mjs`), the list written out, and the line taken out
again.

`research/reach-bed.mjs`: builds the fixture's body on the stand (`coreStand`, no ground, the
lower trunk pinned), finds each solve's chain by its joints' names, and solves every one with
the tree's `solveReach`. Its table:

- solves, and those at the cap; all passes; of those that end, the median and the most;
- how many are at their place (within 0.1 mm), and the worst of them;
- **solved again from its own answer, the most an angle moves**, for those that ended and for
  those that did not: an answer a second solve moves is not one;
- **the three of a step** (the fixture's solves come in threes that share a start, a step apart
  along the path): the greatest second difference of their answers, which is what the motor's
  acceleration is made of;
- microseconds a pass, the least of seven readings.

Tests (`tests/core-reach.test.mjs`):

1. **`a_solve_says_its_passes_and_whether_it_ended`**: a reachable place from the guard: `still`
   is true and `passes` is under the cap; with the cap's worth of passes refused it (a place out
   of reach from the fixture), `still` is false and `passes` is the cap.
2. **`motor_control_meters_its_hands_solves`**: the Warrior on the stand, a hand sent to a place
   for 0.4 s: `state.reach.solves` is three a step, `passes` their sum, and a forked stand's

3. **`the_bed_s_solves_at_the_cap_are_no_more_than`** 117 of its 144: the bout's own count
   ([step-cost.md](../reference/step-cost.md#the-reach-solver-at-its-cap)), so the bed asks what
   the bout asked. A change of the solve lowers the number and none raises it.

### B. The Jacobian in closed form

Another bout: the answers differ in the last digits, so this chunk comes with its tables.

A point `p` on the chain's last segment moves, for a unit of freedom `k` of joint `j`, by

    w x (p - c_j),   w = T_(j-1) (sum over i of axis_i turning[i][k])

where `c_j` is joint `j`'s centre as the chain stands, `T_(j-1)` the turn of everything before
the joint, `axis_i` the axis freedom `i`'s speed turns the child about (`motionAxesToRef`, which
leans a two-freedom joint's axes as its lock asks) and `turning[i][k]` freedom `i`'s speed for a
unit of `k`'s rate (`turningToRef`). Both are the body's own, in `src/core/build/joint-state.ts`,
and the limits read the same angles.

```ts
/**
 * How `point` moves for a unit of each of `free`, with the chain at `angles`: `out[c]` is freedom
 * c's column, m/rad, root's frame. One walk of the chain, no differences.
 */
export function reachJacobianTo(out: number[][], chain: readonly BuiltJoint[], angles: readonly (readonly number[])[],
  free: readonly ReachFreedom[], point: Vec3): number[][]
```

- `solveReach` walks the chain once a pass for the turn before each joint and each joint's
  centre, and reads its columns from them for one point or two; the second point's two rows are
  its column along the pass's two directions, as they are today.
- What the walk writes into (a quaternion and a vector a joint, the axes, the turning table) is
  made once in the module, as `pointAtToRef`'s `scratch` is, and every entry read is written
  first.
- `IK_STEP` goes, and the note above the solver's settings loses its clause.
- `reachJacobianTo` is exported for its test.

Tests:

4. **`the_jacobian_is_the_kinematics_derivative`** replaces "a Jacobian differenced by 1e-7 rad
   agrees with one by 1e-5": against central differences of 1e-5 rad, 200 seeded poses over each
   freedom's range, the Warrior's arm and leg (so a joint of one freedom, of two and of three):
   every column within 1e-8 m/rad.
5. **`a_solve_at_its_place_ends`**: the bed's solves that are within 0.1 mm of their place all
   end (`still`), in under 120 passes.
6. Test 3's count comes down to 43.
7. The reach tests that stand: a reachable place found, a path out of reach followed without a
   flip, a range short of a half turn, the straight arm at its stops, a hand goal followed at
   120 and 1920 Hz.

Mutations, each must go red: a two-freedom joint's axes taken unleaned (test 4, the leg); the
turn before the joint taken with the joint's own (test 4); `turning` left out (test 4, a joint
away from its reference); the columns read by differences again (tests 5 and 6).
