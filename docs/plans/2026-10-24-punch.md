# Punch: robust straight punches that land at human level

## Why

The owner wants punches that land strong, at a person's level, in real fights, not only on one
drill's grid. Today none does. On the Strike drill's still rung (record `2026-10-24-43b15ceb`) no
punch lands a blow at 6.8 m/s: the Puncher starts only 0.6 to 0.7 m from its target, its guard
touching the target's, and its fist meets the target from rest, half open, at 1.4 to 4.4 m/s
([on the Strike drill](../analysis/2026-10-08-punch-layer.md#on-the-strike-drill)). The owner
watched it: too close, awkward, and it does not land.

The body is not the limit; the controller is
([the punch layer](../analysis/2026-10-08-punch-layer.md)):

- searched flat-out pushes bring the fist to 7 to 12 m/s, and straight blows at full reach to 7.1
  to 7.3 m/s, at 480 Hz;
- every fast blow winds up first: for 0.25 to 0.4 s the fist draws back while the chest comes
  forward, and the arm opens only in the last 0.05 to 0.1 s;
- every controller tried decides one step at a time, takes the acceleration the arm gives soonest,
  never starts the trunk early, and stops at 4 to 4.6 m/s;
- a blow's worth is the arm behind it: a fist meeting the target with the elbow 20° short of
  straight meets about 3 kg; the searched fast blows, 35 to 50° short, meet a few hundred grams.

So a punch is learned offline as a timed whole-body motion that reacts as it runs, and plays cheap
in the browser, as the learned run does (`learned-run.ts`). A planner that looks ahead every step is
not the route: the one-step solve already costs 2.2 to 2.8 ms a body a step.

## Robustness

A punch fitted to a few drill setups fails in fights. These rules hold at every step:

- **Train on the fight's distribution.** Each trial draws its start: the guard and its sway, the
  range and bearing, the target's height, stance and motion, the body and its attributes. Fight
  moments (step 3) are part of the training ground, not only of its test.
- **Judge on what training never saw.** The trials are split by their keys' digests into a
  training set and a held-out set; a claim is read on the held-out set, on both bodies, at 120 and
  480 Hz. A punch that lands only through 120 Hz's integration error fails at 480.
- **It reacts while it runs.** The punch reads the body and the target as it goes and corrects, or
  aborts to the guard when the target leaves its reach or its support fails. A fixed timeline is a
  starting point for training, not the skill.
- **Failing safely is part of the score.** A punch that meets a moved target, is covered or is
  shoved mid-swing does not fall; falls count against it.
- **No gain by narrowing.** A punch's success is read over the whole training ground, its entry
  condition's coverage beside its landing rate: a punch that lands more by refusing more has not
  improved.
- **One skill for every body.** One punch, its settings tuned per body where needed; no controller
  per body, loadout or hand.
- **Fights last.** The final check is fights against minds it never trained with, under the
  owner's verdict, and the owner watching.

## The bar

Human level is the untrained adult's, from the sources the project holds
([human strike reference](../reference/human-strike-reference.md#1-punches-straight-jabcross-hook)),
read as the competencies read it: the mean less one SD. A punch lands when all of these hold:

| Reading | Rear hand | Lead hand |
|---|---|---|
| Fist speed over its last 0.1 m before contact | 6.8 m/s (8.0 ± 1.2, Adamec) | the non-dominant mean less one SD (6.2 m/s, Adamec; the SD read from Table 3), the owner to confirm |
| Effective mass the fist meets on its own side (`TouchWatch` masses) | 2.1 kg (2.93 ± 0.81, Adamec) | the non-dominant mean less one SD (3.27 kg, Adamec) |
| Straight: its last 0.1 m within 20° of the surface's normal | yes | yes |
| A punch: its last 0.1 m within 45° of the line from the shoulder to the fist, the elbow within 30° of straight and the chest within 30° of upright at contact | yes | yes |
| On the mark asked, first contact within 0.5 s of the request | yes | yes |
| The striker up 0.3 s after, and back in guard | yes | yes |

Impulse is reported, not gated: the contact model's impulse depends on the rate
([punch](../reference/competencies.md#punch)).

A punch is good enough when 90 % of those thrown land, read on at least 300 held-out trials: a
blow must pass every reading at once, and a person's single readings already sit at the mean less
one SD, so 95 % asks more than an untrained adult gives. In a fight the 90 % is of the blows that
meet the foe past its arms: a fist met by the guard (`guarded`) or by nothing as the foe steps off
(`missed`) is the foe's defence, reported beside it; when to throw is judged in matches (step 6).

The bar is the Warrior's, an adult man's. Another body's is that bar scaled by its mass, size and
strength, so that any humanoid's follows from its own numbers.

## Steps

### 1. The bar in the Strike drill

The drill's reader reads the bar above (speed over the last 0.1 m, straightness, the fist's
effective mass, the lead hand's own bar), and its still rung's range runs from the guards touching
to a step past full reach. The Adamec SDs are read from Table 3 into the reference.

Check: `npm run check`, `npm test`; the record; every subject's landing on the still rung is
read against the old record and the change named.

### 2. The proof: one punch from guard

A timed punch executor (`src/core/skills/`): from the guard, each channel's push (level, start,
length) as data, the stance's balance and the arm's aim beneath it, as `punch-ceiling.mjs` lays
them. Its data is searched (`research/cma.mjs`) on the Strike drill's still rung, the Warrior's rear
hand at full reach, scored by the bar, several seeds, then read at 480 Hz and played in the Arena.

The one question: does a straight, aligned blow at the bar exist from the guard, and does it look
like a punch? Yes: step 3. No: the reason (the muscles' speed, the contact model, the guard it
starts from) goes to the owner before anything more is built.

Check: the record; the reel; the owner watches.

Answered yes, on [the punch's body](../reference/punch-body.md): the arms and trunk turning 2.5
times the curves read, the bar reading a punch's form (along the arm, the elbow near straight,
the chest upright), a timed punch lands at the bar at 120 and 480 Hz, 7.0 and 7.9 m/s. It lands in
its own cell alone; at the curves read nothing does
([a timed punch on the drill](../analysis/2026-10-08-punch-layer.md#a-timed-punch-on-the-drill)).

### 3. Fight moments

A fight moment is a bout's recipe, tape and step: replayed to that step, the striker's mind hands
its body to the drill (as a mind hands back from a sub-mind, `BodyView.resumed`), and the drill
asks the punch at the foe's mark. Recorded bouts of the game's fighters give the moments their
tactics asked a strike; they are a rung of the Strike drill (`fight`), split train and held-out like
the rest.

Check: a moment replayed to its step equals the recorded bout's world digest there; the owner
watches a reel of moments.

Landed as the drill's `fight` rung (`DrillTacticsConfig.fight`, `moment`): the drill takes the
place of its mind's own `moment`th action, the bout before it the mind's own to the bit
(`tests/tactics-contract.test.mjs`); record `2026-10-24-9047625b-070af15a`.

### 4. A punch that reacts

A small policy over the timed punch (as `learned-run.ts` steers its cycle): it reads the body and
the target a few times each control period and moves the pushes' timing and levels, or aborts to
the guard. Warm-started from step 2, trained offline across the still rung and the fight moments,
the rear and lead hands, both bodies and sampled attributes. A run is priced on the pool first; a
run over an hour waits for the owner's go-ahead.

Check: the held-out landing rate, coverage and falls on both bodies at 120 and 480 Hz; the step's
cost (`tests/core-step-cost.test.mjs`); the held-out reel, the owner watching.

### 5. Moving and fighting targets

First, the striker steps in: a still target out of the punch's window is closed on by the
drill's own steps, so every gap of the still rung is a punch thrown, not only those the window
admits where the striker stands.

The Strike drill's rungs 1 to 5 as the [Drills](2026-10-24-drills.md) plan builds them (the moving
pad, the drill opponent stepping, guarding and jabbing back, punches from a walk and after being
struck) and Stand's shove mid-strike join the training ground. A punch that winds up 0.3 s aims
where the target will be.

Check: each rung's held-out reading against the bar; the owner watches each rung.

### 6. In fights

The game's fighting minds throw the punch as their strike action, under the owner's verdict
(Drills step 7). Fight moments are taken again from these bouts, since a better punch changes the
moments a fight makes, and the punch trained on them until its held-out reading holds.

Check: `npm run match` of each fighter with the punch against the same fighter without, and
against minds it never trained with: blows landed at the bar, damage dealt, falls; the owner
watches bouts.

## Done when

On at least 300 held-out trials of the still rung, the fight moments and rungs 1 to 4, at 120 and
480 Hz, the Warrior's and the Rogue's rear and lead punches (the Rogue's at the scaled bar): 90 %
of those thrown land at the bar (in fights, of those that meet the foe past its arms), none falls,
and their coverage is at least today's punch's. In matches, fighters with the punch land blows at
the bar and win more than without it. The owner has accepted the reels and a fight.
