# The rat against the Warrior

**Begun 2026-11-24 on the owner's laptop**, beside the work on the desktop (capture steps, then
modelling the foe). The owner moves the commits between the two machines.

## The goal, in the owner's words

"i want to now expand into other characters and loadouts, starting with the Rat. right now, when
fighting warrior was rat, the rat doesn't really do anything. i want to get this to the point where
the rat can reliably damage, and ideally defeat the warrior within 60 seconds. the warrior is
currently very bad at attacking down, he only has the stomp, which doesn't really work. that makes
me thing that a well tuned rat should be able to hold its own against the current version of the
warrior."

On working here while the desktop works on the rest: "i'll move the commits myself".

So the rat's game Mind (`option-tail` on the rat, empty-handed) wounds the Warrior's game Mind
(`option-truer` on the man, empty-handed) in nearly every bout, and knocks him out within 60 s in
as many as it can.

## What is known before it starts

Four bouts of the game's Minds at 60 s, rat against man, 3 and 4 m apart, each on each side
(`.tmp/rat-fight/bouts.mjs`, at `effcac59c`):

- **The rat wounded the man in none.** Its contacts with him, about 40 a bout at 4 m, were its head
  and paws on his feet, for nothing; its jaw met his shin once, for nothing.
- **The man won all four**, twice by killing the rat at 55 s. Four stomps on the rat's trunk took
  0.80 of its bar: the stomp that misses a man lying down meets the rat's back at its height.
- **The rat was down 33 to 48 s of each 60**, in its Crawl most of that time.
- **The baseline**, the tournament's `gaps` protocol at `ecf5182ef`, 48 bouts
  (`npm run evaluate -- tournament option-tail/rat/empty,empty --against option-truer/man/empty,empty --protocol gaps`):
  the man won all 48; by 60 s the rat had taken 0.022 (0.007 to 0.048) of his life and he 0.785
  (0.714 to 0.855) of the rat's; he knocked it out within 60 s in 0.500 (0.364 to 0.636).
- **The bite never comes into its window in its drill** (the Strike drill's `biting` rung, the rat
  at a standing Warrior's legs): 16 trials of 16 `outside`. Its window allows the mouth 4 cm either
  way across (`biteShift`); the rat's trot stops about 6 cm off the line and stands there.
- **No drill read the rat before this plan.** The table's bodies are the humanoids; the bite's settings are the
  reptile's, set by hand; the Option Mind's fitted value is fitted on the man only.

## Steps

1. **The readings.** The rat against the Warrior as a tournament protocol at the scorecard's 24
   gaps, each side, read for the foe wounded and knocked out within 60 s; and the rat in the table,
   a cell for each skill it has (the bite, its gaits, its righting), each drill built from what the
   skill offers.
2. **The bite lands and wounds.** By the skill recipe: a bite scored on what it takes from a
   standing man in its drill, what the rat's body can do with the bite flat out, then its settings
   searched on the rat. The reach (the bite takes a point within 15 cm of the mouth's height) and the
   press (a tooth wounds by its press held, 0.2 HP per N s past 1 N) are the first suspects. A body
   that cannot is changed (jaw, neck), with tables before and after, walking and fights among them.
3. **The rat stays up.** Its righting read in the way-up drill, and its falls traced to what
   precedes them (a kick, a tread, its own gait), the skill mended before the Mind.
4. **Its tactics**, after the desktop's modelling lands, since both touch the Option Mind and its
   fitted value: the rat's value fitted on its own bouts, and whether biting and drawing back out of
   the stomp's reach comes out of the search.
