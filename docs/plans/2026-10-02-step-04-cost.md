# Step 04: what a body costs a step, taken down function by function with every bout the same

## Goal

A body under control costs 0.58 to 0.61 ms a step, and a step is its bodies' sum: that number is
how many bodies a machine carries ([step-cost.md](../reference/step-cost.md#bodies-in-a-step)).
The solver is 0.25 to 0.28 ms of it. The rest is the body's control, written for clarity: a
matrix as an array of row arrays made for every solve, a closure for every loop.

This plan rewrites the control's dearest functions to do **the same operations in the same order
on the same numbers** in arrays made once, so every bout, run and fingerprint is the same to the
bit, and it takes them in the order of what they cost. Tried on the dearest, the ground's
wrench: 61.1 us a call to 17.3, every number the same, which is 0.09 ms a body a step
([step-cost.md](../reference/step-cost.md#control-written-into-arrays-made-once-tried)).

**What it is not.** It is not a step with no allocation (the owner: some allocation is fine; the
game should not be needlessly slow). A function is rewritten because it is dear and the rewrite
pays, and one where it does not pay stays as it reads best. The collector is about 1 % of a
step and is nobody's target.

**What it can buy.** The control is about 0.33 ms of a body's 0.59. The wrench alone gives back
0.09. The rest that the profile names as worth a rewrite is about 0.16 ms a body; if it gives
between a fifth and a half of its time, as the functions tried did or better, a body comes to
0.42 to 0.47 ms: fourteen bodies in real time become seventeen to nineteen. That is an estimate
from two functions. Under it is the solver's 0.25 to 0.28, which this plan does not touch. Each
chunk says what it measured, and the last says where a body stands.

It needs no other plan. The design is `2026-10-02-step-00-design.md`.

## The rules of a rewrite

1. **The same operations in the same order on the same numbers.** A sum that began at 0 begins
   at 0 (`0 + -0` is `+0`); a product is not regrouped; a `reduce` becomes a loop over the same
   index order; `Math.max(...list)` becomes `Math.max` folded from `-Infinity`; a row swap
   becomes a swap in a list of the rows' order. No operation is added, removed or fused.
2. **A work array carries nothing from one call to the next**: a function writes every entry it
   reads. So a work array is not state (`src/core/state.ts`), a save holds none, and a forked
   bout plays the same (`tests/arena-fork.test.mjs`, `tests/core-fork.test.mjs`).
3. **A work array is made with what uses it**, in the closure of the body's own control (the
   stance, `bodyDynamics`, `jointTracker`, a hand's reach), sized from the body's spec; where a
   size varies by call (the limbs bearing), it is made at the largest the body has. No work
   array is a module's own: two bodies share none.
4. **A chunk lands only if it pays**: the functions it rewrites take less time, beyond the
   spread of two readings. A rewrite that does not is not landed, and the record says what was
   read.
5. Code that runs once, at construction, stays as it reads best. `src/core/math/linalg.ts`
   stays for it and for whatever is not rewritten.

### B. The ground's wrench (`src/core/control/contact-wrench.ts`)

`shareGroundWrench` and `activeSet`, 16 % of a bout's time and 550 KiB a step.

- `groundWrenchWork(soles, points)`: what a share of that many patches at most works in, made by
  the stance with its limbs: `A` (6 by n), `D`, `H` (n by n), `g`, `C` (the limits by n), `x`,
  the active set's system and its right-hand side as one array of (n + limits) by
  (n + limits + 1), the rows' order, the solution, the step, the working set as indices with a
  count, the basis and a row. n is 6 a sole and 3 a point; the limits 17 a sole and 5 a point.
- `shareGroundWrench(work, patches, centre, force, moment, friction, lever, out, miss, parts)`
  fills them in place. The limits' rows are written by straight lines in the order the `limit`
  calls make them, each column's sums in the order its terms come. The elimination is
  `solveLinear`'s on the flat system with the rows' order swapped where it swaps rows.
- `bearing.ts`'s two callers pass the stance's work, and hand the bearing limbs' patches and
  parts in arrays the stance keeps, not a `map` a call.
- The scratch copy that read 17.3 us a call is on the development host at
  `research/runs/scratch/wrench-flat.ts`, with its bench (`wrench-bench.mjs`) and the bout's
  4576 calls (`wrench-calls.json`): the chunk starts from it. It sizes its work by a table of
  sizes seen; the chunk sizes it from the stance.

Tests: `tests/core-contact-wrench.test.mjs` stands, each call given a work. And:

4. **`a_work_used_for_fewer_patches_answers_as_one_made_for_them`**: a work made for two soles

## Not in this plan

- **The solver's 0.25 to 0.28 ms a body.** Rapier's step for sixteen jointed segments with
  their motors. Its settings are measured (`SOLVER`); what else it could give is not read.
- **Fewer iterations.** The active set starts every call from nothing and takes 3.5 iterations
  in the mean; from the last step's working set it would mostly take one. That is another bout,
  with a table of its own, and is the next thing to read once this plan's floor is known.
- **Control on other threads.** A body's control reads the engine and writes its motors; on
  another thread it needs the engine's state for that body sent over each step. It is what
  would multiply bodies by cores, and a design of its own.
