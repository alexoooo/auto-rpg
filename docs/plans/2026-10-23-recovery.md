# Recovery: the owner's stand-up target

## Why

The owner's target ([rising](../reference/rising.md#the-owners-target)): of 64 falls, at least 15
of 16 standing within 6 s in each body and loadout group, 61 of 64 overall, every cardinal way, a
failure-inclusive median of at most 4 s, unassisted. The game's Stand up (`step-up`,
`src/core/mind/ground/step-up.ts`) stands 52 of 64, none within 6 s, a median of 15.1 s
([step-up on the target](../reference/rising.md#step-up-on-the-owners-target)).

Its stood falls spend a median 0.7 s bracing, 0.3 settling, 4.0 rolling, 7.6 rising, 0.9
steadying and 1.8 verified: 6 s leaves about 4 for everything before the hand-back. Twice the
strength stood as often and no faster, so the time is the route's: its bearing stages wait for the
body to be still and near (`progress`), its pose stages run on a clock, and the route goes from
hands and knees through a kneel-up, a tall kneel and a half kneel before a foot bears. Searched,
learned and reference-tracking stand-ups stood single falls in 5.8 to 7.3 s and no screen
([stand-up planning](../analysis/2026-10-11-stand-up-planning.md)): this plan works the staged
route the game plays, a stage at a time, on the target's own falls.

`npm run evaluate -- rising-target` plays the 64 falls in about 30 s on 8 workers, so every step
reads the whole target. Defaults chosen here and open to the owner: a shove the body holds is
given again (`SHOVED`, `src/lab/ground-mode.ts`), and the skeleton is read beside the target, not
held to it.

## Where it starts

Step 1 has landed: the stood falls' time by part of the route, and where the others end
([where the rise stops](../reference/rising.md#where-the-rise-stops)). The roll takes 4.0 s, the
kneel-up 3.2, the lunge 2.2, the steadying and verification 2.7.

## Steps

### 2. Stages that hand on as soon as the next can take the body

A bearing stage is done once the body's centre is over the limbs it bears on and coming no faster
than the next stage can take it, not once it is still; a pose stage ends when its posture is near,
not on its clock. Each change to how a stage ends is read on the whole target against the step
before (paired: the same falls).

Check: stood falls do not drop; the median and the rise's time fall.

Read on the target: a pose stage's clock is about the time its posture takes, so ending it at its
posture leaves the fold to `tall` as it was and breaks the step through (none of the Warrior's
empty-handed falls stands); the whole rise played 1.3 times faster stands 18 of 64, and 1.6 times
none. The stages wait on balance, not on their clocks. A bearing stage at rest short of its aims
hands on for the Rogue ([at rest](../reference/rising.md#what-a-bearing-stage-is-done-by)); the
Warrior loses with it. The roll's `swing` is held until the body is on its side, so the
Warrior's first roll turns it ([the roll](../reference/rising.md#the-roll)). What time is left to
step 2 is in the bearing stages' approach, not their ends.

### 3. A shorter route

From hands and knees a foot steps through to the lunge with the hands still bearing, without the
kneel-up and the tall kneel; from the back, where the body allows, a sit-up and tuck onto the feet
without the roll. Each route is a technique (`TECHNIQUES`, `src/core/mind/ground/techniques.ts`),
read on the target alone and as one of Stand up's choices.

Check: the route stands at least as many of its falls as step-up and faster; collision-clear along
its path (no self-contact between the swinging leg and the trunk).

Read: every way from hands and knees shorter than the kneel-up is closed as the body is built.
The half kneel with the hands down puts the front thigh into the trunk; the squat and the bear
route need the shank tipped forward past the ankle's dorsiflexion stop (0.39 rad) over a foot
with no toes, and the knees do not leave the ground in play
([tried and not kept](../reference/rising.md#tried-and-not-kept),
[postures](../reference/postures.md#the-verdict-by-route)). This step waits on the owner: toes
for the bodies, or more ankle range.

### 4. The Rogue

The Rogue with the club stands 4 of 16: its arms do not raise its chest, and it gives up at
`fours` ([where the rise stops](../reference/rising.md#where-the-rise-stops)). Its stages read
for its own body, with no strength raised.

Check: each Rogue group at 15 of 16.

Read: the Rogue's rises fail most at `fours` (41 of 73 failed attempts with the club, 27 of 59
empty-handed), and those begun leaning reach it square in 3 of 36. Rolled over its left side the
Rogue with the club stands 87 of 128 falls against 71, and 9 of the target's 16
([the roll](../reference/rising.md#the-roll)); empty-handed it is unchanged. What else was tried
for the lean is in [tried and not kept](../reference/rising.md#tried-and-not-kept).

### 5. The hand-back

The steady and the verification take a median 2.7 s after the rise: the stance takes the body
from the rise's last bearing already at height and quiet.

Check: the time from the rise's end to verified standing.

### 6. The game's Stand up

What stands the target becomes `step-up` (or the technique Stand up chooses first), in the Arena
and the Crypt.

Check: the target's every gate met; the skeleton's figures beside it; the owner looks in the Lab's
Stand-up mode.

## Done when

`npm run evaluate -- rising-target` meets every gate with the game's Stand up, unassisted, and the
owner accepts the look.
