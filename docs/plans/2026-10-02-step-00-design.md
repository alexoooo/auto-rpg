# The step: no long ones, more bodies in it, and room for minds that think longer

## Why

The step is 120 Hz: senses, minds, motor control, the solver, the blows. The owner asked three
things of it: that thinking which runs less often than the step is never felt in how the game
runs; that the game carries as many bodies as it can; and that heavier ways of thinking and of
control have a place to go, by one systematic, simple scheme and not one for each screen.

What was measured ([reference/step-cost.md](../reference/step-cost.md); Node 24.19, the core
world, Rapier, 120 Hz, one quiet thread), by how much it is felt:

1. **The longest steps are a hand's reach.** `solveReach` runs to its cap in 117 of a bout's 144
   solves: 42 steps in a row of 6 to 11 ms, a third of a second, where the median is 1.2.
2. **A crypt run's planning is half of its slowest steps, and all of it is sight.** Five times a
   second the party's sight is read sample by sample: 2.4 to 4.6 ms at the 99th per cent, 9 at
   worst. The path search, which looked like the thing to move off the step, is under a
   microsecond a step.
3. **A body costs 0.59 ms a step and the step is the bodies' sum.** Fourteen are real time with
   nothing drawn. The solver is 0.25 to 0.28 of it; the rest is control written with a matrix
   made for every solve. The collector is 1 % of a step and bounds nothing.
4. **Nothing in the game thinks longer than a step today.** Tactics and skills are under 1 % of
   a bout. With sight made cheap the crypt's plan is under half a millisecond at its 99th per
   cent, and what is left over a millisecond is a body being built.

So the set is four plans that each take a measured cost out, and a design, not yet a plan, for
the thinking that will one day be longer than a step.

## Principles

1. **The game never depends on the machine.** A bout or a run is the same on every machine: work
   is bounded by counts (passes, nodes, rollouts) and never by milliseconds; a body's level is
   read from the game and never from the frame rate; there is no cap on bodies. A machine that
   cannot keep up plays the same game slower.
2. **Measure, then change what pays.** Each plan here was tried in a scratch copy first and
   carries that reading. A change for speed that does the same arithmetic is held to the bit by
   the fingerprint; one that changes an answer comes with its tables and an eye gate. A rewrite
   that does not pay does not land.
3. **A mechanism is the core's, its numbers are the fight's.** What a body runs of itself is one
   rule in the core for every fight, given the fight's distances. A fight's own cadence for its
   own planning is that fight's design, and nothing schedules it from outside.
4. **Work is counted where it can run away.** A solve says its passes and whether it ended, and
   a test holds the count. A count is the same on every machine; a time is not.
5. **Some allocation is fine.** The dear functions work in arrays made once because that is
   where they are slow, not because a step must make nothing.

## Thinking that takes longer than a step

Designed here, and planned when the first thing needs it.

**Why not now.** Nothing does. The first draft of this set built a scheduler and a pool of
workers around the crypt's path searches, and the profile then showed the searches cost
nothing: the cost was sight, and sight is made cheap where it stands with no answer changed and
nothing arriving late. A mechanism with no client has nothing to hold it honest. Its first real
clients are on the roadmap: rollouts in play, a planner, a learned mind's slow part.

**The design**, for that day:

- **One primitive: a question asked now, its answer taken at a step the asker names.** A kind of
  thought is a pure function from a question to an answer, both plain data. The asker says the
  step the answer is due; the world's step hands it over at that step, never sooner and never
  later, before any mind steps. How often to ask is the asker's business and no part of the
  mechanism.
- **The game is the same wherever a thought is thought.** An answer is a function of its
  question alone and is taken at a step the game names, so it may be thought in the step it is
  due, in a worker, or on a server, and the bout is the same to the bit.
- **What is out is state**: each thought's kind, question and due step. Answers are not; whoever
  loads a save has the questions thought again.
- **A budget is a count in the question** (nodes, rollouts, plies).
- **When an answer is late, the world waits** (the owner's choice): `World.advance` takes no
  step while a thought due at it is out. `World.step`, called directly, thinks whatever has not
  come, which is every test and research script. A thought whose worker has failed is thought in
  the step. There is no constant for patience.
- **Where it goes**: `src/core/think/`, taken in `World.step` between the sensing hooks and the
  step hooks; a runner over workers beside the page, behind one interface.

## The owner's choices

2026-10-02, in the owner's words:

1. A late answer: "world waits (your default)".
2. How many bodies: "we can design the game in a way that works within the constraints, so if we
   can only support 10 at a time, then we'll need to design around that (by only the close ones
   having control)".
3. "there is no cap -- we should be able to support as many of them as we can".
4. The dead: "after some time they should be fixed in place (e.g. when player is some distance
   from them) and eventually disappear somehow -- but I haven't really though this through yet,
   so let's keep it simple and open to future refinement". Plan 03 holds them by the rule that
   holds a waiting enemy; that they disappear is not planned.
5. One scheme: background thinking and memory "should not be handled in an ad-hoc way (e.g.
   separate logic for crypt vs others)"; and, of this set's first draft: "it's fine to add a
   general mechanism (if it helps) -- just initially you started making assumtions that only
   appleid to one very narrow sub-part of the game".
6. Allocation: "it's fine if there's asome allocation, i just want to implement this in an
   efficient way that is performance-aware -- i don't want the game to be needlessly slow and i
   want to plan for good performance".

## How it extends

| Later | Where it goes |
|---|---|
| Forty bodies in a room | They all run and the step is their sum: what carries them is what a body costs (plan 04, and below). The far ones are held by the rule (plan 03). |
| A body seen moving that does not fight | A fourth level, `carried`: the solver moves it through a played pose with no control. A member of `BodyLevel` and a row of its one switch. |
| A body of another shape | Its level is the same; its control's work is sized from its spec. |
| Another fight with bodies far off (a street, a siege) | It gives `levelsOf` its distances and sets what it answers, as the crypt does. |
| The oracle's rollouts in play | The first client of the thinking design: a kind whose question is a bout's save with the orders to try and a count of rollouts. |
| A planner, or tactics that search | A question whose answer is `Orders` or an `Intent`; the mind carries out the last one it took. |
| A learned mind | At 120 Hz it is a `Mind`, in the step, its weights and work made with the body. Too slow for a step, its forward pass is a kind of thought and the mind holds its last answer. |
| Sight that is blocked, for the core's senses | The crypt's index is the model: a table of where nothing need be read, in front of the rule that reads. |
| Archery | An arrow is a body with no muscles and no level. Finding the shot is bounded by a count; if it is longer than a step it is a thought. |
| Another solve that can run away | It says its passes and whether it ended, as the reach does, and a test holds the count. |

## Not in this set

Each is on the roadmap with its reading.

- **A reach out of reach.** 43 of the bed's 144 solves still run to the cap, 42 of them out of reach. Six
  rules of the solve's step were tried and none ends them at an answer a second solve leaves
  alone ([step-cost.md](../reference/step-cost.md#the-reach-solvers-remedies-tried)): it wants a
  solve whose answer out of reach is defined, judged on the bed (`node research/reach-bed.mjs`).
- **The solver's 0.25 to 0.28 ms a body**, and **the ground's wrench started from the last
  step's answer**: the two levers under plan 04's floor. The second is another bout.
- **Control on other threads**, and the simulation in a worker with the page drawing snapshots:
  what would multiply bodies by cores. A design of its own.
- **A body built in the step**: 2.6 to 4.3 ms as the party comes near an enemy, three or four
  times a run.
- **A limp body put to sleep by the engine**: it misbehaves at eight bodies and is not
  understood.
- **How the dead disappear**: the owner's to shape.
- **The step read on a page.** Every figure here is Node's.
