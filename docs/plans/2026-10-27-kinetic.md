# Kinetic: the general mind on the toed Warrior

## Why

Kinetic is the reference mind for long-term AI experiments: every skill in one shape, one chooser
that chains skills in real time, one legs goal any stance or walk carries out
([Body layer](2026-10-28-body-layer.md)), one registry of percepts that every consumer
reads, worth in the verdict's own unit, and one trainer that learns any skill, chooser or mind.
Standing, walking, punching from either, falling, rising, feints and combinations are compositions
of skills in that shape, never a skill or a code path of their own for a special case. The work
establishes that baseline on the Warrior with toes and generalizes it before any training run.
Skills are then trained on it in their own plans, the punch first ([Punch](2026-10-28-punch.md)),
each paired against the baseline by one command. What a trained skill learns from (its reward, its
inputs, the profile it is read by) is set by the generalization, so training first would be done
again after it.

- **One reference mind.** The options fighter (`src/core/mind/options/`) and Kinetic share the
  chooser, percepts and commitments; what sets them apart is configuration (low support), so the
  options fighter becomes a Kinetic preset (step 14).
- **The body is the toed Warrior.** Toes that bend stand a rise from hands and knees 30 of 32
  times against 0 without ([toes](../reference/toes.md)). On toes the game's step-up stands none of
  the target's 64 falls ([on the game bodies](../reference/toes.md#on-the-game-bodies)), so toes
  are a setting of the human figure that Kinetic's bodies take now and the game's take with the new
  stand-up ([Recovery](2026-10-23-recovery.md) step 3). The Rogue follows the Warrior.
- **What Kinetic is today** (`src/core/entrants/kinetic/`): the options fighter's chooser over
  `followOrders` and `strike`, closing by `approach`, the default footwork at the edge of the foe's
  reach and cover on each free arm, over a composer of its own that owns the body by four parts.
  The composer goes, and Kinetic uses the toolkit's body layer.
- **Learning is the ceiling.** The one learning environment is the punch's
  (`research/punch-learning-environment.mjs`; `run-learning-server.mjs` refuses any other kind),
  its inputs a vector written by hand (`PUNCH_FEATURES`). The last run took about 200 decisions a
  second, so 5 M decisions took about 7 hours. A trainer for any skill, and its speed, decide how
  many experiments the project can run.
- **The options fighter chooses by hand-built worth one step ahead**: a strike is priced at its
  hand's energy times its landing record whatever the range (`strike`, `value.ts`), and every
  option's worth is divided by its time plus a second (`HORIZON`), so a step back is never chosen;
  a strike runs to the guard before anything is chosen again, so no follow-up is chosen by how a
  blow landed and no wind-up is let go; footwork and the guard are not skills outside it; and
  perception is written several times over (`PERCEPTS` beside `CONDITIONS`, reflex triggers, the
  threat readings and the executors' entry conditions).
- **Its worth is in weights, not the verdict.** Joules dealt, a blow taken's cost (its energy
  squared, `cost`), a fall at 100 and orders at 45 (`WEIGHTS`) are summed, while the verdict counts
  only life. A position is worth nothing in itself, so footwork and the guard are
  priced by nothing they lead to. The foe is a flat hazard: a blow coming costs the same per second
  within its reach whatever it is doing (`exposed`, the `exposure` percept), so a counter into its
  wind-up, a strike at its recovery or keeping out while it winds up have no worth of their own.
- **The rise is a separate path**: a sub-mind (`subs`) takes the whole body from the chooser while
  it is down, priced from outside by a down model (`DownModel`).

## The design

The target is drawn in [How a fighter thinks](../diagrams/fighter-mind.html) and its types in
[The fighter's mind: types](../diagrams/fighter-mind-types.html).

### The shape of a skill

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| Name and goal | what it does, at what: `jab(head)`, `move(in the jab's window)`, `cover(head)`, `walk(velocity, facing)`, `fold` |
| Entry | where it can start, in sensed terms: `Readiness`/`Window` (`src/core/skills/action.ts`), extended with posture and motion conditions read from named percepts. One form for every skill |
| Resources | what it holds while it runs (the legs, the trunk, a hand), as the action contract's reservations hold them (`Executor.needs`): all it will need, reserved at its start; it may give one back early and never takes one more |
| Model | what it is worth from here: payoff in life ([value](#value-in-the-verdicts-unit)), duration and where it ends. Hand-built (`Terms`) until its drill measures it |
| Exit | the region where it can hand over (`Readiness | null`): a strike's stroke's end, a posture reached, a range reached; and before its point of no return, where it may be let go ([letting go](#letting-go)) |
| Profile | its model measured on its drill over a few percepts (step 9) |

- **One skill per distinct movement, never one per combination or starting state.** Jab, cross and
  hook are names, each with its own profile; a number stays where it is a goal (a target, a range,
  a velocity). A punch thrown mid-step is the punch, its entry covering that state; there is no
  walking punch.
- **An entry covers the states a skill starts from**: standing, mid-step, drifting, closing. A
  skill covers more starts by widening its entry (training or tuning it on the states footwork
  leaves), measured by its drill's land rate by starting state. A new skill is warranted only for a
  different movement, signalled when one skill's land rate splits sharply across its starts.
- **A skill does its own motion and nothing else.** Outside its entry it refuses and says why. A
  commitment takes the legs over from footwork at whatever phase they are in, and keeps the walk
  or stops it through the legs goal; it never walks to its range. Reaching a range is footwork.
- **Tactics choose skills, never motion**, from their senses alone, by entry and profile.

It is the options framework of reinforcement learning (Sutton, Precup and Singh, 1999): a skill's
entry, motion and exit are an option's initiation set, policy and termination, and its profile is
the option's measured model.

### One commitment and a default layer

Every power strike holds the legs and the trunk with its hand (`straight-punch.ts`, `strike.ts`,
`blade.ts`), as do a kick and the rise. So the intent is not a general scheduler of one skill per
resource. It is:

- **One commitment slot**: at most one skill that holds the whole body or several resources (a
  strike, a kick, a rise, a lunge), or none.
- **Under it, a default layer re-chosen each step**: a legs skill (footwork) while the legs are
  free, and cover for each free hand.

The chooser fills the commitment first, then the default layer. Between commitments, most of a
fight, footwork and the hands' guard or cover run together; during a commitment the free hand
covers. A commitment may give resources back early (the legs after the drive), and the default
layer takes them at once.

- **A resource nothing holds runs its default**, which accepts any state, moving or in contact,
  and brings it to its safe state: a hand to the guard, the trunk upright, the legs standing.
- **The default layer is chosen by the same worth** ([the chooser](#the-reference-chooser)) over
  the skills that hold only its resources: footwork's goal by the position it reaches, a cover by
  the blow it is expected to stop.
- **Facing belongs to what holds the legs**: a footwork skill's goal, or a commitment's own turn.
- **Cover is a per-step choice of the free hands**, not latched, so it reacts within a step.
- **No reflex layer.** A response with no weighing patched over the intent after the choice
  (`reflexLayer`, `REFLEXES`) is a second path of decision: a cover is the default layer's
  per-step choice, and a trigger is a cue the chooser watches, so its change is a decision.

### One path for every motion

Falling, lying, rising and fighting from the ground are skills in the shape, chosen by the one
chooser, as a strike or a step is. No sub-mind takes the body from it: Kinetic's `subs` is empty
and the down model goes. A ground technique that cannot yet share the body is a commitment that
holds every resource; what it is worth is its profile, and the postures it passes through are
exits the chooser prices ([the value of a position](#the-value-of-a-position)). Getting up, moving
away on the ground, fighting from it or waiting are then choices by worth, not a rule. A motion
that seems to need a path of its own is a skill whose entry, resources or exit are not yet
general enough, and that is what is fixed.

### Handing over: at rest or in motion

An entry covers motion as well as pose, so one skill hands the body to the next wherever its exit
lies inside the next one's entry, still or moving: the sequential composition of funnels (Burridge,
Rizzi and Koditschek, 1999).

| Handover | Where | Gains | Costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| At rest | a stable posture: hands and knees, kneeling, the guard | robust; easy to measure; any skill can start there | slow: the body brakes, then speeds up again |
| In motion | a moving state: rolling onto the side, the hips already turning | fast: momentum carries across | a narrow entry, its timing tight |

- **Split only where a describable, reachable state lies between.** A tiny, timing-critical
  handover region makes the two one skill: a punch (the hips and trunk start before the elbow), a
  kip-up.
- **Stable postures are safe states**: the places any skill falls back to.
- **A strike's exit is its stroke's end** (`motion.complete`, `STRIKE_EVENT.thrown`,
  `strike-cycle.ts`). A caller that takes over there sees `Ended` at the handover; the return is
  the hand's default. A caller that does not gets the whole strike, return included.

### Letting go

A commitment may be let go before its point of no return: a strike until its drive starts, a step
until its foot is committed to land. Until then the chooser may end it and choose again, as at an
exit; after it, the skill runs to its exit.

- **Each commitment names its point of no return**, in its own terms (a phase of its motion), and
  the chooser re-chooses there as at an exit.
- **Letting go has a cost in its model**: the time back to the guard and the opening it leaves,
  measured by its drill as the rest of its profile is.
- **A feint is a strike started and let go**: chosen where the foe's reply to the wind-up (its
  phase, a cover raised) is worth more than the blow. There is no feint skill, and a strike the
  foe reads in time is let go for the next choice.

### Percepts, cues and goals

A percept is a cheap, pure reading of the senses and the body's own state (range, closing speed,
bearing, the foe's posture and guard, the threat, each side's life, the gait's phase, what the
controllers did not carry out); a cue compares one with a limit, a number or another percept.

```ts
interface Percept<V> {          // a feature of the senses: number, boolean or a name
  readonly name: string;
  read(senses: Senses, own: BodyView, state: object): V;
  readonly state?: object;      // memory, for prediction or smoothing
}
interface Cue {                 // a percept against a limit
  readonly percept: string; readonly than: "under" | "over" | "is";
  readonly value: number | string | { readonly percept: string };   // a limit read each step
  readonly margin: number;
}
```

- **One registry is the vocabulary of what a mind perceives**, read by every consumer: the
  chooser's cues, a skill's entry, a profile's variables, a learned skill's inputs (its input
  layout names percepts) and a behaviour list's conditions. The editor's fields for `CONDITIONS`
  carry over. It is built in the body layer ([Body layer](2026-10-28-body-layer.md) step 6) with
  its first consumers; the others move to it in step 13.
- **Every percept is computed each step at first.** Computing only what is watched comes later,
  and only if the step cost shows a need.
- **A goal is a name**, never a bare number with a label: a cue whose limit is read each step
  from the bodies and the skills' entries, so "close" is a range under this body's strike window,
  computed, not 0.9 m written down. A resized body, a longer weapon or another foe moves the
  distance and no number is retuned. The names to begin with:

  | Name | Range from the focus |
  | --- | --- |
  | `close` | inside this body's strike window (the commitment's entry along the target) |
  | `at the edge` | just outside the foe's reach (`toEdge`, `src/core/entrants/kinetic/tactics.ts`) |
  | `out of reach` | past the foe's reach by a margin |
  | `guard to guard` | the range two guards meet at |

  Another skill's entry region is a name as well ("in the jab's window"). Footwork is one skill,
  `move(goal)`, that delivers the body to a named goal, a commitment's entry among them; an order
  a person or a Commander gives names a goal ("close in", "keep out of reach") as a skill does. The
  names are built with the registry ([Body layer](2026-10-28-body-layer.md) step 6); a new one is
  a cue, not code, and a new kind of footwork is a new goal, not a skill.

**The foe's phase** is one percept, its value a name: what the foe's body is doing, read from what
is sensed, never from its mind or its report, and late by the reaction time as every reading of
the foe is (a blow read is a blow 0.15 s old). It is built with the registry's first percepts
([Body layer](2026-10-28-body-layer.md) step 6), from the readings that exist already:

| Phase | Read from |
| --- | --- |
| `striking`, with its blow's time to contact | the threat's predicted crossing (`READINGS.predicted`, `predictIntercept`, `src/core/mind/threat.ts`) |
| `down`, `rising` | its spec's down rule (`BodySense.down`), and its centre rising while down |
| `clear` | none of these |

- **A name joins when a profile splits on it.** `winding up`, `recovering` and `off balance` are
  added where a skill's drill readings differ sharply between foes the present phases call one,
  each with its reading of the senses and its misreadings counted on that drill. No classifier is
  written ahead of a profile that reads it.
- **Profiles are indexed by it** beside range and closing speed: a strike into a foe striking (a
  counter) is its own row, measured where a drill's rung gives the foe that phase (rung 4, jabbing
  back).
- **What the foe deals is its phase's**: a blow coming is priced by its time to contact and range
  through the foe's strikes' profiles (this mind's own, until it has a model of that foe), not a
  flat rate per second within reach (`exposed`).
- **What follows with no case of its own**: a cover held for a blow read early, keeping out while
  it strikes, a strike at a foe rising. A counter into a wind-up, a strike at a recovery and a
  wind-up let go when the foe covers come with the names they read.

### Value in the verdict's unit

Every payoff is life, a fraction of a side's full life, the unit the verdict counts
([Drills](2026-10-24-drills.md), step 7). A skill's worth is the foe's life it is expected to take
less its own life it is expected to lose.

- **A blow is life by the rules' own arithmetic**: its expected energy on a struck part made
  damage by `blowDamage` (`src/core/rules/rulebook.ts`) over that body's hit points, never a copy
  of the rule.
- **A fall is what it costs**: the time down and what the foe is expected to deal in it, from the
  ground skills' profiles and the foe's strikes', not a weight.
- **Orders stay the one weight**, a player's choice (`OBEDIENCE`), in life a metre ordered.
  `WEIGHTS`, `cost` and `HEAVY` go.
- **Life is sensed, as a scoreboard** (the owner's decision): each body's life, a fraction of its
  full life, is in the senses, a mind's own at once and the others' late by the reaction time as
  every reading of them is; the clock is the senses' time. No mind re-derives damage from the
  blows it saw. A part's own hit points stay unsensed.
- **The lead and the clock.** From the first minute both sides drain alike, so the side ahead when
  the drain empties them wins. A side ahead gains by time and one behind must close: the value of
  a position counts the lead against the time left.

### The value of a position

`position(now)`: what the situation is worth in life, the lead the mind expects at the verdict
from here, read over few percepts (range against both reaches, closing speed, each side's posture
and phase, the lead, the time left). One interface, as a profile's. Its first implementation is a
few terms, not a model built from the profiles: the lead against the time left, and the range
against both reaches (inside the foe's reach and outside one's own costs; the reverse gains), its
weights in life set for the result. A learned one replaces it, trained on bouts by the trainer
after the design, as any training run is.

- **A skill is worth what it brings, where it leaves the body and the time it takes**: its payoff,
  plus the position at its exit less the position now, less its duration at the rate of play. So a
  step back, an approach, a cover held or staying down a moment longer are priced by where they
  end, and footwork and the guard are chosen as strikes are.
- **The rate of play** is what a second of this fight is worth to the mind in life: the exchange it
  expects, hand-built from the profiles first, then the mean over its bouts. It is how a long skill
  pays for its time, the average-reward form of a value (Sutton and Barto, 2018, section 10.3), and
  it replaces dividing worth by the time plus a second: `rate` and `HORIZON` go.

### Chance

A mind may choose by chance and stay exactly repeatable. The host gives each mind a seed
(`BodyHost`), drawn from the bout's own seed (a field of its recipe, named in a match and an
evaluation) and the body's side, so the two sides draw apart and a case replays to the bit. The
stream's word lives in the mind's `state` (`randomStream`, `src/core/math/random.ts`), so a fork
copies it. `Math.random` and every other source outside the seed are refused in the mind's folders
by the boundary test.

- **What it allows**: a mixed strategy, exploration while learning, a policy that samples its
  actions, timing varied so that a foe that learns cannot read it, a feint mixed with the blow.
- **Kinetic's chooser takes it as a setting**: choosing among the choices within a margin of the
  best, in proportion to their worth (the margin 0 by default: today's choice of the best).
- **A mind that draws is read over repeated trials**, as a mind whose decisions vary already is;
  a seed names conditions, not a choice.

### The profile

One type, grown from the ground edges' `EdgeProfile` (`assets/core/edge-profiles.json`,
`src/core/mind/ground/profiles.ts`: begun, done, seconds, where its failures end): done, seconds
and where it ends, with payoff in life and the cost of letting go added, measured by drills and
stored per skill. Few dimensions, as the edge profiles: range, closing speed and the foe's phase,
not every percept. It replaces `ActionEstimate`, the options fighter's `record()` and its down
model (`DownModel`) as each is ported. A profile is read through one interface
(`expect(now, goal)`: payoff, duration, exit), so a table from the drills is its first
implementation and a learned estimate another.

- **It names what it was measured on**: the digest of its body's spec (the toed foot included),
  the skill's version, the rate and the reaction time. A profile loaded for another is reported
  stale where it is loaded, and one command regenerates it from its drill, so a changed body is
  measured again, never searched again, and never read on another's numbers.
- **It is data a skill or an entrant ships** (`assets/core/`, `assets/entrants/<name>/`), written
  by a script that names the drill records it reads, as the edge profiles are
  (`research/edge-profiles.mjs`). It is the toolkit's, not Kinetic's: any mind's chooser reads the
  entry conditions and the profiles, never a skill's code.

### The reference chooser

```ts
// The skill and goal worth most from `now`, in life; none leaves the defaults running.
function choose(now: Situation): Choice | null {
  let top: Choice | null = null, most = 0;                    // the defaults: the position held
  for (const skill of ready(now)) for (const goal of goalsOf(skill, now)) {
    const e = profile(skill).expect(now, goal);              // payoff, duration, exit
    const worth = e.gain + position(e.exit) - position(now) - playRate(now) * e.time;
    if (worth > most) { top = { skill, goal }; most = worth; }
  }
  return top;
}
```

- **The chooser is a part role** (`chooser`, `CHOOSERS`: `valued`, `ranked`). A bandit or learned
  chooser is another part, brought by configuration.
- **It decides** when a skill ends, hands over or reaches where it may be let go, or a cue it
  watches changes; `rethink` (0.2 s) is a fallback. A chain is a choice at an exit: a hook off the
  jab is chosen at the jab's stroke's end, by how it landed.
- **The foe at an exit** is predicted at its present velocity, its phase as sensed.

What follows, with no case of its own: step back then jab (the step's exit is a better position);
step in then jab (`move` toward the jab's entry); the one-two (the jab's exit); the feint (a strike
let go); the counter (the foe's phase); keeping a lead (the position's clock); the rise (postures
are exits, the ground graph priced).

### Decisions in real time

The game plays in real time with no slowdown, so a mind decides within its step from what it
already holds: its profiles, the value of a position, a learned estimate. Looking ahead is as far
as those reach; searching ahead during a fight (two decisions or more, a rollout, a tree) is out
of scope. Searching ahead is an offline research tool ([the oracle](../reference/oracle.md)), whose
findings reach a mind as profiles and a position's value.

### Carriages and gaits

How the body stands and moves is open to experiment, from a new pose to net new movement logic.

- **A carriage is a fighting pose, as data**: the feet relative to the heading (width, stagger,
  lead side), the height under standing, the trunk's lean and the arms' rest. The square stance
  the body is built in is the first; another (orthodox, southpaw, low) is an entry, not code,
  added when a drill asks for it.
- **A gait is how the legs move the body, as an implementation**: a `LegsGoal` in, a stance goal
  or its own drive (`LegsDrive`) out. Hand-built (the stance's walk, the run; a shuffle that keeps
  a carriage's stagger when footwork asks for one), a set of settings over one of them, or
  learned: over the stance (its
  outputs the footing, the swing's timing, the centre) or over the legs' muscles (its outputs
  pushes). The balance controller under the stance is swappable the same way (`BALANCES`).
- **A gait is a skill in the shape**: its entry (the speeds and states it starts from), its exit
  (where it hands over: the run to the walk at a landing, a walk to a carriage set), and its
  profile, the envelope measured on the body (top speeds each way, turning, shoves held, falls,
  effort), which the walk's envelope already is.
- **Footwork chooses them**, by named goals and profiles: run while out of reach and far from the
  destination, walk in the carriage at the edge. The legs goal names a carriage and a gait, or
  leaves them to the legs' set.
- **A new gait is proved as any skill is**: it passes the probes, the baseline evaluation reads
  it paired with the gait it would replace, and the chooser takes it where its profile is better.
  A learned gait is a configuration of the trainer on the walk's cases.
- **Every skill across carriages and gaits**: the trainer draws a skill's starts from each carriage
  and gait the mind uses, and the drills read its land rate by starting carriage and gait, so a
  new carriage shows at once which skills it breaks.

### Reaction time

- **A rule in seconds**, in the `Rulebook`, the same for both sides, converted to steps at the
  world's rate; a match and an evaluation name it. 0.15 s, the owner's decision: the quick end of
  a person's response to a seen cue. Later a character attribute.
- **On perceiving others only**: the senses hub shows each body the others late
  (`createSenses(world, delay)`, `src/core/mind/senses.ts`); a mind's own body is not delayed, so
  balance and motor control are not punished.
- **Expected in play**: a cover that waits to see a 0.2 to 0.3 s punch catches only its end, so
  reading the foe's preparation pays. People start a response to a seen cue in about 0.15 to 0.2 s.

### One trainer for any skill

A learning environment is data, not a script: a drill rung's cases (its starts drawn and split
train and held-out by their keys' digests), the skill, its input layout (named percepts) and its
output layout (claim terms in the body's units, [Kinetic,
general](2026-10-28-body-layer.md#a-learned-skills-inputs-and-outputs-as-data)), and a reward
of the drill's own score less the asks the body did not carry out. The punch is its first
configuration; a gait (the walk's courses and shoves, its reward progress less falls and effort),
a kick, a cover, a ground edge or footwork is another file. A trained skill is read
on held-out cases at 120 and 480 Hz before any claim.

A bout is an environment as well: a match's recipes for its cases, a pool of opponents for it to
meet, each a mind's configuration as a drill rung names one
([Drills](2026-10-24-drills.md#the-drills)), and the verdict's lead at the end for its reward. So a chooser, a position's value or a
whole mind trains on the same trainer as a skill does.

- **Its speed comes first.** The last run took about 200 decisions a second, while a body under
  control costs 0.36 to 0.46 ms a world step ([step cost](../reference/step-cost.md)), so the
  world's step is not known to be where the time goes. Where it goes (the world's step, the scene
  a headless run builds, the policy's, the messages) is measured before the trainer generalizes,
  and the largest cost cut, so every later run is cheaper.
- **The route with nothing of the toolkit**: a bare entrant (`bare`, `src/core/entrants/bare/`)
  takes its body as a policy (`host.policy`: `BodyObservation` in, `BodyAction` out), its network
  the trainer's, so a mind from the senses to the muscles trains on a bout as Kinetic's chooser
  does. It keeps the testbed's most general route exercised, not only Kinetic's skills.

### What stays free

Kinetic is a reference, not a rule. The tools it is built with (the body layer, the percepts
registry, the profiles, the drills, the trainer, the baseline evaluation) take any mind, and every
part of Kinetic (its skills, chooser, value of a position, rate of play, percepts, carriages and
gaits) is a part role that another implementation replaces by configuration, or a mind drops
altogether. Only the left column binds a mind.

| Fixed: every mind meets it | Free: a mind's own |
|---|---|
| The Mind's boundary: senses (each side's life among them) and its own body in; muscle commands and granted grip requests out | everything inside: skills or none, any chooser, any learning |
| Orders in the world's terms; skill choices only as an optional extra | whether a mind uses the library, profiles or goal names |
| The drills as a common measure of a skill | how a skill moves |
| Reaction time, by the rules | how a mind predicts through it, and how often it decides |
| Real time: a decision within the step | what a mind brings to it from offline work: profiles, values, a trained policy |
| Chance only from the host's seed | whether and how a mind draws: a mixed strategy, sampled actions, exploration |

### Part roles

A mind is a tree of parts by role ([Adding a Mind](../architecture.md#adding-a-mind)). In the
design one idea has one role, so an experimenter meets few words:

| Role | What it is | The roles it takes over |
|---|---|---|
| skill | a movement in the shape | `executor`, `technique`, `sub-mind`, the `guard`'s covers |
| chooser | picks the skills and their goals | `chooser`, `tactics`, `behaviour` |
| gait | moves the body by the legs goal | `locomotion`, `move-rule` (cues over named goals) |
| carriage | a fighting pose, as data | `support` (a low carriage) |
| balance, motor, navigation | as now | |

`reflex` goes with no successor ([no reflex layer](#one-commitment-and-a-default-layer)). A port
renames as it moves: a role goes when nothing configures it, and no role is kept beside the one
that replaces it. A mind outside the toolkit names its own parts.

## Steps

The steps come in two groups, each in order, and every training run waits for both: design is
cheap beside training, so skills are trained on the completed design, each run's reward, inputs
and profile set by it. The first group lays the baseline and the testbed's rules, what every mind
meets whatever it is: the toolkit's body layer, reaction time, chance, life in the
