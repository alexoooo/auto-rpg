# A kick for the empty-handed man

**Begun 2026-11-23, by the owner's order**, relayed through the reviewing session. It came after
the fault in the movement fixes, which is now read and repaired ([the approaches](../approaches.md#minds)).
The truer own-body models played worse because their old errors stood in for the foe walking in.
With the foe carried on its velocity (`carry`), a signed walk start and walks braking for the
place, the truer models read level with the Mind before them, and are the empty-handed man's Mind
(`option-truer`): the owner's word is that correct models win ties.

## The goal, in the owner's words

Of the next skill, 2026-11-23: "Kick, but I want it to be dynamic and strong, not like today's
slow and awkward stomp". And the owner's standing word on any new capability (`AGENTS.md`): "the
first thing I want is to see it".

So the kick is a skill of its own, for the empty-handed man (the Warrior) first. It enters as an
Option any Option Mind can pick by its config. The owner sees it in the Arena before it is judged,
and then it gets the usual drill and match readings.

## What is known before it starts

- **Today's stomp is a tracked path** (`src/skills/strikes/stomp/stomp/stomp.ts`). It has a
  square-up step, a weight transfer, a chamber over 0.6 s, a stroke over 0.4 s and a foot asked at
  2 m/s at contact, and it is thrown only at a foe lying down. That is what the owner calls slow
  and awkward.
- **A blow driven by tracking a declared path carries a tenth of a swing's energy**, and a tracker
  cannot throw at a person's speed: it runs behind its course by its response time
  ([the approaches](../approaches.md#weapon-strikes),
  [the skill recipe](../recipes/skill.md#3-a-native-controller-that-succeeds-on-some-cases)).
  *Repeated.*
- **The punch's driven stroke** (`src/skills/strikes/shared/straight-drive.ts`) carries energy. Each
  channel is pushed flat out until it is near its contact pose, the hips and chest turn first, and
  a learned steer corrects the stroke while the walk goes on (`straight-punch/moving`). It is the
  first strike the owner accepted in a fight. The kick takes its mechanism and adds no tracker.
- **An open-loop blow strikes where the target stood when it committed**, and lands only within
  about 2 cm of its searched distance. A kick at a moving foe needs a steer inside the stroke, as
  the punch has. *Repeated.*
- **A step and a strike are each a skill of their own** (the owner's standing words in
  [the skill recipe](../recipes/skill.md#a-step-and-a-strike)). The kick is thrown from where the
  Mind's walks and steps leave the body. No step is built into it.

## The bars, in the owner's words

1. **Dynamic**: not slow. It is read as the time from the order to contact and the foot's speed
   there, beside the stomp's on the same drill and a person's kick as a scale (reference figures
   decide nothing). No square-up step before it.
2. **Strong**: the blow carries the leg's mass. It is read as the life it takes from the target by
   the strike reader (the mass behind the striking face), against the punch's on the same rung,
   and as **the foe's falls it causes**, falls per kick thrown, which is why the push kick was
   chosen (below).
3. **Not awkward**: it keeps its feet. Falls and the time to recover are priced in its value, and
   the owner's look in the Arena decides.
4. **The owner sees it first**: pickable in the Arena on main before any reading is reported as
   the kick's.
5. **The usual readings**: its table cells (`npm run evaluate -- table --body man --skill kick
   --fresh`), and a match of the game's Mind with the kick against the same Mind without it, on
   fresh draws. The drill and the match each read the foe's falls per kick thrown beside the life
   the kick takes.

## Steps

Each step follows [the skill recipe](../recipes/skill.md#the-steps).

1. **The reader and its score.** The Strike drill gets a rung at a standing Warrior's middle mark,
   thrown with each foot, on the punch's reader: life taken less life lost, a second of the
   action's cycle, with the target's fall after the kick read beside it. Its baseline is the stomp's figures and the punch's on the same rung. The step
   is done when the reader scores today's stomp, thrown at a standing foe, low for a physical
   reason.
2. **What the leg can do.** Push the kicking leg's channels and the hips flat out from guard,
   standing on the other foot, scored on the rung's value: the speed and energy the body can make.
   That gives a scale, not a gate.
3. **A native controller.** The kick is a driven stroke: the weight onto the standing foot, the hips
   turned, then the hip, knee and ankle pushed flat out to a contact pose through the target, and
   the foot set back down to a stance. It is searched by CMA-ES on the rung's twin
   (`research/skills/skill-search.mjs`) until it lands on some held-out cases.
4. **Into the game.** The kick is registered as an Option kind (`OPTION_KINDS`) with its record
   installed, and the owner looks in the Arena. Then its profiles are built sparring
   (`sparring-drawn`, `sparring-judge`, `strike-profiles.mjs --write`), so the Mind prices it.
5. **A learned steer**, if the readings need it: one actor correcting the driven stroke, as the
   punch's, trained from reached states.
6. **Readings**: its table cells and the match against the game's Mind. The findings go to the
   recipe and the approaches, and this plan is deleted.

## Where it stands

Step 1 is read. The Strike drill's `middle` rung (and its twin `middle-twin`) is the still rung's
trials on the Warrior at a standing Warrior's middle mark, asked with each hand and each foot. Each
strike is asked only at the limbs it is offered with. The drill reads the target's fall
(`felled`: of actions begun at a standing target, the target down by the trial's end). Value
below is the table's strike judge, life taken less life lost a second of the cycle, a fall's rise
priced 10 s (Node, core world, `rapier-coordinate`, 120 Hz, the drills' Mind):

| Blow, rung | Landed | To contact, s | Speed, m/s | Energy, J | Felled | Value, hp/s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Punch, `middle` | 32 of 32 | 0.22 | 9.4 | 90 | 0 of 32 | 1.563 (1.23 hp a blow, 0.82 s cycle) |
| Stomp, `middle` | 0 of 32 | - | - | - | - | 0 |
| Stomp, `stomping` (a Warrior lying) | 16 of 18 | 2.60 | 3.9 | 19 | - | 0.041 (0.24 hp, 6.3 s cycle) |

The stomp is never begun at a standing Warrior's middle, every trial "target is outside the leg's
height band", and so even at its widest band (`top` 1, the leg's whole reach over the sole, a
research tuning). That is a physical reason: a stroke down cannot come onto a mark above the hip.
At a body lying, where it is thrown, it takes 2.6 s to contact at 3.9 m/s, against the punch's
0.22 s at 9.4 m/s.

Steps 2 to 4 are under way together. The kick is a skill (`src/skills/strikes/kick/`), an
Option kind (`strike-kick`, offered as every strike kind is) and the man's record. It is not one
of the Warrior's own skills (`assets/characters/characters.json`), so the game's Minds fight as
they did. The owner sees it by ticking Kick in the Arena's skills, and a drill names it in its
subject's `variations`. It shifts the
weight onto the standing foot, drives the kicking leg's hip, knee and ankle at an aim through the
mark (each slowed onto its goal at the edge of its stop), and sets the foot down by the stance's
own step back to where it stood, then the stance's own standing.

The falls came from the set-down. A leg driven back to where it stood, or set down by the pose
alone, left the body on one foot until it drifted off its sole. The stance's step and standing
took the falls on the `middle` rung from 72 % of trials to 16 %.

The first record, the defaults by hand with the pelvis lowered 0.08 m, landed 2 of 32 at up to
7.2 m/s about 1.0 s from the order and fell in 5. In the game's Mind's warrior-drawn bouts it was
thrown about as often as the punch, 240 kicks in 16 bouts, the thrower down within 2 s after 14.
A search on the twin rung (`research/skills/strikes/kick/kick-drill.mjs`, 60 generations) found
the record now installed. On `middle` at 120 Hz it falls in none of 32 trials and fells the
target in 4, where the first fell in 5 and felled none. But it lands on the mark once, at 1.42 s
and 4.8 m/s: it is not yet dynamic. At 480 Hz it lands 5 and falls 4, so it leans on the game's
rate, and a change of a ten-thousandth in its settings moved one trial to a fall and took its
fells to none.

The match, with the kick among the Warrior's own skills: the game's Mind (`option-truer`)
without the kick beat the same Mind with it in 0.976 (0.951 to 0.988) of 288 warrior-drawn bouts,
by 0.79 of a bar (`npm run match -- option-truer --previous option-truer --ablate
options.strike-kick=null --protocol warrior-drawn`). The kick has no profiles yet, so the Mind
throws it without a price. That is why it is no default until a match says otherwise.

Traced, that record's foot rose into the target from below, meeting the thigh at 0.6 to 0.8 m.
The aimed drive now chambers first (`chamber`: the foot at the target's height that far out from
the hip, the knee folded) and then drives through. A search from that record with the chamber
(s7) is the record now. On `middle` at 120 Hz it lands 2 of 32, 1.22 s from the order at a median
13.7 m/s and 154 J, falls in 1 and fells the target in 1; the worse of its two rates scores 0.134
against the record before's 0.038. Half the trials are still outside its window (0.67 to 1.06 m
ahead) and most of the rest meet the target off the mark.

Its profiles are measured on the Strike drill's `sparring-feet` rung (sparring's starts with each
foot), where the game's Mind with the kick ticked lands 25 %, fells the foe 19 % and falls 17 %
of 9,599 starts. They are not in the asset: a foe is modelled by every strike measured on its
body, whatever its character holds (`foeModel`), so a man's kick profile changes every pricing
Mind (47 fights moved) while no Warrior throws it by default. Priced, the kick still loses the
match: the game's Mind with it ticked won 0.049 of 288 bouts against the same Mind without it.
It spends about 40 % of its time in a strike engagement at about 0.9 m and begins 1 to 9 kicks a
bout, so it chooses the kick far more often than it can begin it, and punches less. A profile
reads only the starts where the kick began, nearly all of sparring's drawn ones, so the price
misses the time spent getting to where it can begin. That is the next thing to read.

## Which kick

The owner, 2026-11-23, asked front or round: "i don't have a strong opinion, i'd go with the one
that's more likely to cause the opponent to fall (if you're not sure, take your best guess)". The
best guess, told to the owner, is **the front push kick at the middle**. It drives the leg's mass
straight into the torso near the foe's centre of mass and pushes through the contact rather than
snapping back, so it carries the foe's centre past its feet. A round kick to the body spends its
energy across the torso as a blow. A low round kick to the standing leg fells too, but it is a
harder controller and a harder target. The owner, 2026-11-23, on that guess: "re the front kick,
that makes sense, let's use that (when we get to it)". So the push kick is the owner's choice. If
it fells rarely, that is reported, and the low round kick to the leg is the next candidate.

## After the kick

The order, the owner's, 2026-11-24 ("Finish the kick first, then do capture steps"; on the order
below, "Ok that makes sense"): the kick's remaining steps, then capture steps, then modelling the
foe, then the game's Mind's knockouts within 60 s (80.1 % against the bar's 90 %) if they are still
short. Numbers and the testbed's rest stay parked. Each gets its own plan when begun.

### Capture steps

Steps that catch a body where its balance is going. The owner also asked of the idle footing, the
change that brings the feet back in after standing up: "I'm not sure it was done in a clean and
general way -- I hope it's not contributing to lack of balance." So it is read first: whether
falls in the mirror and warrior-drawn bouts begin during an idle placement, and how often it fires
in a fight. Then one standing rule is sought for the three places that pick where standing feet go
(a walk's end back to rest width, `settleStep`; a carriage's footing, `footingOf`; the idle
footing, `idleGoal`, with its `idleFooting` option and the arbiter's `idleSettle` flag), with
capture steps beside it.

### Modelling the foe

The owner, 2026-11-23: "i do want opponent modelling, but also from what i understand, what we
already have it pretty slow. how could we approach this in a way that's scalable?" On the order:
"after the kick is fine". The approach put to the owner:

- Predict the foe from what a Mind can see: its motion over the next second, and whether it
  strikes. Learn it from recorded bouts with the boosted-trees machinery, not from the foe's
  options, which a Mind cannot see by design (`sensedSpec` drops skills).
- First, read how well it predicts against carrying the foe on its velocity (the tree chooser's
  "Foe keeps moving", `carry`) as the baseline.
- Then use it inside the one search, as the foe's expected motion (the cost of carry) or as one
  sampled response per run (the search's budget unchanged), so the cost does not grow with the
  model's richness.
