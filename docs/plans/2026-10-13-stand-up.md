# On the ground: positions, techniques, and three sub-minds

The owner asked for this plan on 2026-10-13. A body that is down gets a new design, built beside the
two sub-minds that exist, "Rise, then steady" (`support-recovery`) and "Research rise"
(`research-rise`). It neither changes nor replaces them, and keeps nothing of theirs for
compatibility.

**The aims:**

- **Techniques, with their contracts, joined into a graph.** A technique is one controlled way from
  where a body is toward standing. It may be a whole recovery (from the front to standing) or one
  transition (from the back to the front). The positions a body passes through are named, for the
  player, the readout and the choice, but a technique that passes one does not stop there. Several
  techniques may start from the same place, and several routes lead to standing, so the player
  meaningfully chooses how a fighter gets up. The shape is the one in which characters later unlock
  techniques.
- **Three sub-minds, each with one job,** in the "When down" list the player already ranks
  (`FighterConfig.subs`):
  - **Fight on the ground** (`ground-fight`): while down with a foe in reach, keep the feet toward
    it, cover, and kick.
  - **Get away** (`get-away`): while lying with a foe near, roll away from it.
  - **Stand up** (`stand-up`): rise to the feet, and nothing else.
- **Rising works**: quickly, without fixed waits or blind retries, from however the body came to
  lie. The bar is the owner's gameplay target in `docs/reference/rising.md#dynamic-rise`:
  - a median verified recovery within 4 s;
  - at least 15 of 16 directional falls in each body and loadout group;
  - 61 of 64 within 6 s;
  - every cardinal direction passing.
- **The Lab gets an "On the ground" scenario** that exercises all three, with a research bed and a
  search behind it.

**The approach.** The physical question comes first and the machinery after it. Every past attempt
failed at one transition, from hands and knees to a loaded, flat foot under the body (below). So
the plan's first work is one route through that transition, on the body's own strength, tried on
real falls. The way techniques are written is then refined against that route and a second, really
different one. Only then come the player's choices between routes, and the other two sub-minds. If
the transition will not yield at the body's own strength, a stand-up helped in a way that still
looks like effort ships instead (part H).

**Out of scope:**

- `stand-up-acceptance-v1`, its criteria, cases and baseline: untouched (part I).
- The two existing sub-minds and the Lab's Get-up experiment: left as they are.
- The reptile's recovery.
- Character progression. Unlocking needs it, and it does not exist yet (part B).

## Where things stand

- **Rise, then steady** (`supportRecovery`, `src/core/mind/rise/support-recovery.ts`) plays the
  staged recipe (`RISE`, `src/core/mind/rise/stages.ts`; `stagedRise`, `staged.ts`).
  - It goes limp until still before every attempt and after every roll, with no cap.
  - Its pose stages advance on time, whatever pose was reached.
  - A failed bearing stage, or the trunk down again, sends it limp and back to the first stage.
  - From the back it always rolls over the right side, then settles limp and reads the lie again.
  - Stabilising for 6 s without readiness restarts the whole rise, even on a standing body.
  - The step and the lunge lead with the left foot only. Their postures carry one-sided channels
    such as `lumbar lateral flexion right`.
  - Its route kneels upright on the shins before it steps a foot up. The owner has rejected a
    sustained feet-up kneel as a target (`docs/analysis/2026-10-11-stand-up-planning.md`,
    "Rejecting the feet-up kneeling route"), and that route spends 5.3 s before its continuation
    starts, so it cannot meet a 4 s median.
- **Its results** (`docs/roadmap.md`, Rising; `docs/reference/rising.md`, Staged):
  - The Warrior rises from 11 of 16 shoves empty-handed and 9 of 14 with the club.
  - The Rogue rises from 2 of 32.
  - Passing rises take 22 to 29 s.
- **The hard transition.** `docs/analysis/2026-10-11-stand-up-planning.md` records searches,
  references, learning and feedback that all fail at the same transition: from hands and knees to
  a loaded, flat foot under the centre of mass. Its failed rises saturate the wrists and lumbar
  flexion while the centre of mass is still inside the contact hull, so a support polygon alone
  does not show that a movement is feasible.
- **The assist so far.** The `assisted-rise` of the analysis lifts and rights the body at Balance
  100 with a root force and moment: a wire, not a rise. The owner's Haste decision
  (`docs/roadmap.md`, later variants of a gait) rejects a push on the root for the same reason: it
  reads as sliding. Every character's Balance is 0 (`workshopBalance`,
  `src/core/human/attributes.ts`).
- **Locomotion's turnaround** came from four things:
  - phases ended by events, not timers;
  - honest scoring (`WENT`);
  - named gaits in a registry (`GAITS`, `src/core/skills/gaits.ts`), chosen by a rule list
    (`MoveConfig`, `src/core/mind/config.ts`; `move`, `src/core/skills/move.ts`), changed only at
    safe points;
  - CMA-ES on a seeded battery with held-out seeds (`research/locomotion-search.mjs`).
- **Sub-minds.**
  - `hosting` (`src/core/mind/sub-mind.ts`) asks its sub-minds `wants` in rank order every step,
    before any mind steps, and stops at the first that does. That one has the body at once, even
    from a lower-ranked sub-mind in the middle of what it does. The one that had it is ended, and
    the one that takes it is begun and stepped in that same step, so no step goes uncommanded.
  - `hosting` captures each sub-mind's `state` once, when it is made.
- **`view.down`.** While a sub-mind has the body, its host asks no height, so `view.down` reads
  against full standing height (`commandMind`, `src/core/body.ts`; the human's `fallen` bar is
  0.25 m).
- **Fighting a body that is down** exists only from standing: `support-fold`, `groundCombat`
  (`src/core/mind/ground-combat.ts`, `docs/reference/ground-combat.md`). Nothing fights from the
  ground.
- **Who rises today:**
  - The Lab's Script and Guard presets lie still once down (`RECIPE_FIGHTER.subs` is `[lie]`), and
    so does `GAME_FIGHTER` (`src/core/mind/config.ts`), which takes `RECIPE_FIGHTER`'s `subs` and is
    every humanoid's mind unless a fight says otherwise (`src/core/models.ts`): the Arena's default
    side and the Crypt's bodies.
  - Classic, Puncher, Scrapper and the behaviours presets use `support-recovery`.
  - In the Crypt a fall ends a humanoid's fight (`HUMANOID.fallEndsFight`,
    `src/dungeon/actors.ts`), and its body goes limp. Part I changes that.

## What it draws on

- **Controllers with contracts and a supervisor.** Faloutsos, van de Panne and Terzopoulos,
  "Composable Controllers for Physics-Based Character Animation", SIGGRAPH 2001. Each controller
  declares the region of physical state it can start from (pose, velocity and balance, not a name),
  where it delivers, and a check that detects its own failure. A supervisor chooses again from the
  state as it is. Their 2D figure had several get-ups (prone, supine, kip).
- **Backward chaining.** Burridge, Rizzi and Koditschek, IJRR 1999: a chain holds when each
  controller's outlet lies inside the next one's inlet, so a chain is built from its goal back.
  Here: the search starts from the last transition and moves its starts earlier (part G).
- **Canonical positions first.** Kanehiro et al., ICRA 2007; He et al., "HumanUP", 2025; Tao et
  al., "Learning to Get Up", SIGGRAPH 2022.
- **Keyframes with feedback, hard windows and capped waits.** Reichenberg and Laue, "Stand Up,
  NAO!", 2025, which reaches 97 to 98 % in competition. Timed keyframes, feedback and conditional
  waits together, and their caution that balancing improves a movement that works but does not
  make one work that does not.
- **Phases ended by state, not time.** Kuniyoshi et al., IJHR 2004. The timing of energy input at
  a few moments separated successes from failures; overall tracking error did not.
- **Discover, then constrain.** Tao et al. find a strategy at full strength, then lower the limits
  stage by stage. Strength at 40 to 60 % of their strongest looked natural.
- **How people get up.**
  - VanSant, Phys Ther 1988: the commonest rise (supine, sitting, squatting, standing) is used by
    only a quarter of young adults. People differ, which is what several routes give.
  - Schwickert et al., Sensors 2016: median 4.2 s for adults of 20 to 50.
- **Standing in base.** The martial artist's technical stand-up (`post-up` below): a hand and the
  opposite foot hold a tripod while the free leg swings back under the body.

## A. What the body reads while down

**Readings are taken once a step, for every sub-mind,** where `down` is read: in `commandMind`'s
`look` (`src/core/body.ts`), shown on `BodyView` as `ground`. A sub-mind reads whether its body is
down from its host's view and never by a bar of its own, and the same holds for how it lies.

- **When they are read.** Only while the body is down or a sub-mind has it, since reading contacts
  allocates (`contactsOf`, `src/core/engine/rapier.ts`). Otherwise `ground` is `null`.
- **Knowing a sub-mind has the body.** `commandMind` keeps a flag in its `state` (so a fork keeps
  it), set in `release` and cleared in `resume`.
  - `hosting` calls `look` before it hands over, so on a takeover step the flag is still clear.
  - The reading starts that step anyway, because a takeover follows `down`, which `look` has just
    read.
- **The module.** The reader is `src/core/mind/ground/reading.ts`.

**The physical facts come first, and the position is named from them.**

```ts
/** What a body that is down knows of itself this step: plain data, written in place. */
export interface GroundReading {
  /** The load each segment puts on the ground, by `BodySpec.segments` order, as a share of the body's weight. */
  readonly load: Float64Array;
  /** For each foot (`footStatesOf` order), how flat its sole lies: the cosine of its tilt from level. */
  readonly sole: Float64Array;
  /** How the pelvis lies (`lieOf`, `src/core/mind/rise/staged.ts`). */
  lie: Lie;
  /** The centre of mass over the lowest point, as a part of standing height. */
  height: number;
  /** How upright the trunk is: the cosine of `upperTrunk`'s tilt from vertical, over `lowerTrunk`. */
  upright: number;
  /** The centre of mass's velocity, m/s, world. */
  readonly velocity: Vector3;
  /** The root's angular speed, rad/s: a body can spin about a centre of mass that hardly moves. */
  spin: number;
  /** The position named from this step's facts alone. */
  raw: Position;
  /** The position once it has read the same for `SETTLE_STEPS`: what the player and the choice see. */
  position: Position;
  /** For a half-kneel, the side whose foot is planted; for a lie on a side, the side down. */
  side: Side | null;
}
```

- **Load.** A segment's load sums, over each pair of each of its contacts with a fixed collider,
  the pair's impulse over the step's time, along the ground's up, where:
  - the ground pushes it up: the pair's normal points from the body into the collider, so a floor's
    reads down (`tests/core-engine.test.mjs`), and `-normal[1]` is at least `GROUND_UP`, a cosine of
    0.7. The Arena's walls carry no load, and nor does a surface overhead, which an unsigned reading
    (`recoveryReady`'s `Math.abs`) would admit;
  - the pair's own shape (`ContactPair.mine`) is not one of a held item's shapes, filtered as
    `src/core/control/effector-feedback.ts` filters them, so a club's head on the floor is not the
    hand bearing.

  It is summed pair by pair, not from the whole `Contact`, whose impulse and normal sum every pair.
  Only fixed colliders count: a body lying on its foe's legs is not supported by them.
- **What the reading leaves out.** Where each hand and foot is relative to the centre of mass,
  how high the pelvis is and whether a limb is pinned are geometry of the body's own segments.
  A technique reads them from its own body (`OwnBody`), which it is made with (part B), so the
  reading carries only what every sub-mind needs.
- **Bears** means a load of at least `BEARS`, 0.03 of the weight. A touch that carries less is not
  support.
- **A flat foot** bears with its `sole` at least `FLAT`, 0.9, and its loaded contact points (the
  contacts' `patch`, as `src/core/control/recovery-measurement.ts` reads it) under both its heel
  half and its toe half. A level sole on its heel alone, or a foot on its edge or toes, bears but is
  not flat.
- **Raw beside settled.** Hysteresis is the label's alone: `position` changes only once `raw` has
  read the same for `SETTLE_STEPS`, 6 steps (0.05 s), and clears when reading starts. Every other
  field is this step's, so a technique sees a lost foot the step it is lost.
- **Tuning.** The thresholds are the module's own constants, tuned freely: it is a mind's module,
  not the body's.
- **The core's rules apply.** The reader's work arrays are made once, it does no trig of its own
  (`src/core/math/real.ts`, `turn.ts`), and its memory is plain data in one `state`.

**The positions,** tested in this order; the first that holds wins. "Trunk" is `lowerTrunk`,
`middleTrunk` and `upperTrunk`; "hand" is a hand or forearm. **A segment not listed for a position
must not bear**, except in the four lying positions, where anything may.

| Position | Must bear | May bear | Also |
|---|---|---|---|
| standing | both feet, flat | | height at least `STANDING`, 0.85 |
| crouch | both feet | hands | |
| half-kneel | one shank, the other side's foot, flat | that shank's foot, hands | |
| kneeling | both shanks | feet | `upright` at least 0.83 (within about 0.6 rad) |
| fours | both shanks, a hand on each side | feet | |
| sitting | `lowerTrunk` | feet, hands, thighs | `upperTrunk` over `lowerTrunk` by 0.15 m |
| back, front, left, right | a trunk segment | anything | by `lieOf` |
| between | anything else | | |

The names are for the editor, the readout and the coarse choice (part C). No technique starts on a
name alone (part B).

**`standReady`** (exported from `reading.ts`) is the hand-back's test (part D). It holds when all of
these do:

- `position` is standing and `view.down` is false;
- both feet bear flat and together carry at least 0.9 of the weight;
- the centre of mass is over their polygon: `withinSupport` (`src/core/control/support.ts`), which
  returns the nearest point of the drawn-in outline, returns the centre of mass's own point;
- the centre of mass is slower than 0.3 m/s, and `spin` is under `SPIN`, 1 rad/s.

**Tests:** `tests/ground-reading.test.mjs`, fast. Every reading is taken after the world has
stepped, since contacts are the last solver step's.

- Each body (`HUMANOID_MODELS`) is built flat on its back and on its front: guard posture, the
  root turned, its lowest point 0.02 m over the ground. After 0.25 s it reads that lie, and the
  trunk carries most of the load.
- Each is built on each side, and read after 0.1 s, before it can roll.
- The Warrior is built and held in `fours`, `half-kneel` and `squat`
  (`assets/research/posture-holds.json`, as `createPostureHoldProbe` builds them,
  `src/core/tasks/posture-hold.ts`). After 0.25 s it reads fours, half-kneel (left foot planted)
  and crouch.
- The Warrior in the half-kneel with its planted foot turned onto its edge reads `between`: the
  foot bears but is not flat.
- Each body stands in its guard under the stance for 1 s, and the reader, called directly (the
  view reads nothing while the body is up), reads standing with the loads summing to the weight
  within 0.05. It still does with the club's head resting on the floor.
- A body lying against a wall reads its lie, not `between`, and the wall carries no load.
- On the step a held foot is lifted, its load reads under `BEARS` while `position` still reads
  the old label.

## B. Techniques

A **technique** is one controlled way from where the body is toward standing, with a contract. The
contract and the registry are in `src/core/mind/ground/techniques.ts`, and each technique is in a
file of its own beside it. ("Move" is already Locomotion's rule list, so these are techniques.)

**Its size is its own.** A technique may run from the front to standing in one piece, or do one
transition, such as a roll. It may pass through several named positions on the way, which make no
stops: its phases are its own and none is a node of the graph. A movement that works because it
carries momentum and overlaps its parts (the foot coming under while the hands still push) stays
one technique. It is cut in two only when a piece is useful on its own, and both the whole and the
pieces may then be offered.

```ts
/** How a technique ended. */
export type TechniqueEnd = "done" | "failed" | "stalled" | "timed-out" | "interrupted";

/** A technique as it runs: made once, with the sub-mind; its memory is plain data in `state`. */
export interface TechniqueRun {
  readonly state: object;
  /** The phase it is in, by name, for the readout. */
  readonly phase: string;
  /** Whether it may be taken from now without harm: at a phase that says so, or between phases that do. */
  readonly yields: boolean;
  /** Whether it can start now on `side`: a test of the body's facts and its own segments, not of its position's name. */
  admits(reading: GroundReading, side: Side): boolean;
  /** Whether it has delivered to `to`: a fixed test, which no searched number moves. */
  reached(reading: GroundReading, to: Position): boolean;
  /** Start from the body as it is. `side` is a roll's side over, or the leg that leads. */
  begin(side: Side): void;
  /** Drive the body this step; null while it runs, how it ended once it has. */
  step(reading: GroundReading, senses: Senses, dt: number): TechniqueEnd | null;
  /** The body is taken from it: it ends `interrupted`. */
  end(): void;
}

export interface TechniqueSpec {
  readonly label: string;
  readonly stage: "game" | "experimental";
  /** The sides it is made for: a technique written for one leg is offered on that side alone. */
  readonly sides: readonly Side[];
  /** The named positions it starts from and delivers to, for the editor, the readout and the choice. */
  readonly edges: readonly { readonly from: Position; readonly to: Position }[];
  /** Whether a body of `spec` can make it. */
  fits(spec: BodySpec): boolean;
  make(own: OwnBody, view: BodyView, world: World): TechniqueRun;
}

export const TECHNIQUES: Readonly<Record<TechniqueName, TechniqueSpec>>;
```

- **`admits`** is an ordinary predicate over the reading and the run's own body: the side, which
  supports carry load, whether a foot is flat, how fast the body moves and spins, and from the
  body's segments where the hands and feet are against the centre of mass and how high the pelvis
  is. Two bodies both named "fours" may differ in where the hands are, how high the pelvis is and
  whether a limb is trapped: one admits a step and the other does not. Several techniques may
  admit the same body. It is the run's, not the spec's, because the run is made with the body.
- **`reached`** is the technique's validator. A technique ends `done` only when `reached` has held
  for `REACHED_SECONDS`, 0.1 s. It reads the same physical facts as `admits`, and its thresholds
  are written in the spec, never searched (part G).

**Every sub-mind makes its runs once, when it is made,** one per technique it may use.

- Its `state` holds every run's state, `state.runs`, by name, and the name of the running one.
- `hosting` captures a sub-mind's `state` once, and a run made later would not exist in a fresh
  bout that loads a save (`loadState`, `src/core/state.ts`). Either way a fork would not play the
  same.

**Phases.** A technique is phases, each with:

- **Its own end:** an event (a contact gained or lost, an angle passed, the centre of mass over a
  support, a height reached). Elapsed time never proves a phase done. A phase may blend its pose
  over a time, or settle for a capped time, but it ends on its event.
- **Its own progress, if it has one:** a measure of the phase's choosing (a roll's pelvis turn, a
  transfer's load on the planted foot, a rise's height), with the gain it must make in a window.
  A wind-up or a preparation that first moves away from the goal declares none, or one of its own.
  Short of it, the technique ends `stalled`.
- **Its own failure:** the window it must stay in (the trunk's tilt, the height over a floor, a
  support that must keep bearing). Leaving it ends the technique `failed` at once.
- **A deadline.** Past it the technique ends `timed-out`. A timeout ends an attempt; it never
  counts as reaching anything.
- **Whether it yields.** A phase says whether the body may be taken from it (a roll lying flat may;
  a body with one foot under it may not). A run that reaches a point where it yields stays yielding
  until `hosting` has asked at least once, so no yield point passes between two askings unseen
  (part E).

The machinery is shared (`phaseClock` in `techniques.ts`: the deadline, and a progress watch given
its measure, gain and window), and the rules are each phase's.

**Driving.**

- **Pose phases** ask each freedom the rate `(goal - angle) / within`, no faster than `top`, at
  activation 1, `within` 0.2 s and `top` 3 rad/s unless the phase says. A rate is not a motor's
  speed: the driver's speed is along the axis its motor drives, equal to the rate only at a joint
  of one freedom or at the reference pose (`MuscleDriver.turning`, `src/core/muscle/driver.ts`).
  So the rates are turned into speeds through `turning`, as `src/core/control/servo.ts` does,
  before they are written. The staged recipe writes rates as speeds, the confusion the analysis
  records ("Hand support, joint-rate conversion and continuous foot carry"); this lands before
  anything is searched, so no search learns to undo it. It is written as the exported
  `poseDrive` in `techniques.ts`. Goals are the posture less each freedom's bind angle, as
  `stagedRise` converts them (`DofSpec.bind`).
- **Bearing phases** use the bearing solve (`makeBearing`, `bearLimbs`, `carryRoot`,
  `src/core/control/bearing.ts`) through `riseLimbs` (`src/core/mind/rise/limbs.ts`), which reads
  only `recipe.limbs`. Its parameter narrows to `Pick<Recipe, "limbs">`, and `RiseLimbs` is
  exported (see "What it changes of existing code").
- **Speed servos.** Where the pose drive falls short, a phase may push freedoms by `MusclePush`
  speed servos at a share of strength, as SIMBICON does (`src/core/skills/simbicon.ts`).
- **A centre-of-mass reference** relative to the support, recorded from good runs, may refine a
  phase that already works. It is not a way to make one work.
- **No assist,** unless the body's own attributes give one (part H).

**Mirroring.** `mirrorPosture` and `mirrorLimbs` (`techniques.ts`):

- `mirrorPosture` swaps `.left` and `.right` and negates the one-sided channels (`lateral flexion
  right`, `rotation right`).
- `mirrorLimbs` swaps the bearing phases' limbs' segments and channels (`foot.left` to
  `foot.right`).
- They land with a test: mirrored twice is the original, and the half-kneel's mirror built on the
  Warrior has its centre of mass mirrored across the body's plane within 1 mm.
- A technique offers a side only once its mirror passes the bed.

**The techniques,** by the order they are made (part H):

| Technique | Edges | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `plant-up` | front, fours, half-kneel, crouch: standing | the hands stay loaded while one foot comes flat under the hips; the load goes onto it; hip and knee extend while the hands push off; the other foot follows. From a half-kneel or a crouch it starts where its own phases would have brought it |
| `roll` | back: front; left, right: front | onto the front, over the side that `side` names |
| `sit-up` | back: sitting | |
| `post-up` | sitting: standing | the technical stand-up |
| `roll-back` | front: back | the roll the other way |
| `flatten` | any: back, front | Stand up's own, never listed (part D) |

Stand up also turns over by `roll` and `roll-back` to reposition (part D), whether or not the
player lists them.

- **`plant-up` is the first route.** It goes through the transition every past attempt failed at,
  in one piece, without an upright kneel. It may pass through fours or a half-kneel, and stops at
  neither. Its phases come from the bed (part G); the ones above are its hypothesis.
- **`sit-up` and `post-up` are the second,** chosen because they are really different: from the
  back, not the front; a tripod of hand and foot, not both hands; the leg swinging back, not
  coming forward. That `post-up` keeps three supports through the transfer is its hypothesis to
  test: three contacts do not show clearance or a feasible muscle effort.
- **More techniques come with need.** A second roll, a faster `plant-up`, `walk-back` (the hands
  walking back to flat feet) or VanSant's sitting-to-squatting rise are new entries. A smaller
  transition is cut out of a whole technique only when it proves useful alone.

**Unlocking.** A technique's `stage` says whether the game offers it. When character progression
exists, a character's unlocked techniques are a list of names that filters what its configs may
name (`Part.fits`). Nothing reads such a list until then, so none is written.

**Tests:** `tests/ground-techniques.test.mjs`, fast:

- the phase machinery: a phase whose event comes ends; one whose progress stops ends `stalled`;
  one past its deadline ends `timed-out` and never `done`; one that leaves its window ends `failed`;
- `reached` must hold `REACHED_SECONDS` before `done`;
- the mirror's two checks, once mirroring lands.

## C. Choosing a technique

- **The graph.** Named positions are its nodes, and each `(technique, edge)` an edge, from the
  techniques' declared `edges`. It is Stand up's alone. The other two sub-minds read the same
  readings, and Get away rolls, but neither chooses by it.
- **A route** is a chain of edges from a position to standing that visits no position twice, each
  edge's technique allowed by the config and not failed out from that edge's start on every side
  (part D).
- **The choice** (`choose`, `src/core/mind/ground/graph.ts`, pure and exported) is the first
  technique in the player's order, on the first side it admits, that:
  - admits the body now (`admits`), and has not failed out on that side from the settled
    `position` (part D);
  - has an edge from that position whose end has a route to standing that does not come back
    through it.

  Sides are tried in a fixed order: the side the reading names, then left, then right. The search
  is a depth-first walk in the player's order over a graph of a dozen nodes, in work arrays made
  once. It is deterministic and uses no measurement.
- **Preference works across sizes.** A whole recovery (back to standing) beside `sit-up` then
  `post-up` does not shut out `sit-up`: what counts is the player's order of the first edge, and
  that a way on exists, not which route is shorter.
- **A route intention.** The route the choice found is kept in `state.route`. At the next choice,
  its next technique is taken while it admits the body at the position the route expected and has
  not failed out; otherwise the choice is made afresh from where the body is. So a detour (from
  the front with `plant-up` failed out: `roll-back`, then `sit-up` and `post-up`) goes on to the
  route it was taken for, and does not roll straight back.
- **Nothing to choose** is Stand up's to handle: reposition, or stuck (part D).
- **Reach,** for the editor's faults: `reachable` (`graph.ts`) says whether the config's techniques
  give a route to standing from the back and from the front, with nothing failed.
- **Measured routing waits.** The bed records each technique's ends and times for diagnosis
  (part G). A cost from those records enters the choice only once several working routes show a
  need, and then it is named a heuristic, with what a failure's time and an unmeasured technique
  count as stated.

**Tests:** `tests/ground-graph.test.mjs`, fast, on `choose` and `reachable` alone, with readings
and techniques made by hand:

- the player's order is kept among techniques that admit the body;
- a technique that does not admit the body is passed over, whatever its rank;
- with a whole back-to-standing technique beside `sit-up` and `post-up`, and `sit-up` ranked first,
  `sit-up` is chosen from the back;
- from the front with `plant-up` failed out, the detour `roll-back` is chosen when `sit-up` and
  `post-up` are listed, and nothing when they are not; at the back after the detour, `sit-up` is
  chosen, not `roll`;
- a config with no route from the back is not reachable there, and the editor's fault says so.

## D. Stand up

`src/core/mind/ground/stand-up.ts`: `standUp(own, view, world, config): SubMind`. It is a kind of
its own, `"stand-up"` (`SubMindConfig`, `SUB_MIND_PARTS`, `subMind`,
`src/core/mind/sub-minds.ts`; `CONFIG_KEYS`). It only rises.

**`wants`:** `state.active || view.down`. `active` is set at `begin` and cleared at hand-back.

**What it is doing** is one of five modes, in `state.mode`, each step:

1. **Execute.** A technique runs. Each step it is stepped. When it ends, its
   `{technique, side, from, end, seconds}` goes into `state.log`, a ring of fixed length.
   - **The step it ends** is still driven: the ended technique's last command (activations and
     speeds) is carried for that step, explicitly, as Stand up's own. Stand up does not hold the
     body at the next asking.
   - **The next step** Stand up chooses and begins from the body as it is. So every step has a
     command from the mind that owns the body, and every boundary between techniques is one that
     `hosting` sees (part E). A higher sub-mind that wants the body takes it there.

   A running technique's own windows judge its motion: a roll spins and a rise moves fast, and
   neither is a tumble.
2. **Brace,** only while no technique runs and the body tumbles: the centre of mass faster than
   `TUMBLING`, 0.5 m/s, or `spin` over `SPIN`, 1 rad/s. It is what a fall, or a technique that has
   just failed, gets. The joints go to one fixed protective posture (`BRACE`: chin in, forearms
   before the face, knees and hips bent) at moderate activation. It is not the angles the body
   happened to have, which can hold a trapped limb where it is. Bracing ends when the body is slow,
   or after `BRACE_LIMIT`, 1 s.
3. **Steady,** when `position` is standing or crouch and `standReady` does not hold: the guard
   stance holds the body (`motorControl` with Locomotion's legs, as `supportRecovery`'s stabilise
   does), with no step asked. From a crouch, `Locomotion.resume` from the stance as read raises the
   asked height over `RISING_SECONDS`, as it does after any rise; that this lifts a crouch is the
   mode's hypothesis, and `plant-up`'s edge from a crouch is the way up if it does not. It ends in
   one of the two hand-backs below, or, at `STEADY_LIMIT` on a body that is down, in a new choice.
4. **Reposition,** when `choose` finds nothing, before stuck. In order, each tried once from each
   position until the counts clear (below):
   - `flatten`, its own technique, never in the player's list. It relaxes toward the nearest lie
     the reading names, arms out to stop a roll, and is `reached` once a trunk segment bears, the
     body is slow, and no hand or forearm lies under the trunk.
   - a turn to the other lie, by `roll` or `roll-back`, its own whether or not the player lists
     them.

   It is the deliberate way out of an awkward kneel or a trapped arm, which choosing "the nearest
   lie" by name would not be.
5. **Stuck,** when `choose` finds nothing and reposition has nothing left to try. It holds `BRACE`
   loosely for `BACKOFF`, 2 s, then clears every count and starts over. It keeps trying, as a person
   does, at most once every `BACKOFF`, and the readout shows it stuck.

**Failures** are counted per `(technique, side, from)`: the settled `position` when the technique
began. A triple that has failed `RETRIES`, 2, times is failed out.

- **They clear only at a hand-back** (a new time down starts with none) **or at the end of stuck's
  backoff.** Nothing a repositioning or a partial success does clears them.
- **A repositioning that succeeds uses up its try**, and the failures it was taken for stand. So
  `plant-up` failing twice from the front, then a roll to the back and a roll back, finds
  `plant-up` still failed out from the front: the body goes on to another route or to stuck, and
  never round the same loop.
- A settled label that changes on the way clears nothing, so a `plant-up` that fails through fours
  back onto the front counts every time.
- Nothing restarts from a fixed first stage.

**Hand-back,** in one of two ways, after which `active` clears and the host resumes:

- **Ready:** `standReady` has held for 0.1 s. It asks less than `recoveryReady` because the stance
  takes the body from there (`Locomotion.resume` raises the asked height over `RISING_SECONDS`).
- **Steady's limit:** after `STEADY_LIMIT`, 2 s, in steady, a body whose `position` is standing and
  which is not down is handed back though `standReady` has not held. It is the weaker of the two,
  named apart in the log and the readout. The host's stance has the body from there; if it falls,
  `view.down` gives it back to Stand up as a new time down.

A crouch is never handed back: `view.down` would read it down against full height and take it
back the next step. The target's battery verifies quiet standing apart from either, after the
hand-back (part G).

**Holding the body.** Stand up holds the body (part E) while its running technique does not yield.
Bracing, steadying, the step a technique ends and being stuck do not hold it. Stand up wants the
body whenever it holds it (`active` is set), so holding never keeps a body for a sub-mind that does
not want it.

**What the player configures:**

```ts
/** **Stand up** (`standUp`): rise by the techniques it may use, in the player's order. */
interface StandUpConfig {
  readonly kind: "stand-up";
  /** The techniques it may use, in the player's order of preference. */
  readonly techniques: readonly TechniqueName[];
}
```

- **The editor** shows `techniques` as a ranked list of the offered ones, each switchable. That is
  where a character's unlocks will show.
- **The order** lets a player say "from the back, post up" by putting `sit-up` and `post-up` ahead
  of `roll`.
- **Faults:**
  - a technique the body does not fit;
  - a list with no route to standing from the back or from the front (`reachable`).
- **The preset** is `STAND_UP`: every offered technique, `plant-up` first.

**Its name** for the readout is `stand up: <technique>.<phase>` (repositioning's techniques
included), or `stand up: brace`, `stand up: steady`, `stand up: stuck`.

**Tests:** `tests/ground-stand-up.test.mjs`, the Warrior at Balance 0 (on the helped path, at the
help part H names):

- placed on its front, and on its back, it is handed back standing within the 30 s episode, on two
  seeds;
- placed in a half-kneel with an arm under the trunk, where no technique admits it, it flattens,
  then rises;
- placed in a crouch, it steadies and is handed back standing;
- with every technique made to fail (a fixture technique that always fails), it fails each out
  from each position, repositions, reaches stuck, and begins no technique again until the backoff
  has ended;
- with a rise that always fails and rolls that succeed, it reaches stuck: the successful rolls
  clear none of the rise's failures;
- a `plant-up` that fails back onto the front is counted on each try, though the settled position
  passed through fours;
- a body that steadies to `STEADY_LIMIT` without `standReady` is handed back by the weaker rule,
  logged as such, and the host's stance holds it; pushed over, it is Stand up's again with no
  failures carried.

It goes in `tests/slow.json` if it runs over 10 s.

## E. Get away, and Fight on the ground

Both are kinds of their own in `src/core/mind/ground/`. They read the same readings as Stand up
(part A), but neither chooses by its graph. Get away uses `roll` and `roll-back`, the same roll
Stand up turns over with, so a roll is written and searched once. Fight on the ground uses no
technique; its pivot, cover and kick are its own.

### Get away

`getAway`, kind `"get-away"`. It wants the body while it lies (back, front, left, right) and the
nearest foe is within `metres`; once it has the body, it goes on wanting it until the foe is beyond
`metres` plus the band, so it does not let go and take the body back on the same edge.

- **The foe** is `nearestFoe(senses, centre, "standing")` (`src/core/mind/targets.ts`), as Move
  reads it.
- **The dead band** is Move's `LEAVING`, 0.5 m, exported from `src/core/skills/move.ts` and reused.
- **What it does:**
  - It rolls over the side away from the foe, one roll after another, reading the foe again as each
    ends.
  - A roll from the back goes to the front and one from the front to the back, so the body travels
    sideways, about a body's width a roll.
  - It lets the body go (stops wanting it) once the foe is beyond `metres` plus the band and no
    roll holds the body.

```ts
interface GetAwayConfig {
  readonly kind: "get-away";
  /** It rolls away while the nearest foe is within this, m: 2 as it starts. */
  readonly metres: number;
}
```

### Fight on the ground

`groundFight`, kind `"ground-fight"`. It wants the body while it is on its back or sitting and the
nearest foe is within `reach`.

- **What it does:**
  - It turns the feet toward the foe: a hip pivot on the back, the legs swinging round.
  - The forearms cover the head.
  - It kicks a foot at the nearest of the foe's segments within the leg's reach, by an effector
    goal for the foot (`motorControl`'s `EffectorGoal`, `BodySpec.effectors`), chambered then
    extended.
- **Blows need no new rule.** A foot that meets a segment closing is a blow, as any two segments
  are (`energyShares`, `src/core/rules/share.ts`).

```ts
interface GroundFightConfig {
  readonly kind: "ground-fight";
  /** It fights while the nearest foe is within this, m: 1.2 as it starts. */
  readonly reach: number;
  /** Which feet kick: either, the left, the right, or none (cover only). */
  readonly kicks: "either" | "left" | "right" | "none";
}
```

When the discrete actions protocol lands (`docs/plans/2026-10-13-discrete-combat-actions.md`), its
kick becomes an action whose executor runs from these positions. Until then it is the sub-mind's
own.

### The three together

The player ranks them in "When down", e.g. Fight on the ground, then Get away, then Stand up. With
that ranking:

- a foe in reach is fought;
- a foe near but out of reach is rolled away from;
- otherwise the body rises.

**The ranking is the player's policy, with its consequence.** With Fight on the ground first, a
body whose foe stays within reach stays down and fights for as long as the foe stays. Put Stand up
higher, or Fight on the ground lower, and it rises under attack instead. Chunk 07's test shows
both.

**Yielding and finishing are separate.** `hosting` hands the body to a higher sub-mind at once,
which would cut a body off one foot. So `SubMind` gains an optional `holds?(): boolean`: while the
sub-mind that has the body holds it, no other sub-mind takes it.

- **What holds means.** True while the running technique does not yield (part B). A technique may
  yield before it ends (a roll lying flat), and the sub-mind may still drive the body after a
  technique ends (steadying); holding follows the first, not the second.
- **When it is read.** Only while a sub-mind has the body (`state.has >= 0`), and only to keep a
  higher sub-mind from taking it: the ranked asking stops at the holder. The host is never held
  back from a body that stands.
- **A holder wants the body.** Each new sub-mind wants the body while it holds it (Stand up's
  `active`, Get away's hysteresis), so a body is never held for one that has stopped wanting it.
- **Commands at a hand-over.** `hosting` asks the sub-minds before any steps, ends the one that
  had the body and begins and steps the one that takes it in the same step, so the body is driven
  every step by the one that has it. A technique's `end` changes only state, so a load in the
  middle of a hand-over plays the same.
- **Every boundary is seen.** Two rules keep `hosting` from missing one between askings:
  - a sub-mind that ends one technique carries that technique's last command through the step
    and begins the next only at the step after, holding nothing at the asking between (part D);
  - a technique that reaches a point where it yields keeps yielding until it has been asked once
    (part B).

  Both hold together with the first rule: every step has a command from the mind that owns the
  body, and every intended yield is one the ranked asking sees.
- **Existing sub-minds.** `lying`, `supportRecovery`, `researchRise` and the reptile's `recover`
  do not implement it and behave as now.
- **The test.** `hosting` changes by one condition, held by a new fast test,
  `tests/sub-mind-holds.test.mjs`, of a mock host and mock sub-minds. A lower sub-mind running a
  technique keeps the body through a phase that does not yield, loses it to a higher one that
  wants it at the first step that yields, and every step of the run is stepped by exactly one mind.
  A chain of techniques that each yield only at their end loses the body to the higher one at the
  first boundary, not after the chain.

The conditions also exclude each other by position. Get away wants only a lying body, and Fight on
the ground only a body on its back or sitting. A body on one foot is Stand up's, whatever the foe
does.

**Wiring:** each kind has its `Part` in `SUB_MIND_PARTS`, its keys in `CONFIG_KEYS`, and its
fields in the editor (`src/ui/mind-editor.ts`). Links carry them (`src/ui/mind-link.ts`).

The names avoid the acceptance era's `research/stand-up-*.mjs`.

**`research/rising-bed.mjs`**, with `research/rising-worker.mjs`: worker threads, one sequential
loop in each, as `research/core-rise-trials.mjs` and `core-rise-worker.mjs` do.

- **Flags:**

  - Shoved in 8 directions, plus Dropped.

**Three claims stay apart** in every report: that Stand up beats Rise, then steady on the bed; that
it meets the owner's target; and whether any assist helped. None stands for another.

**`research/rising-search.mjs`**: CMA-ES (`research/cma.mjs`) over one technique's settings in the
unit cube, as `research/locomotion-search.mjs` does.

- **What it searches:** each phase's posture offsets, its event thresholds, its gains and
  timings, its servo shares.
- **What it may not touch:** `reached`, the windows' outer limits, `standReady`, and the target's
  checks. The search cannot reach a better score by calling the job done sooner.
- **Its score:** first whether `reached` held at the end, then, for a technique that ends
  standing, whether the body is still standing 2 s after the hand-back; then time, with a failure
  costing the cap.
- **Its starts, backward-chained:**
  1. the technique's last waypoint, placed;
  2. earlier waypoints, placed;
  3. a predecessor's real ends (`--after`), imperfect ones included;
  4. falls.

  Each stage starts from the last stage's best. A technique's searched numbers go into its spec as
  code constants. The player never sees them.
- **Discover, then constrain,** is an experiment inside the search, not a deliverable. The muscles'
  ceilings are raised by an override passed in ("Tuning is immutable"), and stepped down by 0.95 a
  stage to the body's own. Lowering them need not keep the strategy found, so what it gives is
  ideas for the technique's phases, and the strength each phase needed (part H).
- **The budget, fixed before the search starts.** Episodes are capped at 8 s each.
  - Each backward-chained stage: 40 generations of 12 candidates on 8 starts, 3,840 episodes. Four
    stages, 15,360.
  - Discover, then constrain: the ceilings raised to x1.5, then 9 steps of 0.95 down to x1, each
    20 generations of 12 on 8 starts, 19,200 episodes.
  - About 35,000 episodes in all, for each body. When they are spent, the search ends and step 2's
    bar is read (part H).
- **What its report may claim.** A bounded search that finds no route says that, and no more. That
  the body needs more strength is claimed only where a route was found at a raised ceiling and lost
  below a level. Saturation along a route says something of that route, not that no unassisted
  route exists.

## H. Making it rise: the order of attack

1. **The readings, the Lab and the bed** (01, 02), and Rise, then steady's reference reading.
2. **One route through the hard transition, at the body's own strength** (03). `plant-up`, from
   placed starts on the Warrior, backward-chained, driven alone from the Lab's "Driven by". The
   technique's contract is written only as far as this route needs.
3. **That route on real falls** (04): the rolls, the Stand up sub-mind with all its modes and the
   choice over the techniques there are, the hand-back, the host's walk after it, and a `--target`
   reading. A fall that leaves the body sitting, crouched or tangled has a mode that drives it.
4. **A really different second route** (05): `sit-up` and `post-up`, and mirroring. The contract
   (part B) is refined against the two, and only then does the editor's ranked list land.
5. **The player's choices and the other sub-minds** (05 to 07).
6. **Measured routing,** only if several working routes show the player's order is not enough.

### Step 2's budget, and what happens if it stalls

**Step 2 has stalled** if, once the search's fixed budget is spent (part G), `plant-up` reaches
standing within 6 s, at Balance 0, from
fewer than 12 of 16 placed starts on its front and 12 of 16 on fours, on the Warrior or on the
Rogue. The starts are the hard transition's own, not a waypoint after it, and the Rogue is held to
the bar as the Warrior is: the analysis saw its saturation, and it rises from 2 of 32 today. The
work then stops for a report: what was tried, what saturated, and the strength each phase needed.
The help below follows it.

### If it stalls: help that looks like effort

The owner allows a helped stand-up if step 2 stalls, provided it still looks like a body getting
up, not one pulled up by a wire. The help is **per action**: a technique's phase declares the help
it may take, and only the phases that need it take any (the push off the hands, the load onto the
planted foot, the hip and knee extension). Rolling, bracing, steadying and standing take none.

Two forms, tried in this order:

1. **A surge, first.** For one phase, the phase's working channels exert up to a stated share above
   their own strength: the ones the analysis saw saturate (wrist flexion, lumbar flexion) and the
   planted leg's extensors. The body moves by its own joints, as a person straining does. It cannot
   float the body, slide it or right a body whose limbs do not push, and it is the only form that
   helps where the analysis saw saturation.
   - **It is an assist of a second kind, with its own attribute (the owner agreed).** A torque
     beyond a muscle's sourced strength is "a force or moment no muscle gives", so it is an assist
     by AGENTS.md's definition. That definition's ceiling is the character's Balance, a share of
     body weight with a moment on the root, which cannot bound a joint's torque. So the surge is a
     joint-torque assist with a ceiling of its own, the new attribute below, metered every step as
     `Assist` is, and AGENTS.md's assist rule is corrected to name both kinds when it lands. "No
     strength is raised for feel" holds: no muscle's sourced strength changes, and a body with none
     gets nothing.
   - **What it needs in code:** the muscle driver (`src/core/muscle/driver.ts`) takes a surge
     request per channel, as runtime data in its `state`:
     - **A request lasts one step.** Before each control step the requests are none; a phase that
       wants a surge asks again that step. An interruption, an idle body, a failure or a hand-over
       therefore cannot leave help behind.
     - **The body's own capacity stays readable.** `strength(channel, sense)` stays the sourced
       muscle's bound. What the surge grants is read apart, and a controller that wants the whole
       capacity adds the two.
     - **Granted is not delivered.** A raised ceiling does not mean the solver used it. The meter
       reports, per channel and step, the part of the measured torque (`pulled`, the motor's step
       impulse over the step) beyond the channel's own bound at the asked activation. It is what
       went past the muscle's own bound, not what the whole body would have done without help.
   - **Its size:** the strength each phase needed under discover-then-constrain, no more.
   - **Its ceiling** is a new character attribute in `AttributeSpec`, beside `balance` (as the
     owner's Haste decision plans one for muscle speed): **Grit**, the owner's name for it. It is
     the most a surge may add, a per cent of each
     channel's own strength, 0 for every character, with an `owner-grit` source. The Lab and the
     Arena take `&grit=` beside `&balance=`, and every reading on a body with a surge names its
     Grit, as one with an assist names its Balance.
2. **A steadying push, only where a surge cannot do it** (a failure of balance rather than
   strength). It is the body's assist (`Assist`, `src/core/control/assist.ts`), under its Balance as
   always, asked only by the phases that declare it, and shaped so it can only add to work the body
   is visibly doing:
   - **Only on a support:** asked only while the phase's planted foot bears flat and carries at
     least 0.3 of the weight. With nothing under the body it gives nothing, so it cannot lift a
     body off the floor.
   - **A fixed share, not a replacement:** its upward part is at most `SHARE`, a quarter of the
     body's weight, a cap that does not shrink as the push unloads the feet.
   - **Only while the legs extend:** zero on any step where the planted leg's hip and knee are not
     extending, so the body never rises without its legs straightening. The push is asked only in
     the leg's phases; a surge in a hand push answers to that phase's own evidence (below).
   - **Toward the foot:** up, with a horizontal part only toward the planted foot from the centre
     of mass's ground point, carrying the body over the foot as the transfer does, never away
     from it.
   - **A force about the body's centre of mass:** `Assist` gives its force at the root's centre of
     mass, which is not the body's, so the push would also turn the body. Its moment about the
     body's centre of mass is asked back as the assist's moment, within the moment's ceiling, and
     any of it the ceiling leaves is metered and read against the phase's trunk window.
   - **Smooth only while it is allowed:** it ramps in and out over 0.2 s while the support and the
     extension that allow it hold. When either is lost, it is zero that step, without a ramp. It is
     zero for the quiet standing the target verifies.

**The gates for either form,** read on the bed and reported per run:

- the push's share of the helped phases' upward impulse (its upward impulse over its own and the
  ground's on the body together): under a quarter;
- the surge's peak factor on each channel, and how long it was on;
- effort seen where help is given, by each helped phase's own evidence: a rise's planted hip and
  knee extending; a push off the hands, its elbows extending or the hands' load rising. No step
  with help on may raise the centre of mass faster than 0.1 m/s without its phase's evidence;
- the owner's eye: in the Lab and in Arena bouts, it looks like effort, not a wire.

**What it changes, and the owner's call at that point.** Every character has a Grit of 0 and a
Balance of 0, so the helped stand-up helps nobody until a character is given one. The report gives
the smallest Grit, or Balance, at which the bed's falls rise. A Balance above 0 also strengthens
every other assisted controller, the stance's included, so the bout plays differently in every
part; Grit touches only the phases that declare a surge. Setting either for the game's characters
is the owner's decision, taken with that report.

**What the helped path is held to.** From 04 on, every test and reading that part D and part G set
at Balance 0 runs at the help the owner set, named in it. The helped route's bar is the owner's
target's gates at that named help, with the gates above. The unassisted aim stays open: the
`--target` reading at Balance 0 and Grit 0 is still taken, and reported as not met while it is
not. A helped reading never counts toward the unassisted goal or the frozen screen.

## I. Into the game

- **Selectable from the start.** Each new kind can be chosen in any fighter's "When down" list
  (the mind editor, in the Arena and the Lab) from the chunk that builds it: Stand up from 04, Get
  away from 06, Fight on the ground from 07. Until it becomes a default it is offered as
  experimental (`Part.stage`), which the editor shows and lets the player choose.
- **Each becomes a default on its own evidence.**
  - **Stand up** once the bed shows it standing more often and faster than Rise, then steady on
    the same runs: in `config.ts`, the ready-made fighters that rise with Rise, then steady
    (`CLASSIC`, `PUNCHER`, `SCRAPPER`, `BEHAVIOURS`: the editor's Classic fighter, Puncher, Scrapper
    and Behaviours) take Stand up in its place. `GAME_FIGHTER`, the mind every humanoid has unless a
    fight says otherwise (the Arena's default side and the Crypt's bodies, `src/core/models.ts`),
    takes `subs` of its own, `[STAND_UP]`, in place of the `[lie]` it takes from `RECIPE_FIGHTER`
    today. The kind's stage becomes `game`.
  - **Get away** and **Fight on the ground** join those presets only once their own test and eye
    gate have passed, and the ranking's consequence has been seen in the Lab and in bouts.
  - Rise, then steady stays selectable.
- **Its record.** `docs/reference/rising.md#dynamic-rise`'s "Presets retain their existing recovery"
  is corrected when Stand up becomes a default, and the owner's 4 s target stays the bar for rising
  well, read by `--target`.
- **Unchanged:** `RECIPE_FIGHTER` and the Lab presets made from it, the Fighter part's defaults
  (`src/core/mind/controllers.ts`), the support-entry task's start mind
  (`src/core/tasks/support-entry.ts`) and the rising trials (`research/core-rise-trials.mjs`).

  - The Arena's "After a fall" windows of 15 and 30 s (`src/arena/main.ts`) become ones a rise can

  - `tests/smoke.test.mjs` still requires a bout that wounds both sides and plays the same twice;

  - `HUMANOID.fallEndsFight` (`src/dungeon/actors.ts`) becomes false, so a downed humanoid stays

## The owner's choices

**Decided:**

- The design is built beside "Rise, then steady" and "Research rise". It keeps nothing of theirs
  for compatibility, and the Get-up experiment stays as it is.
- Fighting on the ground and getting away are sub-minds of their own, so Stand up only rises.
- Several techniques and several routes to standing, so the player configures, and later unlocks,
  how a fighter gets up. Whole recoveries and smaller transitions may both be techniques.
- Each new sub-mind is selectable as soon as it is built, and becomes a default on its own evidence
  (part I).
- A fall does not end a fight in the Crypt (part I).
- If the hard transition stalls, a helped stand-up may ship, provided it looks like effort and not
  a wire (part H).
- Its help is a new character attribute, Grit: a joint-torque assist of its own, not Balance
  (part H).

**At that point:** Grit's range, and each character's value.
