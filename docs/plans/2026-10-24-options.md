# Options: every skill in one shape

## Why

The actions already have most of the shape; the legs, the guard and the rise do not:

- locomotion takes a velocity and a facing, so spacing is written as a velocity, and no footwork
  skill has a name, an entry condition or a profile;
- a hand's guard is a cover the tactics place each step, not a skill;
- the rise drives the muscles through its own motor control (`step-up`,
  `src/core/mind/ground/step-up.ts`), beside the skill set: a second control stack;
- an entry condition covers position, facing and the body's own speed, not how the target moves
  or how the body is supported, and no skill says where it hands over.

Every skill takes one shape, so a drill, a profile and a tactic read each one the same way. The
[Drills](2026-10-24-drills.md) plan measures skills in this shape, and its profiles are what
tactics choose by. The target is drawn in [How a fighter thinks](../diagrams/fighter-mind.html), and
its types in [The fighter’s mind: types](../diagrams/fighter-mind-types.html).

## The shape of a skill

Every skill, of the legs, a hand or the trunk, has the same four parts:

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| Name and goal | what it does, at what: `jab(head)`, `keep-range(foe, 0.8 m)`, `cover(head)`, `walk(velocity, facing)`, `fold` |
| Entry condition | where it can start, in sensed terms: pose class, a few motion features (centre-of-mass velocity, spin, which parts bear), range, bearing, target speed; a predicate its module exports. A limit on a motion feature is set from the profile, once a drill has measured it |
| Resources | what it holds while it runs (the legs, a hand, the trunk), as the action contract's reservations hold them: all it will need, reserved at its start, so it is never refused halfway; it may give one back early and never takes one more (a one-two reserves the second hand before the first drive and gives the first back as it returns, `combinationExecutor`) |
| Profile | how it does over its entry condition, measured on its drill ([Drills](2026-10-24-drills.md)) under the motion features its entry condition names: a jab thrown walking is measured walking |

- **A skill does its own motion and nothing else.** Outside its entry condition it refuses and says
  why. It may shift its weight and place its feet as part of its motion; it never walks to its
  range. Reaching a range is a footwork skill.
- **A technique is a skill.** Jab, cross, hook and low jab are names, each with its own profile, not
  one skill given a path family, an arm extension and a crouch. A number stays where it is a goal
  (a target, a range, a velocity, a place, a height), which the world mostly sets and which may
  change while the skill runs; a number that changes the kind of motion is a name. The drill
  decides: a profile smooth across the number keeps it, and a cliff splits the skill there.
- **Tactics choose skills, never motion.** The intent names a skill and its goal for each resource,
  and what to interrupt: `legs: keep-range(foe, 0.8)`, `right: jab(head)`, `left: cover(head)`.
  A person's keys choose `walk` as an AI would. Tactics read entry conditions and profiles from
  their senses alone; when no skill fits, they choose one that brings another into its entry
  condition.

It is the options framework of reinforcement learning (Sutton, Precup and Singh, 1999): a skill's
entry condition, motion and ending are an option's initiation set, policy and termination, and its
profile is the option's measured model.

### Running together

The body's resources run at once, one skill holding each: concurrent options (Rohanimanesh and
Mahadevan, 2002). The body gives them (`actionResources`, `src/core/skills/action-resources.ts`):
one for each limb effector (each hand and each foot), with its chain's channels and, for a hand,
its pose and what it holds; `trunk`, every channel in no limb, the neck's included; `legs`, the
stance and both feet's resources, so a foot's resource overlaps the legs. A humanoid's choices fall
on four that do not overlap: the legs, the trunk and each hand.

- **The intent is a set of skill choices whose resources do not overlap**, and the interrupts. A
  skill that needs several resources (a cross, a one-two, the fold) is one choice holding them all;
  a whole-body skill (a kick, the rise) leaves one stream.
- **A resource no choice holds runs its default**: the legs stand, holding their place and
  heading; the trunk holds the guard's posture; a hand holds the guard's pose, swinging with a
  walk. The intent states every choice at every control step and nothing is latched: a skill no
  longer chosen gives way to the default, and a default that takes motion to reach (the fold's
  rise) runs it.
- **Facing belongs to what holds the legs**: a footwork skill's goal, or a strike's own turn to
  its target.
- **Choosing goes in order**: the commitment first, then the legs' skill that brings it into its
  entry condition or keeps it there, then cover with the hands left free. A choice stays while it
  runs; when one ends, only what it frees is chosen again, and every resource on an event (a blow
  coming, a foe down, new orders).

### Handing over: at rest or in motion

An entry condition covers motion as well as pose, so one skill hands the body to the next wherever
its ending lies inside the next one's entry condition, still or moving: the sequential composition
of funnels (Burridge, Rizzi and Koditschek, 1999).

| Handover | Where | Gains | Costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| At rest | a stable posture: hands and knees, kneeling, the guard | robust; easy to measure; any skill can start there | slow: the body brakes, then speeds up again |
| In motion | a moving state: rolling onto the side, the hips already turning | fast: momentum carries across | a narrow entry, its timing tight |

- **Split only where a describable, reachable state lies between.** A handover region that is tiny
  and timing-critical makes the two one skill: a punch (the hips and trunk start before the elbow),
  a kip-up, a sit-up tucking onto the feet.
- **A chain used together with tight timing is a skill of its own**, drilled and profiled as
  itself: a step-in jab, with a longer entry range. Its parts stay for when it cannot be used.
- **Stable postures are safe states**: the places any skill falls back to. The fast path is a
  moving skill with a narrow entry; failed or disturbed, the body ends in a safe state, and a
  slower skill goes on from there.
- **Momentum is in flight in the commitment.** The action contract's prepare, commit and release
  stay; an interrupt costs most in the commit, and a skill declares the region it hands over in
  at its release (its exit), so a chain is checked as one skill's exit inside the next one's entry.

## Where it starts

The action contract (`src/core/skills/action.ts`; [skills](../architecture.md#skills)) is the
shape for discrete actions:

- **Name and goal**: an action is configured by name (`ActionConfig`): the path strike's actions
  each name a family (`straight`, `cross`, `hook`, `downward`, `overhand`, `uppercut`;
  `BlowFamily`) and may set their own `armExtension` and `lower` (`PathAction`,
  `src/core/skills/combat.ts`); a request names the action, the effector and a target
  (`ActionRequest`).
- **Entry condition**: an executor's readiness is ready, waiting, blocked with a reason, or a
  window to come into first (`Readiness`, `Window`: along and across the target, facing, the
  body's own speed). Tactics ask it before they request (`Sight.readiness`) and bring the body
  into the window (`approachActionWindow`, `src/core/mind/action-window.ts`); no executor walks,
  and a preparation only sets the feet at the blow's place.
- **Resources**: reservations, prepare, commit and release, interrupts and a bounded ledger of
  endings (`Ended`).
- **Profile**: an executor's estimate (`ExecutorPart.estimate`, a recipe's net) is the nearest
  thing; the [Drills](2026-10-24-drills.md) plan measures profiles.

## Steps

### 2. Footwork, the guard and the intent

The legs' skills by name: `walk(velocity, facing)` and `go-to(place)` for what `move`,
`movement` and `face` give now, and `keep-range`, `step-in`, `step-out` and `circle` with goals
relative to a target. Each takes a pace, a ceiling under which it picks its own gait (its profile
shows the switch from walk to run), and a height under standing that the legs walk in, over the
range its drill covers (locomotion takes one now only where no fold is configured); none stands
tall. Each hand's guard is `cover(region)`, which finds the threat it covers from (the tactics
place it now, `Cover.threat`). The intent becomes the choices, defaults and interrupts of
[Running together](#running-together). Every tactics part, the orders a person gives
(`orderedIntent`) and the person's keys write that form; the closed orders vocabulary opens with
it.

Check: `npm run check`, `npm test`, `npm run build`; a toolkit change, read with the fingerprint
against the commit before, its message naming the moved cases; a person drives with the keys and
orders in the Arena and the Crypt as before; the owner watches.

### 3. The options fighter chooses together

The options fighter (`src/core/mind/options/`) chooses one skill a resource as
[Running together](#running-together) says, keeping its meta-controller's worth, events and
interruption: its cover reflex becomes the hands' `cover` choice, and its keeping clear of fallen
bodies and solids (`Walker.constrain`) the legs' skills' own. It stays the one options mind; a
learned or planning chooser over the same options is an entrant of its own.

Check: as step 2; a paired reading of the version before against the version after.

### 4. The other fighting skills, and the first chain

Blade cuts and thrusts, held-item recipes, low support and combinations in the shape where they
are not. Low support is `fold`, a skill of the legs and the trunk: it enters still, feet planted,
the centre of mass over them (the support fold's gate, `supportFold`); an interrupt runs its rise
to the end, and it hands over standing still, where the legs' skills enter; it lends the trunk to
a low strike (`ActionClaim.delegate`), whose entry condition is the fold ready. The step-in jab is
the first chain that is a skill of its own, and with it each skill's exit is declared at its
release, which the chain is the first to read. A chain stays where its drill beats its parts
thrown in turn; the one-two (`combinationExecutor` of two drives, the lone punch's executor) is
read so too.

Check: as step 2.

## Done when

The fighting skills (strikes, kicks, blade actions, held-item recipes, the fold, combinations,
the legs' skills, the guard) have the shape; the intent is a set of skill choices, one a resource,
with defaults and interrupts; every tactics part, a person's keys and orders write it; the options
fighter chooses one skill a resource at once. The rise joins the shape with its rework
([roadmap](../roadmap.md#order)).
