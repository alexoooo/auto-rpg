# Options: every skill in one shape, a chooser that chains them

## Why

The options fighter (`src/core/mind/options/`) has its options in one shape (entry, holds, model,
exit) and a chooser part at depth 1, but it chooses by hand-built worth one step ahead:

- **A step back is never chosen.** A strike is priced at its hand's energy times its landing
  record, whatever the range (`strike`, `value.ts`), so a jammed punch is priced as a full one; an
  option is worth its own run alone, so a step that deals nothing loses to any strike that deals
  something.
- **A follow-up is never chosen by how a blow landed**: a strike runs to the guard before anything
  is chosen again.
- **Footwork, the guard and the rise are not skills outside it**: the other minds' locomotion
  takes a velocity and a facing, their guard is a cover a reflex or behaviour places each step, and
  the rise drives the muscles through its own motor control beside the skill set. In the options
  fighter a commitment keeps the legs while it runs, so no footwork runs beside one.
- **Every model is hand-built** (`Terms`), and perception is written several times over: the
  options fighter's percepts (`PERCEPTS`) beside `CONDITIONS`, reflex triggers, the threat
  readings and the executors' entry conditions, so a feature two consumers need is computed twice
  and a drill cannot measure what a chooser reads.

So every skill takes one shape (entry, resources, model, exit), a profile measured on its drill
replaces each hand-built model, and the chooser is a part role that can look ahead to a skill's
follow-up. The [Drills](2026-10-24-drills.md) plan measures the profiles. The target is drawn in
[How a fighter thinks](../diagrams/fighter-mind.html) and its types in
[The fighter's mind: types](../diagrams/fighter-mind-types.html).

## The shape of a skill

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| Name and goal | what it does, at what: `jab(head)`, `approach(in the jab's window)`, `cover(head)`, `walk(velocity, facing)`, `fold` |
| Entry | where it can start, in sensed terms: `Readiness`/`Window` (`src/core/skills/action.ts`), extended with posture and motion conditions read from named percepts. One form for every skill |
| Resources | what it holds while it runs (the legs, the trunk, a hand), as the action contract's reservations hold them (`Executor.needs`): all it will need, reserved at its start; it may give one back early and never takes one more |
| Model | what it is worth from here: payoff, duration and where it ends. Hand-built (`Terms`) until its drill measures it |
| Exit | the region where it can hand over (`Readiness | null`): a strike's stroke's end, a posture reached, a range reached |
| Profile | its model measured on its drill over a few percepts (step 4) |

- **One skill per distinct movement, never one per combination.** Jab, cross and hook are names,
  each with its own profile; a number stays where it is a goal (a target, a range, a velocity). A
  walking arm-only jab and a planted power cross are two movements and two skills; a jab thrown
  mid-step is the jab, its entry covering that state.
- **An entry covers the states a skill starts from**: standing, mid-step, drifting, closing. A
  skill covers more starts by widening its entry (training or tuning it on the states footwork
  leaves), measured by its drill's land rate by starting state. A new skill is warranted only for a
  different movement, signalled when one skill's land rate splits sharply across its starts.
- **A skill does its own motion and nothing else.** Outside its entry it refuses and says why. A
  commitment takes the legs over from footwork at whatever phase they are in and drives its own
  step if it needs one; it never walks to its range. Reaching a range is footwork.
- **Tactics choose skills, never motion**, from their senses alone, by entry and profile.

It is the options framework of reinforcement learning (Sutton, Precup and Singh, 1999): a skill's
entry, motion and exit are an option's initiation set, policy and termination, and its profile is
the option's measured model.

### One commitment and a default layer

Every power strike holds the legs and the trunk with its hand (`straight-punch.ts`,
`combination.ts`, `strike.ts`, `blade.ts`), as do a kick and the rise. So the intent is not a
general scheduler of one skill per resource. It is:

- **One commitment slot**: at most one skill that holds the whole body or several resources (a
  strike, a kick, a rise, a lunge), or none.
- **Under it, a default layer re-chosen each step**: a legs skill (footwork) while the legs are
  free, and cover for each free hand.

The chooser fills the commitment first, then the default layer. Between commitments, most of a
fight, footwork and the hands' guard or cover run together; during a commitment the free hand
covers. A commitment may give resources back early (the legs after the drive, as the one-two gives
back its first hand), and the default layer takes them at once.

Skills that hold less stay legs-only or hands-only and run in the default layer or beside a
commitment that leaves their resource free: cover, a frame, a parry; footwork; the arm-only jab
(the path strike's straight holds only its hand, `combat.ts`), thrown while stepping as its own
skill.

- **A resource nothing holds runs its default**, which accepts any state, moving or in contact,
  and brings it to its safe state: a hand to the guard, the trunk upright, the legs standing.
- **Facing belongs to what holds the legs**: a footwork skill's goal, or a commitment's own turn.
- **Cover is a per-step choice of the free hands**, not latched, so it reacts within a step.

### Handing over: at rest or in motion

An entry covers motion as well as pose, so one skill hands the body to the next wherever its exit
lies inside the next one's entry, still or moving: the sequential composition of funnels (Burridge,
Rizzi and Koditschek, 1999).

| Handover | Where | Gains | Costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| At rest | a stable posture: hands and knees, kneeling, the guard | robust; easy to measure; any skill can start there | slow: the body brakes, then speeds up again |
| In motion | a moving state: rolling onto the side, the hips already turning | fast: momentum carries across | a narrow entry, its timing tight |

- **Split only where a describable, reachable state lies between.** A tiny, timing-critical
  handover region makes the two one skill: a punch (the hips and trunk start before the elbow), a
  kip-up.
- **Stable postures are safe states**: the places any skill falls back to.
- **A strike's exit is its stroke's end** (`motion.complete`, `STRIKE_EVENT.thrown`,
  `strike-cycle.ts`). A caller that takes over there sees `Ended` at the handover; the return is
  the hand's default. A caller that does not gets the whole strike, return included.
- **A whole skill stays beside its split parts only where a handover is used**, and the drills read
  each.

### Percepts, cues and goals

A percept is a cheap, pure reading of the senses (range, closing speed, bearing, the foe's posture
and guard, the threat); a cue compares one with a limit.

```ts
interface Percept<V> {          // a feature of the senses: number, boolean or a name
  readonly name: string;
  read(senses: Senses, own: BodyView, state: object): V;
  readonly state?: object;      // memory, for prediction or smoothing
}
interface Cue {                 // a percept against a limit
  readonly percept: string; readonly than: "under" | "over" | "is";
  readonly value: number | string; readonly margin: number;
}
```

- **One registry** feeds the five forms: a reflex's trigger, the chooser's events (a cue changing
  value, with its margin), an entry's region, a profile's variables, a behaviour list's
  conditions. The editor's fields for `CONDITIONS` carry over.
- **Built consumer by consumer**, starting with what the options fighter and the entry conditions
  read; every percept is computed each step at first. Computing only what is watched comes later,
  and only if the step cost shows a need.
- **A goal is a name**, resolved each step from percepts and entry conditions: another skill's
  entry region ("in the jab's window") or a reach ("out of the foe's reach"), never a bare number
  with a label. Footwork's goal is to deliver a state inside the commitment's entry, so a resized
  body needs no retuning.

### The profile

One type, grown from the ground edges' `EdgeProfile` (`assets/core/edge-profiles.json`,
`src/core/mind/ground/profiles.ts`: begun, done, seconds, where its failures end): done, seconds
and where it ends, with payoff added, measured by drills and stored per skill. Few dimensions, as
the edge profiles: range and closing speed, not every percept. It replaces `ActionEstimate`, the
options fighter's `record()` and its down model (`DownModel`) as each is ported.

### The reference chooser

```ts
// The best line from `now`, `depth` skills ahead. The chooser takes its first skill.
function best(now: Situation, depth: number): { gain: number; time: number } {
  if (depth === 0) return { gain: position(now), time: 0 };   // 0 until position terms exist
  let top = { gain: 0, time: 0 };                             // the defaults: nothing new
  for (const skill of ready(now)) for (const goal of goalsOf(skill, now)) {
    const e = profile(skill).expect(now, goal);              // payoff, duration, exit
    const rest = best(e.exit, depth - 1);                    // only skills whose entry admits the exit
    const line = { gain: e.gain + rest.gain, time: e.time + rest.time };
    if (rate(line) > rate(top)) top = line;                  // gain / (time + HORIZON), as `worth`
  }
  return top;
}
```

- **The chooser is a part role** (`chooser`), with `valued` and `ranked` its first parts and
  `depth` a setting; depth 1 is today's one-step worth. A bandit, learned or searching chooser is
  another part, brought by configuration.
- **It decides** when a skill ends or a cue it watches changes; `rethink` (0.2 s) is a fallback.
- **The foe at an exit** is predicted at its present velocity.

What follows, with no case of its own: step back then jab (the step's exit makes the jab worth
more than a jammed one); step in then jab (`approach` toward the jab's entry); a hook off the jab
(chosen at the jab's stroke's end, by how it landed); the rise (postures are exits, the ground
graph priced).

### Reaction time

- **A rule in seconds**, in the `Rulebook`, the same for both sides, converted to steps at the
  world's rate; a match and an evaluation name it. Later a character attribute.
- **On perceiving others only**: the senses hub shows each body the others late
  (`createSenses(world, delay)`, `src/core/mind/senses.ts`); a mind's own body is not delayed, so
  balance and motor control are not punished.
- **Expected in play**: a cover that waits to see a 0.2 to 0.3 s punch catches only its end, so
  reading the foe's preparation pays. People start a response to a seen cue in about 0.15 to 0.2 s.

### What stays free

| Fixed: every mind meets it | Free: a mind's own |
|---|---|
| The Mind's boundary: senses and its own body in; muscle commands and granted grip requests out | everything inside: skills or none, any chooser, any learning |
| Orders in the world's terms; skill choices only as an optional extra | whether a mind uses the library, profiles or goal names |
| The drills as a common measure of a skill | how a skill moves |
| Reaction time, by the rules | how a mind predicts through it, and how often it decides |

## Where it starts

- **The action contract** (`src/core/skills/action.ts`; [skills](../architecture.md#skills)) is
  the shape for discrete actions: a name (`ActionConfig`), readiness (`Readiness`, `Window`: along
  and across the target, facing, the body's own speed), reservations (`Executor.needs`), prepare,
  commit and release, and a ledger of endings (`Ended`). Tactics ask readiness (`Sight.readiness`)
  and bring the body into the window (`approachActionWindow`, `src/core/mind/action-window.ts`).
- **The options fighter, at depth 1** ([architecture](../architecture.md#minds)): each option an
  `Option` (`option.ts`) with an entry (`Readiness`), what it holds (`Sight.needs`), a hand-built
  model (`Terms`, `value.ts`) and an exit where known (a strike's stroke's end, the edge-hold's
  band); a commitment or footwork (`Layer`). The `chooser` part role (`chooser.ts`, `CHOOSERS`:
  `valued`, `ranked`, `depth` 1) decides on a percept's cue (`PERCEPTS`, `percepts.ts`) or every
  `rethink` s. A strike whose entry says `position` hands its window to `approach`; free hands
  `cover` each step; the walk is kept out of fallen bodies and solids by navigation (`keepOut`,
  `clear-step.ts`). Its paired match against the commit before (`npm run match --
  duellist options`, standard protocol, commit 9e7d7603 against the one after): the Duellist scores
  0.733 (match `2d930f0490215ffc`) against 0.792 (`2da928b8f91bbb94`); paired, it gains 12 bouts and
  loses 5 of 120, +0.058 ± 0.067.
- **The ground layer already chains** (`src/core/mind/ground/`): postures as nodes, techniques as
  edges with an entry (`admits`) and an ending (`reached`), routes priced by measured profiles
  ([routes priced](../reference/rising.md#routes-priced)).
- **The senses hub takes a delay** (`DuelRecipe.senseDelay`, `src/arena/duel.ts`), 0 unless given;
  the Arena's link does not take it.
- **The oracle** ([oracle](../reference/oracle.md#the-tables)) turns 6 of 9 lost sides, almost all
  by not falling; strikes by name and two decisions ahead are not measured.

## Steps

### 2. Reaction time

The delay in the `Rulebook` in seconds, the Arena link's `senseDelay`, and every match and
evaluation naming it. The owner judges it in the Arena: bouts at 0 and about 0.2 s watched side by
side. Decided before the punch is trained on fight moments ([Punch](2026-10-24-punch.md), step 4b).

Check: `npm run check`, `npm test`; a bout at 0 equals today's to the bit; the owner's choice
recorded in the `Rulebook`'s source.

### 3. Footwork, the guard and the intent

The legs' skills by name, in the default layer: `walk(velocity, facing)` and `go-to(place)` for
what `move`, `movement` and `face` give now, and `approach`, `keep-range`, `step-out` and `circle`
with goals named as above. Each takes a pace, a ceiling under which it picks its own gait, and a
height under standing that the legs walk in. Each hand's guard is `cover(region)`, finding the
threat it covers from (`Cover.threat`). Every tactics part, the orders a person gives
(`orderedIntent`) and the person's keys write the intent's form (a commitment or none, a legs
skill, a cover per free hand, and interrupts); the closed orders vocabulary opens with it. In the
options fighter, footwork runs beside a commitment that leaves the legs free. The percepts
registry takes its next consumers: the executors' entry conditions, `CONDITIONS`, the reflex
triggers and the threat readings.

Check: `npm run check`, `npm test`; `npm run match` of the options fighter against the commit
before, both sides, the standard protocol, the paired difference reported, a loss fixed or its
reason recorded before landing; a toolkit change read with the fingerprint against the commit before, its
message naming the moved cases; a person drives with the keys and orders in the Arena and the Crypt
as before; the owner watches.

### 4. Measured profiles

The drills record each skill's payoff, duration and exit over range and closing speed, as the edge
profiles do, and the profile replaces its hand-built `Terms` (then `ActionEstimate`, `record()` and
the down model).

Check: a profile regenerates from its drill record; `npm run match` of the chooser on profiles
against it on `Terms`.

### 5. Depth 2

Built only after the oracle's ceiling of two decisions over one, with strikes by name, shows a
gain worth it ([oracle](../reference/oracle.md#cost): priced before it runs). Then `best()` at
depth 2, the foe predicted at its present velocity, and strikes handed over at their stroke's end.

Check: the oracle's ceiling recorded in its reference; `npm run match` of depth 2 against depth 1.

### 6. The other skills, drilled in the fighter, and the rise

- Blade cuts and thrusts, held-item recipes, low support (`fold`, a legs-and-trunk commitment that
  lends the trunk to a low strike, `ActionClaim.delegate`) and combinations in the shape.
- A tactics slot, or a skill-level drill, so the options fighter's skills are drilled as the
  Strike drill drills a tactics mind's.
- The rise as a whole-body commitment, its edges' profiles the ground graph's, with the
  [Recovery](2026-10-23-recovery.md) plan's step 3.

Check: as step 3's match; each skill's drill read beside its parts where a handover is used.

## Done when

The fighting skills (strikes, kicks, blade actions, held-item recipes, the fold, combinations,
footwork, the guard, the rise) have the shape, their profiles measured on drills; the intent is one
commitment and a default layer, written by every tactics part, a person's keys and orders; the
chooser is a part role with a depth setting; reaction time is a rule; and the options fighter
chooses on profiles, its paired match no worse than before each step.
