# Step 02: the crypt's sight read through an index, with the answers it has

## Goal

A crypt run's own planning is half of its slowest steps, and all of that is sight: `reveal` asks
`walkable` at every 0.2 m of a line to every floor cell within 12 m of every party member, five
times a second, and `walkable` reads nine cells, every door and every obstacle each time
([step-cost.md](../reference/step-cost.md#the-crypts-plan-in-the-step)). The path search is under
1 us a step.

This plan makes sight cheap and changes no answer: the run plays the same to the bit, so nothing
is tuned, nothing is moved off the step and nothing arrives late. Tried in a scratch copy, the
plan's 99th per cent falls from 2.4 to 4.6 ms to 0.2 to 0.3, and the plans over 1 ms from 199 to
439 a run to 1 to 4, each of those a body being built
([step-cost.md](../reference/step-cost.md#sight-read-through-an-index-tried)).

It needs no other plan. The design is `2026-10-02-step-00-design.md`.

## The index (`src/dungeon/map.ts`)

What `walkable(map, p, 0, true, true)` answers for a sample of a sight line:

1. `p`'s cell (`Math.round` of each coordinate) is floor;
2. no sight-blocking obstacle's rectangle is within 0.001 m of `p`;
3. no rock cell among the nine about `p`'s cell has its square within 0.001 m of `p`;
4. `p` is in no closed door's box (0.18 m by 1.5 m about its point, by its axis).

So a sample is seen through with nothing more read when its cell is floor, no obstacle or closed
door could reach any point of that cell, and the sample is more than 0.001 m inside the cell's
edge: rock is beyond that edge.

```ts
/**
 * **Where sight needs no reading.** `clear[z * size + x]` is 1 where the cell is floor and no
 * sight-blocking obstacle and no closed door reaches any point of it. A sample of a sight line in
 * such a cell, farther than `SIGHT_RIM` from the cell's middle on neither axis, is seen through
 * whatever is beside the cell: rock stops sight within 0.001 m of itself and no farther. Every
 * other sample is `walkable`'s.
 *
 * It is of the map as it was when made. It stays right as doors open, since a cell it does not
 * vouch for is read from the map; it is made again then so that those cells are fast. It is
 * wrong once a door has closed, an obstacle has come or the floor has changed: whoever does that
 * to a map makes another.
 */
export interface SightIndex { readonly clear: Uint8Array }

/** A cell's samples within this of its middle on both axes are at least 0.002 m from every other cell, m. */
const SIGHT_RIM = 0.498;

export function sightIndex(map: DungeonMap): SightIndex

export function canSee(map: DungeonMap, a: Point, b: Point, range = 12, index?: SightIndex): boolean

/**
 * ... `seen`, where given, is the cells this reading has seen from other places: a cell in it is
 * not looked at again, what is seen from here is added to it, and it is what is returned.
 */
export function reveal(map: DungeonMap, hero: Point, explored: Set<number>, range = 12, index?: SightIndex, seen?: Set<number>): Set<number>
```

- `sightIndex`: for every floor cell at integers (x, z), `clear` is 1 unless a sight-blocking
  obstacle has `|x - o.x| - o.width / 2 - 0.5 < 0.002` and `|z - o.z| - o.depth / 2 - 0.5 < 0.002`,
  or a closed door has `|x - d.point.x| - 0.5 < hx + 1e-9` and `|z - d.point.z| - 0.5 < hz + 1e-9`
  with `hx`, `hz` the door's half box (0.18 or 1.5 by its axis). Both are the map's own tests
  taken at the cell's nearest point and widened, so the index never vouches for a sample the map
  would stop.
- With an index, `canSee` walks its line by the same arithmetic `clearSegment` does
  (`a.x + (b.x - a.x) * i / steps`, the same `steps`), with no point made for a sample: a sample
  whose cell is `clear` and which is within `SIGHT_RIM` of the cell's middle on both axes goes
  on; any other is asked of `walkable(map, { x, z }, 0, true, true)`. The rule of what stops
  sight stays `walkable`'s alone.
- Without one, `canSee` and `reveal` are what they are: the fingerprint, the scenery's memory and
  every test that edits a map go on reading the map itself.
- `reveal` with `seen` adds to it in its own scan order, so the set a party's sight returns, and
  `explored`, list their cells in the order they do today (the fog's key reads that order).

## The run (`src/dungeon/run.ts`)

- The run holds `{ index, opened }`: a `SightIndex` and the count of open doors it was made at.
  `sight` counts the map's open doors, makes the index again where the count differs (a door
  opens perhaps ten times a run; the count is read five times a second), and reads every member
  into one set:

  ```ts
  const visible = new Set<number>();
  for (const at of from) reveal(this.map, at, this.explored, 12, index, visible);
  return visible;
  ```

- `perceive` and `choose` hand the same index to their `canSee`s. `perceive` calls `sight`
  first, so the index is the step's.
- The crypt's cadence stays its own (`RUN_TIMING`): how often a run looks is the run's design,
  and nothing here schedules it.

## Tests (`tests/crypt-sight.test.mjs`)

1. **`the_index_changes_no_answer`**: the levels of seeds 1 to 8 (`generateLevel`), each with its
   doors closed and then opened one by one, an index made at each: from 60 seeded points a level,
   off the cells' middles, `reveal` with the index lists the cells `reveal` without it does, in
   order; and `canSee` at 14 m agrees over 60 seeded pairs. The scratch reading was 425587 cells
   and 4500 lines.
2. **`a_stale_index_is_right_as_doors_open`**: one index made with every door closed, the doors
   then opened: the same comparison with that index.
3. **`an_obstacle_stops_sight_through_the_index_as_it_does_without`**: the rack and the column
   of `tests/crypt-detail.test.mjs`'s map, and the levels of `generateCryptDungeon` seeds 1 to 4
   (which stand obstacles): lines across each obstacle, beside it within 0.002 m, and ending on
   it, agree; the column stops sight and the rack does not.
4. **`a_sample_at_a_cell_s_edge_is_the_map_s`**: lines along a wall at 0.4985, 0.4995 and 0.5 m
   from the middles of the cells beside it, and through the corner where two rock cells meet
   diagonally: agree, and at least one of them is stopped (the fixture shows the rim).
5. **`a_party_s_sight_is_its_members_in_the_order_it_was`**: four places a metre apart on seeds
   1 to 4: one set revealed into in turn lists the cells that the first place's set with each
   other's added lists, and `explored` lists the same in both.
6. **`the_index_vouches_for_most_of_the_floor`**: on seeds 1 to 8 with the doors closed, at
   least four fifths of the floor cells are `clear`; with them open, more. An index of zeros
   would pass every test above and make nothing faster.

`tests/crypt-core.test.mjs`, `dungeon-party.test.mjs`, `dungeon-hearing.test.mjs` stand as they
are: they play runs, and the runs are the same.

**Mutations, each must go red**: `SIGHT_RIM` at 0.5 (test 4); obstacles left out of the index
(test 3); doors left out of it (test 1, doors closed); the door's box taken at the cell's middle
and not its nearest point (test 1: a door beside a cell's edge); `seen` asked for the wrong key
(test 5); the index never made again (none red, by test 2: the bar below reads it); `clear` all
zeros (test 6).

## The bar and its readings

On a quiet machine, before and after, into the commit message and
`docs/reference/step-cost.md#the-crypts-plan-in-the-step`:

```powershell
node scripts/fingerprint.mjs > before.txt
node research/crypt-plan.mjs --seeds 1,2,3,4
node research/crypt-plan.mjs --seeds 1,2,3,4 --profile
npm test
npm run check
npm run build
node scripts/fingerprint.mjs > after.txt
```

- `before.txt` and `after.txt` are the same file: "crypt fight, seed 1" and "seed 2" are runs
  through the index, and "sight, crypt 1" to "8" are the map's own reading.
- Each run ends as it did, at the same step, with the same bodies built.
- **The bar**: on each of seeds 1 to 4 the plan's 99th per cent is under 0.5 ms and no more than
  five plans are over 1 ms. If a seed misses it, the profile says which function is left, and
  that is written down before anything else is tried.

## Not in this plan

- **A body built in the step.** What is left over 1 ms is an enemy's body made as the party
  comes near, 2.6 to 4.3 ms three or four times a run. It is the roadmap's, with the crypt's
  enemies built from the start.
- **The scenery's memory on the page** (`sampleCorners`, `src/dungeon/scenery-visibility.ts`)
  asks `canSee` the map's way whenever the hero has moved. It is not read: the crypt's step is
  not yet read on a page at all, and that reading says whether it takes the index.
- **Fewer lines.** A sight that casts to the edge of its range and marks what each line crosses
  would ask a tenth of the samples and answer differently at corners. Not needed at this bar.
