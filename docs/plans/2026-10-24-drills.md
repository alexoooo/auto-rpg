# Drills: skills proved one rung at a time, in plain sight

## Why

The owner wants two fighters to have a good fight in the Arena. Today an empty-handed Duellist
against Vanguard mostly punches empty air, and other matchups close in and flail. The research
has judged nearly every change by fight wins on fresh cases. That signal is slow, noisy and
dominated by falls (the oracle's ceiling is not falling,
[the tables](../reference/oracle.md#the-tables)). It also mixes the layers, so a gain or a loss
names no layer. Under it, a punch that cannot land the competency blow on a still pad
([punch](../analysis/2026-10-08-punch-layer.md)) is tuned through spacing and target choices it
cannot use.

So the work is split three ways, and each part gets its own fast, visible signal:

| Layer | Question | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Skill | Can the body do it? | a drill: a rung of a ladder, seconds a trial |
| Proficiency | How well, where? | the skill's profile: its drill readings over the conditions |
| Use | What should a fighter do with the skills it has? | tactics that read the profiles |

The fight is judged by the owner's verdict alone (step 8), a black box. A bout is the check that a
gain carries into fights, not the place a skill is tuned. Each skill's or mind's developer picks
any sub-goals it likes inside its own layer. A sub-goal stays only while improving it moves the
verdict or what the owner sees. No sub-goal enters the verdict or a rating.

## What the owner sees

Visibility comes first. A number is only as good as the trial behind it, and a claimed skill has
often looked broken when watched.

- **Drills, a Lab scenario** (`?play=lab&scenario=drills&drill=…&rung=…`). It plays a rung's
  trials one after another, with the same case builder as the battery that scored them, so a
  watched trial is the scored one. A bout is the same to the bit in Node and the page; a drill
  case is held to the same (step 1's check).
- **The strip**: a square a trial over the rung's grid (landed, missed, fell), laid out by its
  conditions; a click plays that trial. The reel plays every failure first, then a random sample of
  the rest; nobody picks what is shown.
- **Overlays**: the aim, the target's path, the effector's path, each contact; for the trial, why it
  failed (out of range at the start, the target moved, short of the aim, blocked, balance lost).
- **Side by side**: `&previous=<version>` plays the same trial under both versions in step.
- **Ratings links in**: a drill case's "watch" opens Drills at that trial, as a bout case opens
  the Arena (`watchHref`, `src/ratings/main.ts`).
- **The owner's acceptance**: a rung is passed only when the owner has watched it and said so. The
  agent writes the acceptance beside the battery's records (`assets/evaluations/<id>/accepted.json`:
  rung, subject, version, date), tied to the version, and any change to the skill clears it.
  Drills and Ratings show it. A trial that lands but looks broken is a failure the owner marks.

Rules that land with step 1, in `AGENTS.md`:

- A claim that a skill works is a Drills link at the version claimed; without the link it is no
  claim.
- Before sending the link, the agent plays the failure reel in a browser and says what it saw,
  the worst trials included.
- Numbers are necessary, not sufficient: no agent marks a rung passed.

## The shape of a skill

Every skill, of the legs, a hand or the trunk, has the same four parts, so a drill, a profile and
a tactic read every skill one way:

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| Name and goal | what it does, at what: `jab(head)`, `keep-range(foe, 0.8 m)`, `cover(head)`, `walk(velocity)` |
| Entry condition | where it can start, in sensed terms (range, bearing, target speed, own footing): a predicate its module exports |
| Resources | what it holds while it runs (the legs, a hand, the trunk), as the action contract's reservations hold them |
| Profile | how it does over its entry condition (step 7) |

- **A skill does its own motion and nothing else.** Outside its entry condition it refuses and says
  why. It may shift its weight and place its feet as part of its motion; it never walks to its
  range. Reaching a range is a footwork skill. A step-in jab is a skill of its own, with a longer
  entry range, drilled and profiled as itself.
- **A technique is a skill.** Jab, cross, hook and low jab are names, each with its own profile, not
  one skill given a path family, an arm extension and a crouch. A continuous goal parameter stays
  only where the profile covers it.
- **Tactics choose skills, never motion.** The intent names a skill and its goal for each resource,
  and what to interrupt: `legs: keep-range(foe, 0.8)`, `right: jab(head)`, `left: cover(head)`.
  A person's keys choose `walk` as an AI would. Tactics read entry conditions and profiles from
  their senses alone; when no skill fits, they choose one that brings another into its entry
  condition.

It is the options framework of reinforcement learning (Sutton, Precup and Singh, 1999): a skill's
entry condition, motion and ending are an option's initiation set, policy and termination, and its
profile is the option's measured model. A drill samples a skill's entry condition and a little
beyond it, so the edge is measured, and the profile is indexed by the same variables.

Today the intent (`Intent`, `src/core/mind/intent.ts`) carries velocities, a facing, covers and
action requests; the recipe strike brings the body to its range (`BlowAttack`); a blow's path
family, `armExtension` and `lower` are fields tactics set.

## The drills

Every drill takes the same subject options: the body (Warrior, Rogue, Skeleton; the Reptile later),
the loadout, the character (physique, Balance, Haste), the skill's version and `previous`, the
rate (120 Hz; 480 Hz to check a reading is physical) and a few seeds a condition, a seed moving the
start a little. Each family is one battery (`src/evaluation/<family>/`, arity 1), its rungs its
protocols; Ratings lists them under Skills.

**Strike**, one drill per action (jab, cross, front kick, club blow, sword cut, sword thrust), on
the same rungs:

| Rung | Target | Options | Readings |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | still pad | range, height, offset across, hand or foot | landed, time to contact, speed and impulse, upright, time back to guard |
| 1 | pad moving in a line | and its speed and direction (toward, away, across) | the same |
| 2 | drill opponent stepping | range, rhythm, region (head, trunk, legs) | and the wound dealt |
| 3 | drill opponent guarding | and its guard | and blocked or through |
| 4 | drill opponent jabbing back | and its rhythm | and the wound taken, falls |
| 5 | struck from motion | from a walk, after a step, after being hit | the same |

**Footwork**, getting to a range and keeping it:

| Rung | Target | Options | Readings |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | still | start distance, bearing, range wanted | time to the range, overshoot, falls |
| 1 | moving in a line | its speed and direction | range error over time, falls |
| 2 | drill opponent stepping in and out | rhythm, step | the same |
| 3 | circling at range | way, speed | and facing error |
| 4 | in, then out | range in, range out, time inside | time each phase, falls |
| 5 | among obstacles | layout | the same |

**Stand**: a shove standing in guard, then walking, then mid-strike (size, direction, height;
upright, steps, time to settle; mid-strike, whether the strike finished). **Combination**: a strike
then a strike (`combination.ts`), on Strike's rungs 0 to 3 (both landed, the time between).
**Cover**: a swinging club, then a drill opponent striking on its script, then cover and a counter
(side, height, speed, warning; covered, wound taken, counter landed). **Rise**: the owner's 64
falls, armed, then with an opponent near. **Run**: straight, then turns and stops.

Two fixtures serve most rungs:

- **The moving target**: a pad on a rail, by path (a line, back and forth, a circle), speed and
  height.
- **The drill opponent**: a fighter body under a script of orders, as a bout's recipe and tape
  give it (`Duel.tape`). Its scripts: stand, step in and out, sidestep, circle, guard, jab on a
  rhythm, press forward; by rhythm, step, loadout and body.

A skill not built (a dodge, a clinch, fighting from the ground) brings its drill when it comes.

## Where it starts

What exists: the competency suite (`research/competencies.mjs`, [competencies](../reference/competencies.md)),
a research script on still fixtures with no records or view; the punch and kick stands
(`punchStand`, `research/punch-calibration.mjs`; `research/front-kicks.mjs`); the shove and walk
trials (`research/core-stance-trials.mjs`); the swinging target and the guard probe
(`src/core/tasks/swing-target.ts`, `defense.ts`); the `blade`, `buckler` and `court` batteries and
the `rising-target` and `equipped-rise` evaluations ([evaluations](../architecture.md#evaluations));
the Lab's Attack scenario, free play at moving targets, not graded cases.

## Steps

### 1. Drills and Strike's rung 0

The Strike battery with rung 0, its case a core task (`src/core/tasks/`) moved from `punchStand`,
for the game's jab and cross; the Drills scenario with the strip, the failure reel, overlays,
`previous` and Ratings' watch link; the acceptance file; the rules above in `AGENTS.md`. Nothing
about the punch changes: the owner sees today's punch as it is.

Check: `npm run check`, `npm test`, `npm run build`; a trial's reading in Node equals the page's
for the same case (its world digest at the end); the owner opens a failing trial from Ratings
and from the strip.

### 2. Strikes in the skill's shape

The action contract gains the entry condition and the refusal outside it. Each punch and kick is
a named skill: jab, cross, hook, front kick and the other variants that the path family, the arm
extension and `lower` choose today. The recipe strike stops bringing the body to its range. The
tactics parts that set those fields choose names instead. Locomotion and the guard stay as they
are until step 5.

Check: `npm run check`, `npm test`, `npm run build`; a toolkit change, read with the fingerprint
against step 1's commit, its message naming the moved cases; rung 0's trials inside a skill's entry
condition read as at step 1, and those outside are refused with their reason; the owner watches
rung 0.

### 3. The moving target and Strike's rung 1

Check: the pad keeps its path and speed with nothing touching it; the owner watches rung 1.

### 4. The first question: does any punch we have hit something that moves?

Every punch executor (the path strike, `vanguard-punch`, the recipe strike) on rungs 0 and 1, one
body first, then the others where a result is close. What the answer changes: hits on rung 0
that fall away on rung 1 send the work to prediction; failures at rung 0 send it to the motor
skill. That work is tuning the hand-built cycle against the drill, an attack-and-defence assist
lowered over time, or a learned skill with a motion prior, whichever the readings point to.
The finding goes in a reference record.

Check: the record; the owner watches the failure reels.

### 5. Footwork and the intent in the skill's shape, and Footwork's rungs 0 and 1

The legs' skills by name: `walk(velocity, facing)` and `go-to(place)` for what `move`,
`movement` and `face` give now, and `keep-range`, `step-in`, `step-out` and `circle` with goals
relative to a target; each hand's guard is `cover(region)`. The intent becomes one skill choice
per resource and its interrupts. Every tactics part, the orders a person gives (`orderedIntent`)
and the person's keys write that form.

Check: as step 2; a person drives with the keys and orders in the Arena and the Crypt as before;
the owner watches both rungs.

### 6. The drill opponent, and rungs 2 to 4 of Strike and Footwork

Check: each script plays as written in an empty arena (its body's path against the script's);
the owner watches each rung.

### 7. Profiles that tactics read

A skill's profile is its battery record summarised over its entry condition's variables: hit
chance by range and target speed, time to contact, recovery, falls. It is written as data an
entrant ships (`assets/entrants/<name>/`). A tactics part reads the entry conditions and the
profiles to choose when and which skill to use, never the skill's code. A changed body or skill is measured again, not searched again.

Check: a profile regenerates from a record; a tactics part choosing by profile against the same
tactics without it, by `npm run match`.

### 8. The owner's verdict

A bout lasts at most two minutes; from the first minute each side loses 1/60 of its full life a
second; a side is out when its life reaches zero. There is no other rule: a fall ends nothing,
and the cap judges nothing, since by two minutes the drain has emptied every life (both out on one
step is a draw, as now). The game's end rules (`GAME_ENDS`, `src/arena/end-rule.ts`) become the
damage and the drain; the cap is 120 s. Records read under the old verdict are read again before
any comparison.

Check: `npm run check`, `npm test`; a bout of two untouched fighters ends at 120 s drawn; the
owner watches a bout in the Arena.

### 9. The other families, as their skills are worked on

Stand, Combination, Cover, Rise and Run, each wrapping its existing fixture where there is one,
each skill in the skill's shape.

## Done when

Every skill has the skill's shape and the intent is one skill choice per resource; Strike,
Footwork and Cover each have their drills with the owner's acceptance at rung 4 for at least one
skill; a fighter's tactics choose by the entry conditions and profiles; and the owner watches two
such fighters fight in the Arena under the verdict and accepts it.
