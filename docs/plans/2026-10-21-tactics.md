# Tactics: what a fighter decides

## Scope

The choices a fighter makes during a bout, for any Mind: which target and surface, when to
approach, commit, break away or wait, what to do from the ground, and how far to obey orders.
Tactics run on the capabilities as they are; they never wait for the
[capabilities](2026-10-21-capabilities.md) to finish. Every humanoid's own mind, the Duellist,
the options fighter, the entrants and any new Mind are all clients, and an improvement in one is
read as that Mind's ([Minds](../architecture.md#minds)).

Components and the cycle are the [framework](2026-10-21-framework.md)'s, including its compute
rule; evaluations are [Ratings](2026-10-21-ratings.md)'. The movement interface tactics use is
[Movement and command](2026-10-21-movement-and-command.md)'s.

## Open questions

Each is asked of a concrete failure seen in bouts, one decision or model at a time:

- **Targets after a guard.** When a fighter meets a guard, when should it change its target
  surface, its action or its approach? Openings and target regions are compared through the
  shared aiming and action path, keeping the opponent's and the contact's identity.
- **Spacing and commitment.** Approach, clinch breaking, circling and follow-up timing against a
  moving opponent. Readiness, terminal records and actual motion separate a poor decision from a
  skill that could not carry out its request. After Movement's A2, spacing is a range or direct
  goal with a ceiling, not a velocity.
- **Models of an option's worth.** Expected attack value, incoming cost, travel time, falls and
  recovery, calibrated to the components the fighter actually runs, with uncertainty and
  temporary inability and no hidden opponent state.
- **Ground choices.** Getting up, moving away, fighting from the ground or waiting, compared on
  the same falls. This selects among recovery components; developing one is the capabilities'.
- **Selection and reflexes.** Valued, ranked and behaviour-list selection, interruption and
  reflex ownership, where a failure points at them, keeping skill admission, resource ownership
  and hand-back.
- **Orders.** Obedience against self-preservation under explicit orders, read with orders given;
  a bout without orders cannot measure it. A follow-orders model reads Movement's speed rule (A1),
  so the value of an order and the motion agree.

## Reading a change

A tactical change is read by the paired report (Ratings, step 5): the changed Mind against its
previous version and against the panel, on matched conditions, both sides, stopped when settled
within its budget, with its worst matchups and stalemates. Two Minds are compared on the
characters and loadouts both fit, with coverage beside the comparison. `research/options-readout.mjs`
and the bout tapes diagnose what choices were made and what followed.

## What a player may choose

A player's choices are trade-offs a Mind's config already exposes, found as Minds develop:
caution against aggression, preferred spacing, available actions, ground choices, the selection
rule. A choice is exposed when its effect is understood: its own labels, validation and fields,
lists for algorithms, actions and body parts, bounded numbers for meaningful quantities, and
presets that round-trip through the editor, the link and a save. Searched constants stay the
implementation's. An unsupported composition is refused with its reason.

## Done when

This plan has no end state of its own: it holds the open tactical questions while they are open.
A question leaves it when a change that answers it has landed with its paired report and the
owner has watched the bouts.
