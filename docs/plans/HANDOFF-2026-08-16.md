## Where things stand in one paragraph

Three topics were planned and two are finished. **Room view** is complete and moved no
hash. **Combat arms** is complete at five sessions rather than four, and the fifth is
the one that mattered. **Articulated bow** is one step of five in: the command can
express "loose" and nothing consumes it yet. The headline mechanical result is that a
representative Fighter-versus-Brute fight now produces real damage and real losses for
the first time -- the Fighter's end health falls to `0.8575` from a band
(`0.9885-0.9985`) it had never left under any policy, arm rate or shield configuration
anyone had tried, and the Brute wins nine trials in two hundred having previously won
none in six hundred.

## The finding that matters most

Two sessions were spent on hypotheses that were both correct about their own mechanism
and both irrelevant to the outcome. Session 02 doubled the Brute's arm authority (health
`0.4989 -> 0.5271`); session 03 gave it a policy that aims where the shield is not
(shield share of resolutions `9.68% -> 8.70%`). Across six configurations the Brute
recorded **zero kills** and the Fighter's health never moved.

The cause was not on anybody's list. `channels()` in
`crates/sim/src/combat/resolution.rs` scales a hit's transverse component by the
**weapon's own** `edge_factor`; `club()` carries `Fx::ZERO`; the leftover channel,
`pressure`, reaches no anatomy at all -- `ContactProjector` bills
`incoming = cut_raw + thrust_raw` and the word `pressure` does not appear anywhere in
`crates/sim/src/anatomy.rs`. A swing is transverse motion. **So a swung club could not
injure anyone at any speed, by construction**, and both preceding sessions were
delivering more club energy into a channel multiplied by zero.

The fix was *not* to make a wooden club cut. It was that blunt force had no
representation, and the model already separates integrity loss from bleeding
(`cut_share` scales the wound by the cut fraction), so crushing fits an existing shape.
The derivation, the corrected energy arithmetic and the ranked alternatives are in
[`docs/performance/v2-articulated-gate.md`](../performance/v2-articulated-gate.md).

## The methodological lesson, which is not optional

**Five sessions, five pin predictions, four wrong -- all four in the same direction.**
Each predicted a registered hash would move; in each case none did. Every one was
reasoned from *the subsystem being edited* rather than from *what the fixture actually
exercises*. Examples, because the pattern is easier to see than to state:

- Session 02 predicted `ARTICULATED_STREAM_DIGEST` would move on a two-handed coupling.
  `club()` binds `GripBinding::Right`, so the shipped duel contains no two-handed grip
  and a correctly-scoped coupling is inert on every fixture by construction.
- Session 04 predicted `ARTICULATED_COMMAND_HASH` would move when the shield normal
  began following the arm. The command digest is taken against an **unstepped** world
  whose only shielded body spawns at yaw zero with a tucked arm, and the stream
  fixture's own comment says "both bearings are zero, and that is the fixture's whole
  trick".

The bow's step 1 is the first correct prediction, and it was correct because it traced
each digest to the line that writes the changed value -- which also found that the
plan's "nothing else may move at all" was **false**: three digests hash the submitted
command's payload *and its declared width*.

**Method:** read the prediction off the fixture, write it down before running the gate,
and verify afterwards by diffing hash literals against the previous commit rather than
by trusting the suite. Normalize underscores and case on both sides when you do --
markdown writes `0x2fac296932b97439` and Rust writes `0x2fac_2969_32b9_7439`, and a
comparison that misses that will report "no change" while a pin is moving.

## What shipped, by topic

### Room view -- complete, no hash could move (client-only)

- Corner, tee and stub rotations are derived from the neighbour **pair**. The old rule
  used `solid.findIndex(Boolean)` over `[N,E,S,W]`, so `{N,E}` and `{N,W}` both yielded
  zero and three of four corners were mis-rotated. The test that pinned the wrong answer
  was rewritten to bound the right one from both sides, with expected arm positions
  derived from `tools/art/room.py` geometry rather than recorded.
- The fog frontier counts undisclosed neighbours for **topology only** and still never
  draws one. This was a policy decision, not a bug fix, and the reason sits at the rule.
- `#/game` starts at zoom 8 with a dead-zone hero follow, and pan/zoom gained a mutate
  path instead of rebuilding the camera every frame.
- Wall joins are synthesized from crossing `wall_straight` runs, because the authored
  corner pieces sit on tile *edges* while straights sit on the *centreline* and cannot
  physically meet. The authored corners remain in the kit, unused.
- The `#/game` cylinder is now a procedural warrior on `v2-18`'s exact joint names, so
  an authored rig is a drop-in. It uses **no client-side clock**: the walk reads the
  sim's published `UNIT_STRIDE_PHASE`, so a pinned snapshot pins the whole pose.

### Combat arms -- complete, no registered hash moved in any of the five

01 made `GripBinding::Both` expressible from a duel config; 02 gave it a benefit
(halved effective inertia, one shared fatigue bill); 03 added an asymmetric `lab`
matchup and the `openings` policy (code 6, scores regions by shield coverage); 04 freed
the guard bearing **and** made the shield's normal follow the arm that carries it; 05
added the blunt channel.

Session 04 is worth a second look: the plate's facing and its arm are now one rotation
of one reach vector, so their disagreement is **exactly zero at every bearing and tick**
rather than merely narrowed from the measured 32-degree median. That is a closed form,
not a histogram, and the old distributions were kept beside it rather than replaced.

Session 05's shape is the part to preserve: `crush` is billed on what the edge and point
**declined**, so a blade -- whose edge and point already claim the whole budget --
is *exactly* unchanged rather than approximately, and `crush_factor` lives behind
`Material` as a `const fn` rather than as a fifth `SurfaceSpec` field, which is why it
moved no spec-table pin.

## Traps that cost real time here

- **Run cargo from PowerShell, never the Bash tool.** Git Bash's coreutils `link`
  shadows MSVC's `link.exe` and fails as `link: extra operand`, which reads as a
  toolchain bug and is not one.
- **Never use PowerShell `Set-Content`/`Out-File`/`WriteAllText` on source files.** Two
  agents corrupted files with BOM/CRLF this way. Node with explicit `utf8` read/write
  is safe and was used for every scripted edit here.
- **`$LASTEXITCODE` after a `Select-String` pipeline is meaningless.** Capture the
  native exit code directly or you will read a green suite as a failure and vice versa.
- **Several tests fail only under `--features cartesian-recoil`.** Run that suite; do
  not infer it from the default one. It takes about eight minutes.
- **`wasm-memory`'s warm-set fixtures re-measure whenever an allocation pattern moves.**
  Growth inside a *guarded* cycle reads as a leak and usually is not: trace the
  per-round page counts first. The crush session's growth was one allocator step
  arriving later than before -- flat at 224 pages for eleven rounds, one step to 248,
  then flat through forty.
- **Line insertions in `crates/sim/src/world.rs` and `crates/web/src/lib.rs` drift the
  anchors in `docs/reference/hashes.md`.** `check_docs` catches them; recompute against
  source rather than deleting them, and replace in descending line order or `#L97` will
  match inside `#L972`.
- **Do not run `cargo fmt`.**
